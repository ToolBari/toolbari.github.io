How to scan a document with a photo

Lay the page flat on a plain surface and take the photo in bright, even light, holding the phone straight above the page so the edges look square. Shadows across the text and tilted angles make the scan harder to read, so move the lamp or the page until the light is even.

  1. Upload the photo with the picker above.
  2. Use the crop numbers to cut away the table or background around the page. Leave the width and height at 0 to keep the full photo.
  3. Rotate the photo if it came out sideways.
  4. Pick a filter: grayscale for plain text, high contrast for faded print, or black and white for a crisp copy.
  5. Download the scanned image and keep it with your files.

Which filter should you choose?

Original colours keeps the photo as it is, which suits colour documents, certificates and pages with stamps or handwriting in ink. Grayscale removes colour and suits plain typed pages, because it makes the text stand out and the file look like a photocopy.

High contrast pushes dark text darker and light paper lighter, which helps faded print and pencil notes. Black and white turns every pixel fully black or fully white using the threshold slider: lower values keep more detail, higher values give a cleaner page. Try the filters one by one and keep the one that reads best.

Questions people ask

Does this tool upload my photo anywhere?

No. Your photo stays in your browser — it is drawn onto a canvas on this page and never sent to a server.

What kind of photo works best?

A sharp photo taken straight above the page in bright, even light, with the whole page inside the frame and no shadows across the text.

Can I scan more than one page?

One photo at a time. Scan each page separately and download each result, then combine the images however you like.

Why does my scan look grey instead of white?

Photos of paper often look grey because of shadows and warm light. Try the high contrast or black and white filter to brighten the background.
