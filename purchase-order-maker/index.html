What goes on a purchase order

A purchase order tells a supplier exactly what you want to buy. It names the buyer and the supplier, carries a purchase order number and a date, and lists each item with its quantity and unit cost. The totals at the bottom show the subtotal, any tax, and the amount to pay.

The purchase order number matters more than it looks: it is the reference both sides use if anything is questioned later. Use a numbering pattern you can keep up, such as PO-001, PO-002, and keep a copy of every order you send.

How the totals are worked out

Each line total is the quantity times the unit cost. The subtotal is the sum of all the line totals. Tax is worked out from the subtotal using the rate you enter, and the order total is the subtotal plus the tax.

If no tax applies, leave the rate at zero and the total will equal the subtotal. The rate is yours to enter: the page never assumes a tax rate, because rates differ by place and by what is being bought.

Questions people ask

What details should a purchase order include?

Buyer and supplier names and addresses, a purchase order number, the date, item descriptions with quantities and unit costs, and the totals. Add delivery details in the notes if your supplier needs them.

How is tax worked out?

Enter your tax rate as a percentage and the maker adds that share of the subtotal to the total. Leave the rate at zero if no tax applies to this order.

Can I edit an item after adding it?

Remove the line and add it again with the corrected details, then generate the document again. The preview always reflects the current item list.

Is the purchase order saved anywhere?

No. The document exists only in this page until you print or download it, so save a copy before you leave.
