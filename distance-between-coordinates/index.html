Distance Between Coordinates Calculator

Enter two latitude and longitude pairs and this tool calculates the straight-line distance between them using the haversine formula. The result is shown in kilometres, miles and metres, with the compass bearing from the first point to the second.

This is the straight-line distance over the surface of the Earth, not a road or flight route. Real travel distances are longer because roads and flight paths do not follow a straight line.

How to use the calculator

Enter the latitude and longitude of the first point, then the latitude and longitude of the second point, and press Calculate distance. Coordinates use decimal degrees: latitudes run from -90 to 90 and longitudes from -180 to 180.

Use a minus sign for south latitudes and west longitudes. For example, a latitude of 23.8103 is north of the equator and a longitude of -0.1278 is west of the prime meridian.

How the distance is worked out

The tool uses the haversine formula, which finds the shortest distance between two points on a sphere from their latitudes and longitudes. The calculation uses an Earth radius of 6371 kilometres, and the working is shown next to the result.

The bearing is the initial direction of travel from the first point, measured in degrees clockwise from north. The nearest compass point is shown next to it, so a bearing of 90 degrees reads as east.

Straight line versus travel distance

The straight line is the shortest possible distance between two places, so any real journey is longer. Roads follow streets and terrain, and even flights follow routes shaped by air traffic and weather.

That is why a flight path can look curved on a flat map: the shortest route over a sphere does not look straight when the sphere is flattened. The distance this tool gives is the direct line, not the route a plane or a car would take.

Questions people ask

What format do the coordinates need?

Decimal degrees, such as 23.8103 for latitude and 90.4125 for longitude. Use a minus sign for south latitudes and west longitudes.

What is the haversine formula?

It calculates the shortest distance between two points on a sphere from their latitudes and longitudes. This page uses an Earth radius of 6371 kilometres.

What does the bearing tell me?

The bearing is the direction you would start travelling in from the first point, measured in degrees clockwise from north. The nearest compass point is shown next to it.

Why is the road distance longer?

Roads follow streets and terrain, so a journey between two places is almost always longer than the straight line this tool measures.
