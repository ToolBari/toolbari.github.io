How group points work

The group stage of the Women's World Cup uses the points system that football fans know well. A win is worth three points, a draw is worth one point to each team, and a loss is worth nothing. After three matches each, the two teams with the most points in the group go through to the round of 16, and the other two go home.

When two or more teams finish level on points, the first tiebreaker is goal difference: goals scored minus goals conceded across the three group matches. If goal difference is also level, the next tiebreaker is goals scored. Those are the standard FIFA tiebreakers, and this calculator applies them in exactly that order.

That order matters more than it might seem. A team that wins 3-0 and loses 1-0 has the same points as a team that wins 1-0 and loses 3-0, but the first team has a goal difference of plus two and the second has minus two. In a tight group, one goal in the final minutes of the last match can be the difference between going through and going home.

The six matches in a group

Four teams in a group means six matches. Each team plays the other three once, and the six pairings are fixed: team one against team two, team one against team three, team one against team four, team two against team three, team two against team four, and team three against team four. The calculator lists the matches in that order, and you can leave any of them blank if it has not been played yet.

Across the whole tournament there are eight groups, so the group stage has 48 matches in total. Every group follows the same pattern, which is why this one calculator works for any group you want to try. You can run it for the group your team ends up in, or for a group you are simply curious about.

The group stage runs from the opening match on 24 June 2027 through the first two weeks of July, with Brazil's three group matches already fixed on 24 June, 29 June and 4 July. The other groups play their matches in the same window, and the full dates are set once the draw has placed every team.

How to use the calculator

Start by naming the four teams. You can type any names you like — the 18 teams that have already qualified, your own favourite teams, or made-up names for a practice run. The names must all be different, because the calculator needs to tell the teams apart.

Then enter the scores. Each match has two boxes, one for each team's goals. Scores must be whole numbers of zero or more, and you must fill in both boxes for a match or neither: a half-entered match gets a friendly message asking you to finish it or clear it. Matches left blank are treated as not played yet, so you can build a half-finished table during the tournament.

Press the button and the table appears, sorted by points, then goal difference, then goals scored. Under the table, the working shows each team's points broken down into wins and draws, so you can check the arithmetic at a glance. The worked example button fills in a complete group for you, so you can see the calculator working before you enter your own numbers.

Worked example

Here is a complete group, worked out by hand so you can check the calculator against it. The teams are Alpha, Bravo, Charlie and Delta. The results are: Alpha 2-0 Bravo, Alpha 1-1 Charlie, Alpha 3-1 Delta, Bravo 2-2 Charlie, Bravo 1-0 Delta, and Charlie 0-0 Delta.

Alpha won two and drew one, so Alpha has 7 points. Alpha scored 6 goals and conceded 2, so the goal difference is plus 4. Bravo won one, drew one and lost one, which is 4 points, with 3 goals scored and 4 conceded, a goal difference of minus 1. Charlie drew all three matches, which is 3 points, with 3 scored and 3 conceded, a goal difference of zero. Delta drew one and lost two, which is 1 point, with 1 scored and 4 conceded, a goal difference of minus 3.

The final table reads: Alpha 7 points, Bravo 4, Charlie 3, Delta 1. Alpha and Bravo go through to the round of 16. Notice that Charlie, unbeaten in three matches, still goes out — three draws are worth fewer points than one win and one draw. That is the maths of the three-point system, and it is why teams chase a win even late in a drawn match.

What the table means

Only the top two teams in each group survive. The group winners and runners-up move into the round of 16, where the tournament becomes a straight knockout. The third and fourth placed teams are out, whatever their goal difference or how close they came. That makes the final round of group matches some of the most dramatic football of the tournament.

The table also tells you how safe a team is. A team on 7 points after three matches is always through. A team on 6 points is almost always through. A team on 4 points is often through but not always — it depends on the other results in the group. A team on 3 points can still go through if the group is tight, and a team on 1 point almost never does.

Goal difference is the tiebreaker that most often decides the close calls, which is why the calculator shows it next to the points. When you are trying out scenarios, watch the goal difference column as closely as the points column: a 3-0 win and a 1-0 win are worth the same points, but they are not worth the same place in the table.

Trying out scenarios

The most useful way to use this calculator is to ask what-if questions. What if your team wins its last match 2-0? What if the other match in the group is a draw? Change one result, press the button again, and watch the table move. That is how fans work out what their team needs on the final day of the group.

Try the last-round puzzle: with two matches left to play, work out which results send each team through. Sometimes a draw is enough, sometimes only a win will do, and sometimes a team needs a win and a favour from the other match. The calculator makes those puzzles quick to solve, because it does the sorting for you.

You can also use the calculator before the tournament starts, with the 18 teams that have already qualified. Pick any four — for example Brazil, Spain, Japan and Germany — invent some results, and see how the table would look. It is a good way to get a feel for the format before the real groups are drawn.

Questions people ask

How many points is a win worth in the group stage?

Three points for a win, one point each for a draw, and nothing for a loss. Those are the standard points used in the group stage.

What decides a tie on points?

Goal difference comes first, then goals scored. Those are the standard FIFA tiebreakers, and this calculator applies them in that order.

Can I calculate a half-finished group?

Yes. Leave any unplayed match blank and the calculator builds the table from the matches you have entered. A match must have both scores entered or both left blank.

How many group matches are there in total?

There are 48 group matches: eight groups of four teams, with six matches in each group.

Which teams are in each group?

Nobody knows yet. The groups are formed by the final draw on 11 December 2026, so until then you can use any four teams you like in the calculator.

Does the calculator save my table?

No. The calculator works fully in your browser and stores nothing, so your table disappears when you leave the page.
