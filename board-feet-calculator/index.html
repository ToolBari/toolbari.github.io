Board Feet Calculator

Enter a board's length in feet, its width and thickness in inches, and how many pieces you have, and the calculator works out its board feet. Add as many boards as you need and the calculator totals them all, with the volume in cubic feet too.

Board feet measure volume, not weight or grade. Actual lumber sizes can differ from the sizes on the label, so measure your boards if the total matters.

How board feet are worked out

One board foot is a piece of wood one foot square and one inch thick, which is 144 cubic inches. To work out the board feet of any board, multiply its length in feet by its width in inches by its thickness in inches, then divide by 12.

For example, a board 8 feet long, 6 inches wide and 2 inches thick is 8 times 6 times 2 divided by 12, which is 8 board feet. Two identical boards would be 16 board feet, which is why the calculator asks how many pieces you have.

Using the total

Add every board in your project to the list and the calculator totals them for you, along with the volume in cubic feet. That total is what lumber is usually sold by, so it is the number to take to the yard or the supplier.

It is worth allowing a little extra for offcuts and mistakes, because boards are rarely cut perfectly the first time. Measure twice, work out the total, and only then buy, so you do not end up short halfway through a job. If a board is oddly shaped, work out its board feet from the largest rectangle it can be cut from, since that is the wood you are paying for.

Questions people ask

What is a board foot?

A board foot is a unit of lumber volume: one square foot of board, one inch thick. It equals 144 cubic inches of wood.

How is the calculation done?

Multiply length in feet by width in inches by thickness in inches, then divide by 12. For example, an 8 foot board that is 6 inches wide and 2 inches thick is 8 board feet.

Why does the calculator ask for pieces?

Because identical boards are often bought in groups. The pieces count multiplies the single-board result so a whole stack is totalled in one step.

How do board feet relate to cubic feet?

One board foot is one twelfth of a cubic foot, so dividing the board-foot total by 12 gives the volume in cubic feet.
