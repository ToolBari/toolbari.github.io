CAGR Calculator

Enter a start value, an end value and the number of years to find the compound annual growth rate between them. The calculator also builds a year-by-year table showing how the value grows at that steady rate.

CAGR is a smoothed average rate, not a record of what happened each year. It is arithmetic on the numbers you enter, not financial advice, and it says nothing about future growth.

The CAGR formula

CAGR stands for compound annual growth rate. It answers one question: if a value grew at exactly the same rate every year, what rate would take it from the start value to the end value? The formula is the end value divided by the start value, raised to the power of one over the number of years, minus one.

As a worked example, a value that grows from 1000 to 2000 over 10 years has a CAGR of 7.18 percent per year. That is 2 raised to the power of 0.1, minus one. The year-by-year table shows the same story: after one year the value is 1071.77, after five years it is 1414.21, and after ten years it reaches 2000.

Reading the year-by-year table

The table applies the steady rate year after year, so each row is the previous row multiplied by one plus the rate. Real growth is rarely this smooth: some years will be above the average and some below it, and the table hides those ups and downs. Use it to understand the pace of growth, not as a record of what happened in any single year.

If the end value is lower than the start value, the CAGR is negative, which means the value shrank on average each year. The calculator handles that the same way, and the table shows the value falling year by year at the steady rate. A shorter period makes the rate more sensitive to the endpoints, so compare CAGRs over the same number of years when you compare two growth stories.

Questions people ask

What if the end value is lower than the start value?

The CAGR comes out negative, which means the value fell on average each year. The calculator shows the negative rate and the table shows the value shrinking year by year.

Can the number of years be a fraction?

Yes. Enter a value like 2.5 for two and a half years, and the calculator uses it in the formula and adds a final row to the table at that exact point.

Is CAGR the same as the average yearly growth?

No. A simple average of yearly growth rates can be misleading when the value moves up and down. CAGR is the single steady rate that connects the start and end values.

Why does the table show the same growth every year?

The table shows what steady growth at the CAGR would look like. Real growth varies from year to year, so the table is a guide to the pace, not a history.
