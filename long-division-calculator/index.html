Enter a dividend and a divisor to get the quotient and remainder with the full long-division working. Each step shows the divide, multiply and subtract stages, just as you would write them by hand.

This tool divides whole numbers only, with a dividend of at most 12 digits. It shows the standard long-division method, which is one way of dividing; the answer is the same by any correct method.

How long division works

Long division breaks a division into small repeated steps: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Take 936 divided by 4. First look at the 9: 4 goes into 9 twice, 2 times 4 is 8, and 9 minus 8 leaves 1. Bring down the 3 to make 13. Then 4 goes into 13 three times, 3 times 4 is 12, and 13 minus 12 leaves 1. Bring down the 6 to make 16. Finally 4 goes into 16 exactly four times, 4 times 4 is 16, and nothing is left. The quotient is 234 and the remainder is 0.

When the divisor does not fit into the number you are working with, the quotient digit is 0 and you bring down the next digit. For example, 1002 divided by 4 gives 250 with a remainder of 2: after working through 10 and 20, the last step is 2 divided by 4, which is 0 with 2 left over. The calculator shows every one of these steps, including the zero digits, so the working matches what you would write on paper.

Checking your answer

A quick check is to multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder. The result must equal the dividend: for 1002 divided by 4, 250 times 4 is 1000, plus the remainder 2 gives 1002. The remainder is always smaller than the divisor. If it is not, the quotient digit at that step was too small.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between the quotient and the remainder?

The quotient is how many whole times the divisor fits into the dividend, and the remainder is what is left over. For 1002 divided by 4, the quotient is 250 and the remainder is 2.

Can I divide by zero?

No. Division by zero is not defined, so the calculator asks for a divisor that is not zero.

Why does a zero appear in the middle of my quotient?

A zero appears when the divisor does not fit into the number formed at that step, so you bring down the next digit. It is a real digit of the answer, not a mistake.

How do I check a long division answer?

Multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder. The result must equal the dividend you started with.
