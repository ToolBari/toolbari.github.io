What makes a mission statement work

A good mission statement is short, true, and about the people you serve. One or two sentences is enough. It should name the people, the work, and the value behind it, without jargon and without claiming more than you do.

Test it by reading it to someone who knows your organisation. If they nod, it is close. If they laugh or look puzzled, the words are not yet true. The best mission statements sound obvious once you hear them, because they describe what is already there.

Choosing a tone

Warm suits organisations built on care and relationships, such as charities, schools, and community groups. Bold suits teams that want to sound confident and direct. Plain suits anyone who dislikes decoration and wants the words to do the work.

Hopeful suits work that looks forward: education, health, and anything that helps people build a better future. The tone changes the phrasing, not the facts, so pick the one your audience would recognise as you.

Honest limits

This tool assembles your words into sentence patterns. It cannot know whether the value you named is one you live by, or whether the work you describe is what you actually do. A mission statement that is not true does more harm than none at all.

If none of the six statements feels right, the inputs may need more thought. Try writing who you serve and what you do in plainer words, and pick a value you can point to with a real example.

Questions people ask

How long should a mission statement be?

One or two sentences is enough. If it takes a paragraph to explain what you do, the statement is trying to cover too much, and trimming it will usually make it stronger.

What is the difference between a mission and a vision?

A mission describes what you do now and for whom. A vision describes the future you are working towards. This tool builds mission statements; a vision needs a different conversation.

Can I edit the statements?

Yes. They are starting points built from your words. Change any phrase, combine two statements, or use one as the first draft of something better.

Does this tool know my organisation?

No. It only uses the words you enter here. That is why the result is only as true as your inputs, so write them honestly.
