Combination and Permutation Calculator

Enter n and r to get the number of combinations (nCr) and permutations (nPr), with or without repetition. The formula is shown with your numbers filled in.

Counts here are exact whole numbers computed with big-integer arithmetic, but results with more than 1000 digits are not displayed. The tool counts selections only; it does not list them.

Combinations and permutations

A combination counts groups where the order does not matter. Choosing 3 people from 10 for a committee is a combination: the same three people in a different order are the same committee. A permutation counts arrangements where the order does matter, such as the first, second and third places in a race. The formulas are nCr = n! / (r! x (n - r)!) and nPr = n! / (n - r)!. For n = 10 and r = 3, nCr is 120 and nPr is 720. The permutation count is larger because each group of 3 can be arranged in 3! = 6 orders, and 120 x 6 = 720.

With repetition allowed, the same item can be chosen more than once. The formulas change: combinations with repetition are C(n + r - 1, r) and permutations with repetition are simply n^r, because each of the r positions has n choices. For n = 10 and r = 3, that gives 220 combinations with repetition and 1000 permutations with repetition.

Reading the formula

The exclamation mark means factorial: 5! is 5 x 4 x 3 x 2 x 1 = 120, and 0! is defined as 1. The calculator cancels the factorials before multiplying, which keeps the arithmetic exact. For 10C3 it computes (10 x 9 x 8) / (3 x 2 x 1) = 120 instead of dividing two large factorials.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between nCr and nPr?

nCr counts groups where order does not matter, while nPr counts arrangements where order does matter. For n = 10 and r = 3, nCr is 120 and nPr is 720.

When do I use repetition?

Use repetition when the same item can be chosen more than once, such as picking 3 scoops of ice cream from 10 flavours. Without repetition each item can be chosen at most once.

What does n! mean?

n! is the factorial of n: the product of all whole numbers from 1 to n. For example 5! is 5 x 4 x 3 x 2 x 1 = 120, and 0! is defined as 1.

Why must r be no bigger than n without repetition?

Without repetition you cannot choose more items than exist. With repetition allowed, r can be larger than n because items may be chosen again.
