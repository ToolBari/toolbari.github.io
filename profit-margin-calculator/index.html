The three formulas

  • Profit = selling price − cost
  • Margin = profit ÷ selling price × 100
  • Markup = profit ÷ cost × 100

Worked examples

  1. Cost 800, price 1,000: profit 200 → margin 20%, markup 25%.
  2. Cost 1,500, price 2,000: profit 500 → margin 25%, markup 33.33%.
  3. Cost 250, price 400: profit 150 → margin 37.5%, markup 60%.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between profit margin and markup?

Margin divides the profit by the selling price; markup divides the profit by the cost. On a cost of 800 and a selling price of 1,000, the profit is 200, the margin is 20%, and the markup is 25%.

How is profit margin calculated?

Subtract the cost from the selling price to get the profit, then divide the profit by the selling price and multiply by 100. For example, (1,000 − 800) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 20%.

What is a good profit margin?

There is no single good number — it depends on delivery costs, fees, returns, taxes and the market. This calculator only shows the arithmetic; it does not advise what to charge.
