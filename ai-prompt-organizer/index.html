Save prompts with a title and tags, then search and filter them whenever you need one. Everything is stored in your own browser, so your prompts stay on your device and work offline.

This tool works fully in your browser and calls no AI service, API, or backend. Prompts are stored in this browser only, so clearing browser data removes them. Export your prompts as text to keep a backup.

How to organize your prompts

Give every prompt a short, clear title that says what it is for, such as the task and the audience. Add one or two tags that describe the topic or the project, then save. Later, type a word in the search box to find prompts by title, text, or tag, or pick a tag from the filter to see only that group. Copy a prompt when you need it, edit it when you improve it, and delete the ones you no longer use. A small, tidy collection beats a long list you cannot search.

Tagging tips that keep search useful

Tags work best when they are short and consistent. Pick a handful of tags you will actually reuse, such as writing, work, study, or fun, instead of a new tag for every prompt. Use the same spelling each time, since the filter treats different spellings as different tags. If a prompt fits two groups, give it both tags. When a tag stops being useful, edit the prompts that carry it and drop it. The tag filter always shows the tags your saved prompts use, so it grows with your collection.

Your data stays in your browser

Saved prompts live in your browser's own storage on this device. Nothing is sent to a server, no account is needed, and the organizer works fully offline. That also means your prompts do not follow you to another device or browser, and clearing browser data removes them. Use the export button to download all your prompts as a plain text file, and keep that file somewhere safe if your collection matters to you.

Limits of browser storage

Browser storage is a convenience, not a backup service. Browsers may clear stored data when space runs low, and private browsing sessions often discard it when the window closes. Very long prompt collections can also slow the page down. Keep the collection focused on prompts you actually reuse, export a backup now and then, and treat the exported text file as the real copy of anything important. A quick export once a month is enough for most people.

Questions people ask

Where are my prompts stored?

They are stored in your browser's local storage on this device. Nothing is sent to a server, and no account is needed.

Can I use my prompts on another device?

Not automatically. Browser storage stays on one device, so use the export button to get a text file and move that file to your other device.

How do tags work?

Add tags separated by commas when you save a prompt. The tag filter lists every tag you have used, and search also matches tag words.

What happens if I clear my browser data?

Your saved prompts are removed, because they live in browser storage. Export them as text first if you want to keep them.
