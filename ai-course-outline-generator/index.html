How to use the course outline generator

Type your course topic, pick a level, and choose how many modules you want, from three to six. The tool builds an outline in the classic shape: an opening module, core ideas, practice, common mistakes, going further, and a final project. Each module has three lessons and one learning objective written as a sentence starting with you can. Copy the outline when the shape fits your course.

The level you pick changes the guidance shown with the outline. Beginner assumes no prior knowledge, intermediate assumes the basics, and advanced assumes solid experience. The module shape stays the same at every level, because good courses follow the same arc whatever the subject.

Why every module needs one objective

A learning objective says what a learner can do after the module, in plain words. One objective per module keeps the teaching focused: if a lesson does not serve the objective, it does not belong in the module. Objectives also help learners, who can see what they are working toward and check their own progress at the end.

Write objectives with a doing verb: explain, describe, complete, spot, plan, finish. Avoid vague verbs such as understand or know, which cannot be checked. The objectives in this outline use doing verbs, so you can test each one with a short task or a question at the end of the module.

From outline to finished course

An outline is a plan, not a course. Once the shape is right, work through one module at a time: write the lesson content, add one worked example per lesson, and finish each module with a short practice task. Keep lessons short enough to finish in one sitting, and put the hardest idea in the middle of the module, when attention is highest.

Check every fact in your lessons against a reliable source, especially numbers, dates, and how-to steps. Learners trust a course that gets the details right, and one wrong fact can undo that trust. Ask someone who knows the subject to read the outline before you build the lessons.

Questions people ask

What does the outline include?

Each module has a title, three lessons, and one learning objective. The outline also shows the course level and a short note about what that level assumes.

How many modules should a course have?

Three modules suit a short introductory course, and six suit a full course with a final project. Four is a good middle ground for most topics.

Can I change the lessons?

Yes. The outline is a template-built starting point. Rename lessons, move them between modules, and rewrite the objectives to fit your learners.

Does the tool save my outline?

No. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere or stored. Copy the outline before you leave the page.
