How the planner works

The planner adds up six categories of Easter spending: food and drink, decorations, eggs and treats, gifts, travel and visits, and other. Enter what you plan to spend in each one, and it shows the total, plus each category's share of that total as a percentage, so you can see at a glance where the money is going.

If you set an overall limit, the planner compares the total with it and tells you how much room is left, or how far over you are. The limit is yours to set: this page never suggests one, because a sensible Easter budget depends entirely on your own household and circumstances.

A worked example

Suppose you plan to spend 120 on food and drink, 30 on decorations, 40 on eggs and treats, 60 on gifts, 50 on travel and visits, and 20 on other things. Enter those six numbers and the planner shows a total of 320.

Food is the biggest share at 37.5 percent of the total, because 120 divided by 320 is 0.375. Gifts come next at 18.75 percent, then travel at 15.63 percent. If your limit is 350, the planner shows 30 left inside it. The percentages always add up to 100, apart from tiny rounding differences.

The six categories

Food and drink usually takes the biggest share: the Easter meal, drinks, and anything you bake or buy for guests. Decorations cover table settings, flowers, and anything that makes the house feel festive. Eggs and treats are the chocolate eggs, sweets, and small surprises, especially if children are involved.

Gifts are presents for family and friends, travel and visits cover the cost of getting to gatherings or hosting people who travel to you, and other catches everything else: cards, wrapping, party supplies, or a spring outing. If a cost fits nowhere else, other is where it belongs.

Keeping Easter inside your limit

The best time to set a limit is before you start spending, not after. Decide the total first, then split it across the categories with this planner, and shop to the plan. If the first version of the plan comes out over the limit, trim the biggest category first: a small cut to food saves more than a large cut to decorations.

Watch the small extras, because they are the usual reason plans fail. A few treats here and a decoration there add up quietly, and by Easter Sunday the total can be far above the plan. Writing the numbers down, even roughly, is usually enough to keep them honest.

Easter 2027

Easter Sunday 2027 falls on 28 March 2027, with Good Friday on 26 March and Easter Monday on 29 March. The long weekend is a natural time for family gatherings, which is why food and travel often take the biggest shares of the Easter budget.

Because Easter moves from year to year, it is worth planning a few weeks ahead. Book travel early if you are visiting family, buy the eggs and treats before the last-minute rush, and leave the final days for cooking and for enjoying the weekend itself.

Planning for guests

Food spending scales with the number of guests, so count heads before you count costs. A meal for four costs roughly half a meal for eight, and the planner's food category should grow with the guest list. If guests are bringing a dish each, the food figure can come down, and the saving can go elsewhere.

Travel works the other way: if you are hosting, your guests carry the travel cost, but you may spend more on food and drink. If you are travelling, the travel category grows and the food category shrinks. The planner does not care which way round it is, as long as the numbers are yours.

Saving money on Easter

Easter does not have to be expensive. Decorations bought once and stored carefully can be used for years, which makes the decorations category tiny after the first spring. Baking at home costs less than buying ready-made treats, and a home-made Easter cake is usually the thing guests remember best.

Sharing costs with family helps too. If several households are gathering, agree in advance who brings what: one brings the main dish, another the dessert, another the drinks. The planner still works the same way, but each household enters only its own share, and the total stays honest for everyone.

Easter with children

Children are the heart of Easter, and the happiest parts of the day cost very little. An egg hunt in the garden or the park needs nothing more than a bag of small eggs and a few hiding places, and the excitement is the same whether the eggs are large or small. Decorating boiled eggs with paint or dye is another cheap pleasure that fills a whole afternoon.

Keep the treats category modest and let the activities carry the day. A picnic, a walk to look for spring flowers, or an afternoon of crafts costs almost nothing and is remembered far longer than another chocolate egg. The planner's eggs and treats figure is a guide, not a target: less is often more.

After Easter: what to keep

When Easter is over, keep the plan. Decorations can be stored and used again next year, which makes next year's decorations category much smaller. And the numbers themselves are useful: knowing what you spent this year makes next year's limit much easier to set.

Note what worked and what did not. If the food figure was too high, plan a simpler meal next year. If the eggs and treats figure was too low, the children probably noticed. A planner is only as good as the memory it feeds, and a few notes now save a fresh guess next spring.

It is also worth noting the dates. Easter Sunday 2027 is 28 March, but Easter moves every year, and next year's date will be different. Write the dates on the plan when you file it away, so the numbers and the weekend they belong to stay together.

Questions people ask

How do I use the planner?

Enter what you plan to spend in each of the six categories, set an overall limit if you have one, and press the button. The planner shows your total, each category's share as a percentage, and whether you are inside or over your limit.

What if I leave a category blank?

It counts as zero. Leave blank any category you are not spending on, and the planner will ignore it in the total and show it as zero percent of the total.

What if I have no limit?

Leave the limit blank. The planner still shows the total and the share of each category, and simply skips the over-or-under comparison.

What counts as other?

Anything that fits in none of the five named categories: cards, wrapping, party supplies, a spring outing, or any small cost that belongs to Easter but has no home of its own.

When is Easter 2027?

Easter Sunday 2027 falls on 28 March 2027, with Good Friday on 26 March and Easter Monday on 29 March.

Does this tool suggest prices?

No. All amounts are yours to enter in your own currency, and the limit is yours to set. This page never suggests a price or a budget.
