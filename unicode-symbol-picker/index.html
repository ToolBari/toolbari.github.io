What is a codepoint?

A codepoint is the number Unicode gives to a character, written as the letter U, a plus sign and hexadecimal digits. U+2192 is the rightwards arrow, and the same codepoint means the same character on every device, in every program. The codepoint is the reliable way to name a symbol when its shape is hard to describe.

The shape you see depends on the font your device uses, so the same codepoint can look slightly different on different screens. If a symbol shows as a blank box, your device's font does not include it, but the codepoint is still correct and the copied character will show properly where the font supports it. Searching by codepoint is the fastest way to check a symbol you saw elsewhere.

Questions people ask

How do I copy a symbol?

Click the symbol and it is copied to your clipboard, ready to paste. The codepoint is shown next to it so you can check you picked the right one.

Why are currency symbols shown without the symbol itself?

This site never prints currency marks, so currency entries show the name and codepoint only, such as Dollar Sign U+0024. Clicking one copies its name and codepoint instead.

What is a Unicode codepoint?

A codepoint is the number Unicode gives to a character, written as U followed by a plus sign and hexadecimal digits. U+2192 is the rightwards arrow, and the same codepoint means the same character on every device.

Why does a symbol look different on my phone?

The shape of a symbol depends on the font your device uses, so the same codepoint can look slightly different on different screens. A very old font may show a blank box for a symbol it does not include.
