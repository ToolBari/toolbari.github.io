How to use the sunrise and sunset calculator

  1. Enter the latitude and longitude of the place. Latitude runs from -90 at the South Pole to 90 at the North Pole; longitude runs from -180 to 180, with east of Greenwich positive.
  2. Pick the date you want times for.
  3. Enter the UTC offset of the place in hours — for example 6 for Bangladesh, which is 6 hours ahead of UTC.
  4. Press Calculate sunrise and sunset. The tool shows sunrise, sunset, solar noon and day length, with the working underneath.

How the calculation works

The tool follows the NOAA solar-calculator algorithm. It converts the date to a Julian day and Julian century, then works out the Sun's geometric mean longitude and anomaly, applies the equation of center to get the true and apparent longitude, and finds the obliquity of the ecliptic. From those it gets the solar declination and the equation of time, then the hour angle at the standard zenith of 90.833 degrees — the point where the Sun's upper edge meets the horizon, with refraction included. Solar noon is 720 minutes minus 4 minutes per degree of longitude minus the equation of time, and sunrise and sunset are solar noon minus and plus 4 minutes per degree of hour angle. A second pass recomputes the declination and equation of time at each first-pass event time, which tightens the result.

Questions people ask

How accurate is this sunrise and sunset calculator?

The results are accurate to about 1 minute for a flat sea-level horizon with standard refraction. The NOAA algorithm is the same method used by almanacs, but hills, buildings and weather can shift the times you actually see.

What does the zenith of 90.833 degrees mean?

Sunrise and sunset are timed when the Sun's upper edge touches the horizon. The zenith of 90.833 degrees allows for the Sun's apparent radius and for refraction bending sunlight near the horizon.

Why does the Sun never rise or never set on some dates?

Near the poles the Sun can stay below the horizon all day in winter or above it all day in summer. When that happens for your latitude and date, this calculator says so instead of showing a time.

What is solar noon?

Solar noon is the moment the Sun crosses your local meridian and reaches its highest point for the day. It falls halfway between sunrise and sunset and is rarely at 12:00 on the clock.
