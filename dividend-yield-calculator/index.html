Dividend Yield Calculator

Enter the annual dividend per share and the current share price to find the dividend yield as a percentage. If you also enter how many shares you own, the calculator shows the yearly income those shares would pay at that dividend.

Dividends can change or stop, and share prices move. This calculator does arithmetic on the numbers you enter and is not investment advice.

The dividend yield formula

Dividend yield is the annual dividend per share divided by the share price, multiplied by 100. It tells you how much income each share pays in a year as a percentage of what the share costs. A yield of 4 percent means each share pays back 4 percent of its price in dividends over a year.

As a worked example, an annual dividend of 2.40 per share with a share price of 60 gives a yield of 4.00 percent, because 2.40 divided by 60 is 0.04. If you own 100 shares, the yearly income is 2.40 times 100, which is 240.

From yield to yearly income

The shares field is optional. Leave it empty if you only want the yield percentage, or fill it in to see the yearly income for your holding. The income is the dividend per share times the number of shares, assuming the dividend stays the same for the whole year. If the company pays dividends four times a year, each quarterly payment is one quarter of the annual figure, so a yearly income of 240 arrives as four payments of 60.

Yield moves when the price moves, even if the dividend does not change. If the share price falls, the same dividend becomes a bigger percentage of the price, so the yield rises. A high yield can therefore mean a cheap share, a generous dividend, or a falling price, and the number alone cannot tell you which.

Questions people ask

What is the annual dividend per share?

It is the total dividend one share pays over a year. If a company pays quarterly, add the four quarterly payments together to get the annual figure.

Why does the yield change when the price changes?

Yield is the dividend divided by the price, so a lower price makes the same dividend a bigger percentage. The dividend itself has not changed, only the price it is measured against.

Do I have to enter the shares I own?

No, that field is optional. Leave it empty to see only the yield percentage, or fill it in to see the yearly income for your holding.

Is a high dividend yield always good?

Not necessarily. A very high yield can come from a share price that has fallen sharply, which may signal trouble. This calculator only does the arithmetic and is not investment advice.
