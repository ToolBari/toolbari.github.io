Women's World Cup 2027 Match Schedule

What is confirmed so far

The Women's World Cup 2027 is hosted by Brazil, and the tournament runs from Thursday 24 June to Sunday 25 July 2027. Thirty-two teams take part, and 64 matches are played in total. Those are the settled facts, and everything else on this page is built from them.

Only a handful of dates and venues are confirmed at the level of individual matches. The opening match is Brazil against an opponent decided by the draw, at the Maracana in Rio de Janeiro on 24 June. Brazil's other two group matches are also fixed: 29 June in Sao Paulo and 4 July in Brasilia. The final is at the Maracana on 25 July. That is the complete list of confirmed match dates, and this page does not add to it.

The other confirmed date on the road to the tournament is the final draw, on Friday 11 December 2026 at the Museum of Modern Art in Rio de Janeiro. The draw is the moment the groups are formed, and it is the moment the full fixture list becomes possible. Until then, any list of fixtures you see anywhere else is either a guess or a placeholder.

The eight host cities

Eight cities host matches. Rio de Janeiro has the Maracana, the most famous stadium in Brazil, and it hosts both the opening match and the final. Sao Paulo's Arena Itaquera hosts Brazil's second group match, and Brasilia's Estadio Nacional hosts the third.

The other five host cities are Belo Horizonte with the Mineirao, Fortaleza with the Arena Castelao, Porto Alegre with the Beira-Rio, Recife with the Arena de Pernambuco, and Salvador with the Arena Fonte Nova. Between them they cover the south, the south-east, the centre-west and the north-east of the country, so the tournament travels a long way across Brazil.

For anyone thinking about which matches to watch, the cities matter because Brazil's group matches are spread across three of them. The opening match and the final are both in Rio, which makes the Maracana the stadium of the tournament's two biggest days. The full allocation of matches to cities is part of the schedule framework, and the details of which teams play where are only settled once the draw has placed every team in a group.

How the tournament is structured

The format is the same shape as the last few Women's World Cups. Thirty-two teams are divided into eight groups of four. Every team plays the other three teams in its group once, which gives six matches per group and 48 group matches in total. The top two teams in each group go through to the round of 16.

From the round of 16 onward, the tournament is a straight knockout. Sixteen teams become eight in the round of 16, then four in the quarter-finals, then two in the semi-finals. There is a third-place match between the two losing semi-finalists, and then the final decides the champions. The knockout rounds add 16 matches to the 48 group matches, which is how the total reaches 64.

Points in the group stage follow the standard system: three points for a win and one point each for a draw. If teams finish level on points, goal difference is the first tiebreaker, followed by goals scored. That is the standard FIFA practice, and it is why a single goal in a group match can change who goes through.

What is not decided yet

Honesty matters more than completeness here. As of mid-November 2026, eighteen teams have qualified, and fourteen places are still being decided. The qualified teams are Brazil as hosts, Denmark, France, Germany and Spain from Europe, Argentina and Colombia from South America, Australia, China PR, Japan, Korea DPR, Korea Republic and the Philippines from Asia, Algeria, Cameroon, Malawi and Morocco from Africa, and New Zealand from Oceania.

Some famous teams are not on that list yet. The United States and England, the European champions, had not qualified as of mid-November 2026 and were still playing for their places. The other fourteen places are still being decided.

Group memberships, team-versus-team fixtures and kick-off times are all still unknown. They depend on the draw on 11 December 2026, which is the moment the eight groups are formed. Everything else is decided after that.

Planning around the big days

The opening match is the natural first date in any plan. Brazil open the tournament at the Maracana, and an opening match always carries extra ceremony, so it is worth setting the evening aside rather than fitting it around other plans. Brazil's second and third group matches, on 29 June and 4 July, are the other two dates that are already fixed for the hosts.

The final on 25 July is the date to protect most carefully. It is a Sunday, it is at the Maracana, and it is the match the whole month builds toward. If you are planning a gathering, the final is the obvious day for it, and the earlier matches are good chances to try out your setup with a smaller crowd.

Between the confirmed dates there is a lot of football to discover once the draw is made. The best approach is to lock in the five confirmed moments now and then come back after 11 December 2026, when the groups are known, to fill in the matches that matter to you — your own team's group games, the matches in your favourite city, and the knockout rounds.
