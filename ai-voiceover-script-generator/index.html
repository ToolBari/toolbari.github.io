How to use the voiceover script generator

Type your topic, pick a duration of 15, 30, or 60 seconds, and choose a tone. You can also name your audience, and the script will include a line written for them. Press the button and the tool assembles a narration script: an opening line, body lines, and a closing line, with pause markers showing where the speaker should breathe. Above the script you see the word-count target for your duration and the actual word count of the script. The timing estimate comes from 150 words per minute, a common speaking pace. It is approximate, so always read the script aloud with a timer before you record.

Word targets for each duration

At 150 words per minute, a 15-second script needs about 38 words, a 30-second script about 75 words, and a 60-second script about 150 words. Those are targets, not rules: a calm speaker reads fewer words in the same time, and an energetic speaker reads more. Pauses also take time, which is why the script includes pause markers.

If your script runs long, cut a body line or shorten the opening. If it runs short, add one more body line rather than stretching the words you have. A script that fits its slot with room for two short pauses sounds confident; one that rushes to fit sounds nervous.

Reading a script well

Mark the words you want to stress before you record, and read the script aloud three times first. The first read finds the awkward phrases. The second finds the rhythm. The third is the one you can record with confidence. If a line trips you up twice, rewrite it: the fault is in the sentence, not in you.

Pauses matter as much as words. A short pause after the opening line lets the listener settle in, and a pause before the closing line gives it weight. The pause markers in this script show where to breathe, but trust your ear: if a pause feels too long, shorten it, and if a line runs together, add one.

Questions people ask

How accurate is the timing estimate?

It is approximate. It assumes 150 words per minute, a common speaking pace, but real pace varies by speaker and by script. Always read the script aloud and time it.

What do the pause markers mean?

Each [pause] marker is a short break where the speaker should breathe and let the listener catch up. A pause usually lasts about one second.

Can I change the script?

Yes. The script is template-built and meant to be edited. Rewrite any line in your own words and adjust the length to fit your slot.

Does the tool save my script?

No. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere or stored. Copy the script before you leave the page.
