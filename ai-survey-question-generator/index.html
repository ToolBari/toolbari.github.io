How to use the survey question generator

Type what the survey is about and who will answer it, then pick a question type. Satisfaction suits products and services. Feedback suits general opinions. Usability suits websites and tools. Learning suits courses and training. Mixed takes questions from all four types. Press the button and you get exactly ten questions, each with a suggested answer scale. Copy the whole set when it fits your survey.

The scales are suggestions, not rules. A five-point scale suits opinions and ratings. A yes or no scale suits facts. Open text suits anything you cannot predict. Use the scale that matches what you want to learn from each question, and change any scale that does not fit.

Writing questions people answer honestly

One question should ask one thing. A question that asks two things at once, such as how fast and how friendly a service was, cannot be answered with one rating. Keep questions short, use plain words, and avoid leading phrases that push the reader toward a nice answer. If a question sounds like it wants praise, it will get praise, and you will learn nothing.

Put the easy questions first and the open questions last. Early wins keep people going, and open questions at the end catch anything the earlier questions missed. Ten questions is a good length: enough to learn from, short enough to finish. Longer surveys get abandoned halfway, and the answers you lose are the ones from your busiest readers.

Testing before you send

Always test a survey on a small group first. Give it to five people who match your audience and watch where they pause, reread, or ask what a question means. Those pauses mark the questions to rewrite. A question that confuses one tester in five will confuse many more in the full survey.

After the test run, check the answers as well as the questions. If everyone picks the same answer, the question may be too easy or too leading. If answers spread across the whole scale, the question is doing its job. Fix the weak questions, then send the survey out with confidence.
