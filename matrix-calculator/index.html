Adding, subtracting and multiplying

Two matrices add or subtract entry by entry: the top-left entry of A + B is the top-left entry of A plus the top-left entry of B, and so on for every position. Multiplication is different. To multiply A by B, take each row of A and each column of B, multiply the matching entries and add the products. For 2 by 2 matrices, the top-left entry of A times B is a11 times b11 plus a12 times b21. The order matters: A times B is usually not the same as B times A.

Because this calculator uses square matrices of the same size, every operation here is defined for the size you pick. With 2 by 2 matrices there are 4 entries to combine; with 3 by 3 there are 9. The steps section shows the arithmetic for each entry of the result, so you can check the answer by hand.

Determinants

The determinant is a single number that can be computed from a square matrix. For a 2 by 2 matrix with entries a, b on the first row and c, d on the second, the determinant is a times d minus b times c. For a 3 by 3 matrix, expand along the first row: multiply each entry by the determinant of the 2 by 2 matrix left after removing its row and column, with alternating signs. A determinant of zero means the matrix has no inverse.

Questions people ask

How do I multiply two matrices?

Take each row of the first matrix and each column of the second, multiply the matching entries and add the products. For 2 by 2 matrices the top-left entry of the product is a11 times b11 plus a12 times b21.

Can I add matrices of different sizes?

No. Addition and subtraction need both matrices to have the same number of rows and columns, so this calculator always uses the same size for A and B.

What is a determinant?

The determinant is a single number computed from a square matrix. For a 2 by 2 matrix it is a times d minus b times c. It is zero exactly when the matrix has no inverse.

Does the order of multiplication matter?

Yes. Matrix multiplication is not commutative, so A times B is usually different from B times A. The calculator always computes A times B in the order you entered them.
