Refresh Rate Test

This test samples your display with requestAnimationFrame and measures how many frames your screen actually shows per second. It reports the refresh rate in hertz, the average frame time, and draws a frame-time graph so you can see how steady the frames are.

Press Start test and keep this tab in view for about two seconds while the test samples frames.

Each bar is one frame. Taller bars mean longer frames, so a flat graph means a steady refresh rate.

The result measures what this browser tab achieves on this display, not the screen's advertised maximum. Background tabs, power saving and other programs can change the reading, so run the test with this tab in view and nothing heavy running.

How the test works

The test asks the browser for a new animation frame, over and over, and records the time between each pair of frames. After about 120 frames it works out the average gap and turns it into a refresh rate in hertz. A hertz is one frame per second, so a display showing 60 frames per second refreshes at 60 hertz.

The frame-time graph draws one bar per sampled frame. When the bars are all about the same height, the display is refreshing steadily. A few tall bars among short ones mean some frames took much longer than the rest, which is what a stutter feels like when you watch a video or play a game.

What the numbers mean

A 60 hertz display shows a new frame about every 16.7 milliseconds, a 120 hertz display about every 8.3 milliseconds, and a 144 hertz display about every 6.9 milliseconds. Shorter frame times mean motion looks smoother, which is why higher refresh rates feel nicer when scrolling or gaming.

If your measured rate is lower than your screen's advertised rate, check that the browser tab is in view, that power saving is off, and that no other program is using the graphics heavily. Laptops on battery often cap the refresh rate to save power, and some screens only reach their top rate at certain resolutions.

Questions people ask

What is refresh rate?

The refresh rate is how many times per second a display draws a new image, measured in hertz (Hz). A 60 Hz display draws 60 frames per second, so each frame lasts about 16.7 milliseconds.

Why does my result differ from my screen's advertised rate?

The test measures what this browser tab actually achieves on this display. Power saving, background work, and the browser itself can lower the measured rate below the screen's maximum.

How long does the test take?

About two seconds. The test samples around 120 frames and then reports the average rate, the average frame time, and a graph of every frame.

Does the test work on a phone or tablet?

Yes, if the browser supports animation-frame timing. Keep the page in view while it measures, because background tabs are slowed down by the browser.
