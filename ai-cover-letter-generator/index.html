What makes a cover letter work

A good cover letter does three things: it names the role and the company, it shows two or three strengths that match the job, and it gives one real result that proves them. Short is better than long: three or four short paragraphs are enough for most roles. Use the employer's own words where they fit, because the person reading the letter is looking for a match with the job description. Avoid repeating your resume line by line; the letter should add the story behind it.

Choosing a tone

Tone is the feeling your letter gives off, and it should match the workplace. A formal company expects a professional tone, while a small creative team may welcome a warmer one. Confident wording suits roles where you need to show independence, and a concise tone suits busy hiring managers who read many letters. Whatever tone you pick, keep it steady through the whole letter. Mixing a formal opening with a casual closing makes the letter feel unfinished.

Before you send it

Read the letter out loud once. Fix anything that sounds unlike you, and check every fact: the company name, the role title, your strengths, and your achievement. Make sure the achievement is true and that you could talk about it in an interview. Check the spelling of names, and confirm the letter is addressed the way the employer asked. A letter that is honest, specific, and free of errors makes a far better impression than a long one.
