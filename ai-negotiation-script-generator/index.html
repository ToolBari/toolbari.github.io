AI Negotiation Script Generator

This tool turns your goal into a short negotiation script you can practise before the conversation. Enter what you want, why it is fair, and the objection you expect, then pick a tone and get an opener, a case, a reply and a close.

A script prepares your wording, not the outcome. The other side may say no, and some decisions are outside their power to change. This tool works fully in your browser and calls no AI service, API, or backend. It assembles your words into a talking-points script; the negotiation itself is yours.

How to use this script builder

Start with the clearest version of what you want, in one sentence. Then write your reason in plain words: what makes the request fair, and what evidence supports it. Add the objection you most expect to hear, pick the tone that fits the relationship, and press the button.

The tool builds four parts: an opener that sets the tone, your case, a reply to the objection, and a close that asks for a next step. Read the whole script aloud once. Change any sentence that does not sound like something you would actually say.

Why each part of the script matters

The opener matters because the first minute sets the tone for the whole conversation. A calm, respectful opening makes the other person more willing to listen. The case is where you state the facts: what you want, why it is reasonable, and what supports it.

The objection reply shows you have thought about the other side. People negotiate better with someone who has already considered their concerns. The close matters most of all: without a clear next step, even a good conversation can end with nothing agreed.

Tips for the real conversation

Speak slowly and pause after your main point. Silence feels awkward, but it gives the other person time to think, and filling it often weakens your case. Listen to the full reply before you answer, and repeat their concern in your own words so they feel heard.

Take notes during the conversation, and agree on the next step before you leave: who does what, and by when. If you are caught off guard, it is fine to say you need time to think. A short pause beats a rushed promise.

Honest limits

No script can predict how the other person will respond, and a prepared reply is only a starting point. If the conversation takes an unexpected turn, drop the script and listen. The goal is a fair agreement, not a perfect performance.

This tool works with the words you give it. It cannot judge whether your request is reasonable, check any facts you state, or tell you what the other side will accept. Use it to prepare, then trust your judgement in the room.

Questions people ask

Can a script guarantee a yes?

No. A script helps you say your case clearly and calmly, but the other side may still say no. Preparation improves your chances; it does not control the outcome.

Which tone should I pick?

Friendly suits a good relationship where you want to keep things warm. Firm suits a request you have already raised and need settled. Collaborative suits a problem you want to solve together.

What if they say no straight away?

Ask what would need to change for it to work, and whether there is a smaller step you could agree on. A no to the full request is often a yes to part of it.

Does this tool negotiate for me?

No. It prepares the wording you will use. The conversation, the listening, and the agreement are all yours.
