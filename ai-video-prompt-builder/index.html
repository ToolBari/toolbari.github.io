How to build a good video prompt

Start with a subject doing something, not just standing still: a cat crossing a garden is a better subject than a cat alone. Then pick a shot type to set the framing, a camera movement to set the motion, a style for the look, and a mood for the feeling. The duration hint tells the video tool how long the clip should feel. Add extra details for anything the pickers do not cover, such as the light, the background, or a second element in the scene.

Camera movement, explained

Camera movement is what makes a video prompt different from an image prompt. Static holds the camera still while the subject moves. Pan moves the camera left or right, tilt moves it up or down, and dolly moves it closer or further away. Orbit circles the subject, tracking follows it, and handheld adds a natural, slightly unsteady feel. Pick the movement that matches the action: a slow dolly in suits a quiet moment, while a tracking shot suits something moving through a scene.

Trying variations

The two variation buttons keep your subject, shot type, mood, duration, and extra details, and change only the camera movement and style. Each variation picks a different pair, so you can compare how the same scene feels with a still camera and a cinematic look versus a moving camera and a documentary look. If one movement feels right, rebuild with it and try the styles one by one until the mood matches what you imagined.

What this tool does not do

This tool writes prompt text only. It does not generate video, and it is not connected to any video service. Use it to write the words, then paste them into a video tool of your choice. Short, simple actions work best in generated clips, and very long or complicated scenes often come out muddled, so keep the subject and the action clear and let the pickers do the rest.

Questions people ask

Does this tool make videos?

No. It writes prompt text only and generates no video. Paste the finished prompt into a video tool of your choice to make the clip.

What is a shot type?

It is how the camera frames the subject: a wide shot shows the whole scene, a close-up fills the frame with one detail, and a medium shot sits between the two.

How do the variations work?

Each variation keeps your subject, shot type, mood, duration, and extra details, and changes only the camera movement and style to a different pair.

How long should a video prompt be?

One or two sentences is enough. Name the subject and the action, then add the shot, movement, style, and mood. Extra detail helps, but a long list of demands often confuses the result.
