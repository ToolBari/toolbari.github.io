How to test your mouse

Start by moving the mouse slowly across the panel and watching the trail follow it. Then press each button in turn: left, middle (usually the wheel pressed down) and right. Each press and release is shown by name, so you can confirm every button works.

Next, spin the scroll wheel up and down and check the direction shown matches the way you moved it. Finish with a few single clicks and a double-click, and watch the counters climb. If anything does not respond, try the mouse in another port or with fresh batteries before deciding it is broken.

What to look for

A healthy mouse gives a smooth trail, a press and release for every button, and wheel turns that match the direction you spin. A trail that jumps or stutters, a button that only works sometimes, or a wheel that scrolls the wrong way can all be signs of dirt, a worn switch or a low battery.

Wireless mice can also lag or drop events when the receiver is far away or another wireless device is nearby. If the test looks wrong on one computer but fine on another, the computer or its settings are the more likely cause than the mouse itself.

Questions people ask

Which mouse buttons does the test show?

Left, middle and right buttons are shown by name. Extra side buttons are reported by their button number when the browser delivers them.

Why does right-click not open a menu here?

The test panel blocks the browser menu so a right-click can be recorded as a test event. Right-click anywhere outside the panel to open the menu as usual.

Does this work with a trackpad?

Yes. Trackpad taps and clicks appear as mouse events, and two-finger scrolling appears as wheel events with a direction.

Does the test record anything about my mouse?

No. Everything happens in this page and nothing is sent anywhere. Reloading the page clears the counters and the trail.
