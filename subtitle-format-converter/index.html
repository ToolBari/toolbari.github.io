Subtitle Format Converter

Paste subtitle text or upload a subtitle file to convert it between SRT and WebVTT. The tool reports how many cues it converted and lists any lines it could not read, and the result downloads as a file.

    The converter reads cue numbers, timestamps and text lines; it does not keep WebVTT styling, positioning or cue settings, which SRT has no way to hold. Always spot-check the converted file in a player before sharing it.

    SRT and WebVTT differences

    SRT is the older format. Each cue has a number, then a timestamp line, then the text, with a blank line between cues. Its timestamps use a comma before the milliseconds, such as 00:00:01,000. WebVTT is the format browsers use for video on the web. It starts with a WEBVTT header line, its timestamps use a full stop before the milliseconds, and cue numbers are optional.

    Both formats carry the same words and timings, so conversion is mostly a matter of the header, the numbering and the comma or full stop. WebVTT can also carry styling and cue positions, which SRT cannot hold; those details are dropped during conversion, and the words and their timings are kept.

    How conversion works

    Paste subtitle text into the box or upload a file, then press Convert. With auto-detect, a file starting with WEBVTT is treated as WebVTT and converted to SRT, and anything else is treated as SRT and converted to WebVTT. You can also pick the direction yourself if the automatic guess is wrong.

    Each cue is checked as it is read. A cue with a missing or broken timestamp is skipped, and its block and line numbers are listed so you can fix them. Cues that are fine are still converted, so one bad cue does not stop the whole file. The converted text appears in the result box and downloads as a file with the right extension.

    Questions people ask

    How does the converter know which format my file is?

    It looks at the text: a file starting with WEBVTT is treated as WebVTT, and a file with numbered cues and comma timestamps is treated as SRT. You can also pick the direction yourself if the automatic guess is wrong.

    What happens to lines the converter cannot read?

    Cues with a missing or broken timestamp are skipped, and their line numbers are listed so you can fix them. Cues that are fine are still converted, so one bad cue does not stop the whole file.

    Are styling and positioning kept?

    No. WebVTT can carry styling and cue positions that SRT cannot hold, so those details are dropped during conversion. The words and their timings are kept.

    Is my subtitle file uploaded anywhere?

    No. The file is read and converted entirely in your browser, and the download is made on your device. Nothing is sent to any server.
