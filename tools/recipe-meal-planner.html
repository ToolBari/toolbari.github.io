What this planner does

Cooking every day is easy when you know what you are making, and hard when you have to decide at the last minute. This planner takes the daily decision away. You pick a recipe for breakfast, lunch and dinner for each of the seven days, and the planner turns those choices into a weekly plan and one shopping list that covers the whole week. Everything happens in your browser, on your phone or your computer, and nothing you choose is sent anywhere. Your plan is saved on your own device, so you can close the page and come back to it later without losing anything.

The planner is built around simple home cooking. The recipes use ordinary ingredients that you can find in any grocery shop, and none of them needs special equipment or a long list of steps. Most take under half an hour, and the few that take longer mostly look after themselves while they cook. Each recipe lists its ingredients with sensible quantities and its steps in plain order, so you can follow it even if you are new to cooking.

A meal plan is more than a list of dinners. It is a way to shop once, cook with less waste and stop standing in front of an open fridge wondering what to make.

How to use the planner

Start by working through the seven days, one meal at a time. Each day has three dropdown menus, one for breakfast, one for lunch and one for dinner, and each menu lists the recipes that fit that meal. Pick the recipe you want for each slot, or leave a slot empty if you already have plans for that meal, such as eating out or a family meal at someone else's house. You do not have to fill every slot to use the planner, and you can change any choice later without starting again.

When you pick a recipe, its ingredients and steps appear below the planner so you can check them before you commit. This is useful when you are deciding between two dishes, or when you want to check whether you already have the main ingredients at home.

Once you have chosen your meals, press the Build my week button. The planner shows your full week, day by day, and underneath it a shopping list that combines the ingredients from every recipe you picked. Ingredients that appear in more than one recipe are grouped together, so you can see at a glance that you need an ingredient several times rather than buying it twice by accident. From there you can copy the list to your clipboard and paste it into a note or a message, or print the page and take the list to the shop on paper.

On a phone, the dropdown menus are large and easy to tap, and the plan and the shopping list stack in a single column that fits the screen. On a desktop computer the same page works in exactly the same way, with the same menus and buttons. Your choices are saved automatically as you pick them, so if you close the tab halfway through planning, your week will still be there when you return.

Planning a week in ten minutes

The fastest way to plan a week is to stop trying to make it perfect. Set a timer for ten minutes, sit down with the planner, and fill the days in order. Start with the meals you already know your household enjoys, because a plan built on favourites is a plan you will actually follow. Then add one new dish for the week, something you have not cooked before, and give it a day when you have a little more time and energy.

Repeating two or three favourites across the week is not lazy cooking, it is smart cooking. A soup you like can appear on Monday and again on Thursday, and a stir-fry that works well can be lunch on one day and dinner on another. Repetition makes the shopping list shorter, because the same ingredients serve several meals, and it makes the cooking faster, because you already know the steps.

Think about your week as it really is, not as you wish it were. A busy evening needs a fast meal, so put the ten-minute recipes on the days when you get home late. A quieter day can hold the longer recipes, such as the baked potatoes or the lentil curry, which need more time but little attention. If you know a day will be too busy for cooking at all, leave that meal empty in the planner rather than planning a recipe you will not cook.

Leftovers are part of the plan, not an afterthought. When a recipe serves four and your household is smaller, the extra portions become lunch the next day, which means one cooking session covers two meals. The planner lets you repeat a dish on purpose, so you can plan a leftover lunch right after a big dinner and save yourself a whole round of cooking.

The shopping list and how it saves money

The shopping list is where the planner earns its place. As soon as you build your week, every ingredient from every recipe you picked is gathered into one list, and repeats are combined. If three recipes need onions, the list shows onions once with a note that three recipes use them, so you buy enough in one trip instead of running out halfway through the week or buying a fresh bag every time.

Shopping from a list changes how you shop. Without a list, most people buy what looks good in the shop and forget half of what they actually need, which means extra trips and extra spending on things that were never planned. With a list built from real recipes, you buy what the week needs and nothing more. The list also shows you where one ingredient does several jobs, such as a bag of rice that serves the stir-fry, the fried rice and the curry, so you can buy one larger pack instead of several small ones.

The list helps you waste less food as well. Food waste usually comes from buying ingredients with no plan for using them, and a meal plan is the opposite of that. Every item on the list has a meal waiting for it, and the meals are spread across the week so that fresh ingredients are used before they spoil. If a recipe leaves you with half a bag of something, you can pick another recipe that uses the same ingredient and let the planner add it to the list.

Copy the list to your clipboard and paste it into a notes app or send it to someone else in the household who is doing the shopping. Or print the page and take the list on paper, crossing items off as you go.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is planning seven brand-new recipes in one week. New recipes take longer, need ingredients you may not have, and sometimes do not turn out the way you hoped. One new dish a week is enough to keep cooking interesting, and the rest of the week should be dishes you know and trust. A plan full of experiments is exciting on Sunday and exhausting by Wednesday.

The second mistake is forgetting leftovers. A plan that cooks a fresh meal three times a day, seven days a week, is twenty-one cooking sessions, and almost nobody has the time or energy for that. Plan for leftovers on purpose: cook a larger dinner, save the extra portions, and let them become lunch the next day. The planner makes this easy, because you can pick the same recipe twice and the shopping list will count its ingredients accordingly.

The third mistake is shopping without a list. It feels faster to walk into a shop and buy what you remember, but memory is unreliable, and the result is usually a basket full of extras and a fridge missing the one ingredient dinner needs. Build your week first, take the list with you, and shop from it. If the shop is out of something, pick a substitute that fits the recipe rather than abandoning the plan.

Finally, do not treat the plan as a rule that cannot change. A meal plan is a guide, not a contract. If Tuesday is too busy for the dinner you planned, swap it with Wednesday's meal or move it to the weekend. The planner saves your choices as you go, so changing the plan takes seconds, and a plan that bends is a plan you will keep using.

Questions people ask

Do I need an account to use the meal planner?

No. The planner runs entirely in your browser, and there is no sign-up, no password and nothing to download. Your week is saved on your own device, so you can plan, close the page and come back later without creating an account.

Can I use the planner on my phone?

Yes. The planner is designed to work on a phone, with large dropdown menus and buttons that are easy to tap. The plan and the shopping list stack in a single column, so nothing runs off the side of a small screen.

How does the shopping list work?

When you build your week, the planner gathers the ingredients from every recipe you picked into one list. Ingredients that appear in more than one recipe are shown once, with a note saying how many recipes use them, so you can buy enough in a single trip.

Can I change a meal after I build my week?

Yes. Change any dropdown to a different recipe, or back to no meal planned, and press Build my week again. The plan and the shopping list update at once, and your new choices are saved on your device automatically.

Are the recipes suitable for beginners?

Yes. Every recipe is simple home cooking with ordinary ingredients and steps written in the order you do them. Most take under half an hour, and none needs special equipment, so they are a good place to start if you are new to cooking.

Will my plan still be there tomorrow?

Yes, as long as you use the same browser on the same device. Your plan is saved in your browser storage, so it is still there the next day and the next week. Clearing your browser data or switching to a different device will remove it, so copy or print the list if you want a backup.
