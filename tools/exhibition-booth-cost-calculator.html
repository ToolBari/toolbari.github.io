What this calculator does and who it is for

Exhibiting at a show costs far more than the space alone, and first-time exhibitors are routinely surprised by the total. This calculator adds up the whole cost — space, stand build, power, furniture, staff and travel — so you see the real figure before you book, not after the invoices arrive.

It is built for anyone planning to exhibit: a small business booking its first booth, a maker selling at a craft fair, or a team that exhibits every year and wants a quick budget. You do not need any exhibition experience — just your plan and the prices you have been quoted.

What it will not do is tell you what anything costs. Every figure comes from you: the space price from the organiser, the stand quote from the builder, the travel you have priced. The calculator does the arithmetic; the prices are yours to check.

How to use the calculator on your phone or computer

Start with the booth size in square metres and the space price per square metre from the organiser. Then enter the stand build, power, furniture, staff and travel costs. Staff is entered as a count and a cost per person — food, accommodation and pay for the show — so the calculator can multiply them together. Enter all amounts in your own currency.

Tap Work out the booth cost and the calculator shows the estimated total with a full breakdown, so you can see which part costs the most. The copy button copies the whole breakdown as plain text, ready to paste into a budget or a message to a colleague.

Nothing you enter leaves your browser — there is no account, no upload and no record kept anywhere else. Run the numbers for different booth sizes and compare them: the difference between a 9 square metre booth and an 18 square metre booth is not just the space — it is the stand, the power and the staff too.

The costs explained

Space is the floor you rent, usually priced per square metre. It is the first cost and often the largest single line, but it is rarely the whole cost. The space price usually covers the floor only — the walls, the carpet, the lights and the power are separate, and the organiser's price list says what is included.

Stand build is what turns empty floor into a booth: walls, counters, display units, graphics and lighting. A shell scheme — the organiser's standard walls and carpet — costs less than a custom-built stand, and for a first show it is usually the sensible choice. A custom stand looks better but costs far more, and the money is better spent on the first show's other costs.

Power and services cover electricity, lighting, internet and cleaning. Power is the one people forget: a booth with lights, screens and a kettle needs more power than the basic supply, and extra power is charged separately. Furniture and fittings — tables, chairs, shelves, a lockable counter — are usually hired from the organiser or brought along, and both cost money.

Staff and travel are the human costs. Staff need food, accommodation and pay for the days of the show, and travel covers getting people and goods to the venue. For a show far from home, staff and travel together can cost as much as the space — which is why they belong in the budget from the start, not as an afterthought.

Printed materials are a smaller cost that is easy to miss: leaflets, business cards, price lists and a sign or banner for the booth. None of them is expensive alone, and together they add a noticeable figure — especially if they are printed in a hurry the week before the show. Put them in the furniture and fittings figure, or add them to the other costs, but do not leave them out.

What exhibitors forget

Power is the most-forgotten cost. The space price rarely includes enough power for a booth with lights and screens, and the extra power is charged per kilowatt or per socket. Check what power the space price includes, add up what your booth actually needs, and budget the difference.

Flooring is the second. A bare concrete floor looks unfinished and feels cold underfoot, and the organiser's carpet or flooring is an extra charge. Some exhibitors bring their own flooring; either way, it costs money and it belongs in the budget.

Staff food is the third. Staff who stand all day need food and drink, and buying it at the venue for several people over several days adds up. Budget for it plainly, or arrange it in advance — hungry staff are unhappy staff, and unhappy staff do not sell well. The calculator's staff cost per person is the place to put it.

Insurance is the fourth. Many organisers require public liability insurance for exhibitors, and your own goods and stand need cover while they are at the venue. It is not a large cost, but it is a required one — check what the organiser asks for and add it to the budget rather than discovering it at the last minute.

Small booth versus big booth

A small booth — 9 square metres is a common size — is cheaper in every way: less space, a smaller stand, less power, fewer staff needed. It is the right choice for a first show, a small product range, or a business testing whether exhibitions work for it. A small booth well run beats a big booth half empty.

A big booth costs more than the extra space alone. Doubling the floor doubles the space cost, but it also needs a bigger stand, more furniture, more power and usually more staff. The total can easily be two and a half times the small booth's total, not twice. The calculator shows this plainly when you run both sizes side by side.

The right size depends on what you are showing and how many people you need to staff it. A product that needs demonstrating needs room; a product that sells from a counter does not. Start small, learn what the show is like, and grow the booth when the results justify it — not before.

A worked example with real numbers

Imagine a 9 square metre booth. Space costs 12,000 per square metre, so the space is 108,000. The stand build is 45,000, power is 8,000, furniture is 6,000. Two staff at 10,000 each is 20,000, and travel is 12,000. The estimated total is 108,000 plus 45,000 plus 8,000 plus 6,000 plus 20,000 plus 12,000, which is 199,000.

The space is just over half the total — 108,000 of 199,000. The rest is everything else, and it is the rest that surprises first-time exhibitors. A budget that only counts the space would be wrong by almost half.

Now double the booth to 18 square metres. The space doubles to 216,000, the stand grows to 80,000, power to 14,000, furniture to 10,000, three staff at 10,000 is 30,000, and travel stays at 12,000. The total is 362,000 — not double the small booth, but close to it, and the extra staff and stand costs are the reason. Running both sizes through the calculator before booking is the habit that prevents a surprise.

Common mistakes that blow an exhibition budget

The first mistake is pricing the space but not the stand. The space is the floor; the stand is everything that turns it into a booth, and the stand can cost as much as the space. Always budget both, and get a stand quote before you book the space.

The second mistake is forgetting travel. Getting people and goods to the venue — and back — costs real money, especially for a show far from home. Travel includes the goods as well as the people: shipping a stand and stock is not free, and it is easy to forget until the invoice arrives.

The third mistake is budgeting for the show and forgetting the days around it. Staff arrive the day before to set up and leave the day after to pack down, and those days need food and accommodation too. Budget the whole trip, not just the show days, or the real cost will be higher than the estimate.

Questions people ask

How much does an exhibition booth cost?

It depends on the size, the show and what you include. Enter your own figures in this calculator: space, stand build, power, furniture, staff and travel all add up, and the space alone is usually only about half the total.

What is included in the space price?

Usually the floor only. Walls, carpet, lights, power and furniture are normally separate — check the organiser's price list for exactly what the space price covers before you budget.

Do I need power at my booth?

If your booth has lights, screens, a kettle or anything that plugs in, yes. The basic supply rarely covers a working booth, and extra power is charged separately. Add up what your booth needs and budget for it.

How much is a stand build?

It varies from a shell scheme — the organiser's standard walls and carpet — to a custom-built stand, which costs far more. Get a quote from the stand builder before you book the space, and budget the quote, not a guess.

What do first-time exhibitors forget?

Power, flooring, staff food, and the travel for people and goods. The space price is only the start — the rest of the costs together are often as large as the space itself.

Can I exhibit on a small budget?

Yes. Start with a small booth, use the organiser's shell scheme, bring your own furniture if you can, and staff it with fewer people. A small booth well run beats a big booth half empty.
