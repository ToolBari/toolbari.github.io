What this tool does

A domain name is the address people type to reach a website. It is the part after the web address beginning, the short name that stands for your shop, your blog or your project. A good domain name is easy to remember, easy to say out loud and easy to type without mistakes. A bad one is long, full of strange spellings or so close to another name that people land on the wrong site.

This page helps you find a good one. Type one word that describes what you do, pick a category, and the generator builds more than twenty name ideas from that word. It joins your word with short prefixes and suffixes, the way real site names are built, and shows every idea as a button you can tap to copy. The whole thing runs in your browser, it is free, and you do not need an account or a signup of any kind.

The ideas are starting points, not finished answers. A generated name is only useful if it is still free to register and if it fits what you are building. That is why this page also explains how to check a name with a registrar in about thirty seconds, and why the warn box at the top says plainly what this tool cannot do. Use the generator to make a shortlist, then check the shortlist properly before you settle on a name.

How to use it

Then tap the category box and choose what the site is for: a shop, a blog, a tools site or a personal page. Tap the green Generate button and a list of ideas appears below. Tap any idea to copy it. The button changes to Copied for two seconds so you know it worked, and the name is on your clipboard ready to paste into a registrar search.

On a desktop computer the steps are the same, and you can also press the Enter key after typing your word instead of clicking the button. The same word gives different endings for a shop than for a blog, because a shop name and a blog name do different jobs. A shop name should sound like a place to buy something. A blog name should sound like a place to read something. A tools site name should sound useful and quick. A personal page name should sound like you.

Try a few seed words before you decide. If your first word is very common, the ideas may feel ordinary, so try a second word that is more specific. A baker could try bread, then try oven, then try crumb, and compare the three lists. Keep a shortlist of five or six names you like, then check each one with a registrar. Do not fall in love with the first idea on the list. The first idea is only the first combination the tool tried, not the best name for your site.

What makes a domain name good

Short names win. A name of one or two short words is easier to remember, easier to type on a phone keyboard and easier to say in a conversation. If you have to spell your domain name out loud letter by letter every time you mention it, it is too hard. Say the name out loud before you choose it. If a friend can hear it once and type it correctly, it passes the most important test there is.

Simple spelling wins too. Avoid words that people spell in two different ways, avoid silent letters, and avoid clever spellings that only make sense when you see them written down. A name that looks smart on a business card but confuses everyone who hears it is a bad name. The same goes for numbers. A number in a name forces every listener to guess whether you mean the digit or the word, and most of them will guess wrong.

Hyphens are a similar trap. One hyphen is survivable, but two or three turn a name into a puzzle. People forget the hyphens, or put them in the wrong place, and end up on someone else's site. A name with no hyphens and no numbers is the safest choice, and this generator never puts a hyphen or a number in an idea except for a couple of short endings that are easy to say.

Finally, a good name fits the thing it names. A shop name should hint at what the shop sells. A blog name should hint at what the blog is about. A personal page can simply be a name, because the person is the subject. The category you pick in the generator steers the endings toward that fit, so a shop seed gets shop-like endings and a blog seed gets blog-like endings.

The idea patterns this generator uses

Every idea on this page is built from a small set of patterns, and knowing the patterns helps you judge the results. The first pattern is a prefix: a short word in front of your seed, like get, try, my or go. Prefixes are useful when your seed word on its own is already taken, which is true for most common words. Adding get or try in front keeps the name short and keeps the meaning clear.

The second pattern is a suffix: a short word after your seed. The suffix depends on the category you picked. A shop gets endings like shop, store, mart and bazaar. A blog gets endings like blog, writes, notes and stories. A tools site gets endings like tools, lab, kit and hub. A personal page gets endings like me, life, world and space. The suffix tells a visitor what kind of place they are going to before they even open the page.

The third pattern is a combination: a prefix and a suffix together, or a short extra ending like now, today or plus. These give you longer names that are still easy to say, and they are useful when the short combinations are already taken. The generator removes duplicates, so every idea in the list is different, and every idea contains your seed word in lowercase with no spaces.

None of the patterns use a trademark, a famous brand name or a word that belongs to someone else. The ideas are built only from your seed word and from plain English words. That is on purpose. A name that borrows from a famous brand can cause trouble later, and a name built from your own words is yours to use.

How to check whether a name is available

A name idea is only the beginning. Before you print it, paint it or tell anyone about it, you must check whether someone already owns it. That check takes about thirty seconds on a registrar website. A registrar is a company that sells domain names. You type the name into its search box, and it tells you whether the name is free or already taken. If it is taken, the registrar usually shows similar names that are free, which can give you more ideas.

Three registrars you can use for the check are Namecheap, Porkbun and GoDaddy. All three let you search without an account, and all three show you the result in plain words. You do not need to buy anything to run the search. Pick one, paste the name you copied from this page, and read the result.

If the name is taken, do not give up on the idea at once. Try the same name with a different ending, or try a small change to the word, or go back to the generator and try a different seed. If the name is free, you can buy it from the registrar there and then, but there is no rush. A name that is free today will usually still be free tomorrow, so take the time to say it out loud, show it to a friend and sleep on it before you pay for it.

One warning: if a name you want is taken and someone offers to sell it to you for a large amount, walk away. That is how the domain resale market works, and a new site does not need an expensive name. A fresh name that you like and that is free to register is a far better start than a costly name bought from a stranger.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is picking a name nobody can spell. A clever spelling looks good on a screen and fails everywhere else: on the phone, on the radio, in a conversation. If you have to spell it out every time, choose a simpler name. The second mistake is hyphens and numbers everywhere. A name like my-best-shop-24 is not a name, it is a password, and nobody will type it correctly from memory.

The third mistake is copying a famous brand. A name that is one letter away from a well-known shop or app is not clever, it is a problem waiting to happen. The brand owner can object, and your visitors will be confused about which site is yours. Build your name from your own words and your own idea, not from someone else's reputation.

The fourth mistake is choosing a name before checking it. People fall in love with a name, print it on cards, tell their friends, and only then discover that someone else already owns it. Check first, celebrate after. The fifth mistake is picking a name that is too narrow. A shop called only-coffee cannot easily sell tea later. If you might grow, pick a name with room in it.

What it cannot do

This tool generates name IDEAS. It does not check whether a name is available — check that with a registrar. It cannot tell you whether a name is already a trademark, and it cannot tell you whether a name is free in every country. It does not register names, sell names or hold a name for you. If you find a name you like, the next step is yours: check it with a registrar and register it there if it is free.

The generator also cannot judge taste. It builds combinations from patterns, and some of them will sound odd. That is normal. A list of twenty ideas is meant to give you five good ones, not twenty perfect ones. Read the list with your own judgment, keep what sounds right, and ignore the rest. The final choice is always yours, and it should be a name you are happy to say out loud for years.
