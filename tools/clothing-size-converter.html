Clothing Size Converter

Convert clothing sizes between US, UK, EU and Asia systems, with inches and centimetres. Pick a category, pick your size in one system, and see the matching size in every other system from the full tables on this page.

Clothing sizes vary by brand and by country — these tables are the standard published conversions, not a promise that a size will fit. Where it matters, compare your own measurements in inches or centimetres with the brand's own size chart before you buy.

How to use this converter

Pick a category (women's tops and dresses, women's bottoms, men's tops or men's bottoms), pick the system your size is in (US, UK, EU or Asia), then pick your size from the list. The converter shows the matching size in every other system, plus the body measurements in inches and centimetres for that row of the table. The full tables are printed below, so you can check the whole range at a glance without converting one size at a time.

The converter works from the standard published conversion tables — the same tables the clothing industry uses. It does not guess: it looks up your size in the table for your category and reads across the row. If your size sits between two rows, pick the nearer one and check the measurements: the inches and centimetres are the more reliable guide, because a size number means slightly different things to different brands.

A note on Asia sizes, before you use them: Asian sizing runs smaller than US sizing and varies more by brand and by country. The Asia column in these tables is the typical published mapping, and it is the least exact column in the converter. If you are buying from an Asian brand, compare your measurements in centimetres with that brand's own size chart — it is the only way to be sure.

Why sizes vary by brand

The tables on this page are the standard published conversions, but no brand is forced to follow them, and many do not. The most famous reason is vanity sizing: some brands cut their sizes generously so that customers feel good about fitting into a smaller number. A size 10 in one brand can be a size 12 in another, and both labels are honest in the sense that neither is regulated — clothing sizes are a convention, not a standard.

The second reason is the target customer. A brand that sells to tall people cuts longer; a brand that sells to older people cuts roomier; a brand that sells fast fashion cuts smaller, because its customers are younger and slimmer on average. The same size number means different bodies in different shops, which is why trying clothes on — or checking the measurements — beats trusting the number.

The third reason is country. Asian sizing runs smaller than US sizing for the same letter, because the average body it is cut for is smaller. European sizing is more consistent than most, because it is tied to body measurements in centimetres. And UK sizing, while close to US sizing in numbers, is cut for a different average body. The converter gives you the standard mapping; the brand gives you the reality. When they disagree, trust the measurements.

How to measure yourself

The measurements in these tables are body measurements, taken with a soft tape measure over light clothing, standing straight and breathing normally. For the bust or chest, measure around the fullest part, keeping the tape level and snug but not tight. For the waist, measure at the narrowest point, usually just above the navel — not where your trousers sit, which is lower. For the hips, measure around the fullest part, usually about 20 centimetres below the waist.

Measure twice and take the average, and have someone help you if you can: measuring your own back with a tape is hard to do level. Write the numbers down in inches and centimetres, and keep them with you when you shop — they are more useful than any size number, because they are yours and they do not change from brand to brand.

If you are between sizes, the usual advice is to go up for fitted clothes (a slightly loose fit can be taken in, a tight one cannot be let out) and to check the fabric: a garment with stretch forgives a tight fit, and a garment without stretch does not. And if your bust and waist fall on different rows, buy for the larger measurement — it is easier to take in a garment than to let it out.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is trusting the size number instead of the measurement. A size 10 in one brand is not a size 10 in another, and a size that fitted you five years ago may not fit you now — not because your body changed, but because the brand changed its cutting. The measurements in these tables are the stable part; the numbers are the moving part.

The second mistake is measuring the garment instead of the body. A pair of trousers labelled 32 inches is cut larger than 32 inches, because it has to fit over a 32 inch waist with room to move and sit. If you measure the trousers and compare with the table, you will think you need a bigger size than you do. Measure your body, not your clothes.

The third mistake is forgetting that Asia sizes run small. A US medium ordering an Asian medium will usually find it tight — the typical mapping is a full size smaller, so a US medium is an Asian large. The converter shows this, but it is the mistake people make most often when ordering from Asian brands, because the letters look familiar and the bodies they fit are not.

What this converter cannot do

This converter cannot promise a fit. It converts between the standard published size systems, but brands cut differently, bodies differ in shape as well as size, and a table cannot know your shoulders, your torso length or how you like clothes to fit. Two people with the same bust measurement can need different sizes if one is tall and one is short, or one is broad-shouldered and one is narrow.

It also cannot cover every system. Children's sizes, shoe sizes, bra sizes and maternity sizes all have their own systems, and this converter covers adult tops, bottoms and dresses only. And it cannot keep up with brands that change their cutting: the tables are the standard conversions, and a brand that cuts generously or tightly will differ from them. Use the converter to find your likely size, then check the brand's own chart and, where you can, try the garment on.

Questions people ask

Why do I wear a different size in every shop?

Because clothing sizes are a convention, not a regulated standard, and brands cut differently. Some brands cut generously so customers fit into a smaller number, some cut for taller or older customers, and some cut for a younger market. The measurements in inches and centimetres are the stable part — the size number is the moving part.

Are US and UK sizes the same?

No. For women's sizes, UK numbers run 4 higher than US: a US 8 is a UK 12. For men's tops the letters are the same in both systems, and for men's bottoms both use the waist in inches. The tables on this page show the full mapping for each category.

Why are Asian sizes smaller?

Because they are cut for a smaller average body. The typical mapping is about one size smaller than US: a US medium is an Asian large, and a US large is an Asian extra-large. Asian sizing also varies more by brand and by country than US or EU sizing, so check the brand's own chart in centimetres before you buy.

Should I go by the size or the measurements?

By the measurements. A size number means slightly different things to different brands, but your bust, waist and hips in inches or centimetres are yours and do not change from shop to shop. Measure yourself, compare with the tables on this page, and check the brand's own size chart where it matters.

How do I measure my waist properly?

Measure at the narrowest point, usually just above the navel, with a soft tape over light clothing, standing straight and breathing normally. Do not measure where your trousers sit — that is lower and wider. Measure twice and take the average, and have someone help you keep the tape level.

What if I am between sizes?

Go up for fitted clothes: a slightly loose fit can be taken in, but a tight one cannot be let out. Check the fabric too — a garment with stretch forgives a tight fit, and one without stretch does not. If your bust and waist fall on different rows, buy for the larger measurement.
