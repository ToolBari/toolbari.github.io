What this vault does and who it is for

Most households hold several insurance policies at once: home, vehicle, health, life, and travel cover, each from a different insurer, each with its own renewal date and its own contact number. Small businesses add more — public liability, professional cover, equipment cover — until the dates blur together. The papers live in different drawers, the renewal notices arrive by post or message at different times, and the one date that matters is the one nobody remembers. A policy that lapses quietly is the risk this vault exists to remove: cover that ends without anyone noticing, discovered only when a claim is needed.

This vault is a single checklist for all of it. Each policy gets one row: its name, its type, the insurer, the policy number, the renewal date, and the contact number you would need in a hurry. The vault sorts the list so the nearest renewal comes first and flags everything due in the next sixty days. It is built for anyone with more than one policy — a family keeping home and car cover together, a small business owner with several policies, or someone helping an older relative keep track of theirs.

It is important to be clear about what the vault is not. It is not cloud storage and it does not hold your documents: nothing is uploaded anywhere, and no copy of any policy lives here. It is a checklist that tells you what you have, when each policy renews, and who to call. The originals stay with you, safe, and the vault makes sure you never have to hunt for a date or a number when it matters.

How to use the vault, step by step

Start by gathering your policies — the paper copies, the renewal notices, or the confirmation messages — and add them one at a time. Give each policy a clear name, such as Home cover or Delivery van, so you can tell it apart at a glance. Pick the type from the list, add the insurer's name, the policy number, and the renewal date exactly as it appears on the policy. The contact number should be the number you would call to make a claim or ask about a renewal, not a general sales line.

Once a policy is added it appears in the list, sorted by renewal date, with the number of days left beside it. Policies due within sixty days also appear in the renewing soon section, so the urgent ones are impossible to miss. When you renew a policy, update its renewal date on its row: the policy moves back down the list and the soon section clears. When a policy ends or is cancelled, delete its row so the list stays true.

The copy button turns the whole vault into plain text you can paste into a note, a message, or a document. Copy it regularly — the vault lives in this browser only, so a copy is your backup. On a phone the whole flow works by touch: type, tap, read. Add a policy the day it starts, while the dates are in front of you, because that is the moment the details are easiest to record and easiest to forget.

What the output means

The main list shows every policy with the days left until its renewal date. A positive number means the policy is still in force for that many days; a policy showing zero days renews today; a negative number means the renewal date has passed, and the policy needs attention at once — either it was renewed and the date here needs updating, or it has lapsed and needs sorting out with the insurer.

The renewing soon section lists everything due within sixty days, nearest first. Sixty days is the window that gives you time to act: time to read the renewal notice, to compare cover, to ask the insurer a question, and to renew without rushing. A policy that appears here is not a problem — it is a prompt, and the prompt is the whole point of the vault.

The summary line counts your policies and the number renewing soon. The contact number on each row is the number to call when something happens: a claim, a question about cover, or a renewal you want to discuss. Keep it current — an old number in a moment of stress is worse than no number at all, because it wastes the time you need. Nothing on this page is a price, a quote, or a recommendation: the vault never says whether a policy is good value, only when it renews and who to call.

A worked example

Imagine a family with three policies: home cover that renews on 15 March, car cover that renews on 2 June, and health cover that renews on 1 September. It is early February, and the renewal notice for the home policy has just arrived. They open the vault and add all three policies, each with its insurer, policy number, renewal date, and the claims number printed on the policy.

The vault sorts the list with home cover first — about five weeks away — and puts it in the renewing soon section. That is the prompt to read the renewal notice properly: is the cover the same as last year, has anything changed, and is the renewal still the right choice? They decide to renew, pay, and update the renewal date in the vault to next year's date. The home policy moves to the bottom of the list, and the car policy becomes the next one due.

Two months later the car policy appears in the renewing soon section, and the same routine runs: read the notice, decide, renew, update the date. By September the health policy gets its turn. Nothing was forgotten, nothing lapsed, and nobody had to search a drawer for a policy number — because every date and number was already in one list, checked once a month in five minutes.

Tips for a good result

Name each policy so its purpose is obvious: Home cover, Delivery van, Travel cover for the family trip. A vague name like Policy 1 forces you to open the paperwork to know what it is, and that defeats the purpose of the list. If two policies are similar, add a word that separates them — the car model, the property, the person covered.

Put the claims number in the contact field, not the sales number. The number that matters is the one you need at the worst moment, and it is usually printed on the policy itself or on the renewal notice. When a policy renews, update the date the same day; a vault with last year's dates is worse than no vault, because it looks complete while being wrong.

Review the vault once a month, on the same day — the first of the month, or the day you pay the rent. Read the renewing soon section, act on anything inside sixty days, and copy the list so you have a backup. Keep the original documents somewhere safe and dry, and keep a note of where they are. If a renewal notice arrives and the date or the insurer does not match the vault, trust the notice and fix the vault: the policy is the truth, and the vault is only a copy of it.

Limits of the vault — what it cannot do

The vault is a checklist, not storage. It does not hold copies of your policies, it does not upload anything, and it cannot show you a policy you have not added. Keep the originals safe — the vault records that a policy exists and when it renews, and nothing more. If you lose a policy document, the vault can tell you the insurer and the policy number, but only the insurer can replace the document.

The vault does not send reminders. It cannot notify you when a renewal approaches, because it lives in this browser and does nothing while the page is closed. The monthly review is the reminder: open the page, read the renewing soon section, and act. It also does not renew anything for you — renewals happen with the insurer, on their terms, and the vault only records the new date once you have renewed.

The vault does not check whether a policy is good value, whether the cover is enough, or whether a cheaper option exists. It never quotes a premium and never recommends a policy. And if this browser's data is cleared, the list is gone — copy it regularly and keep the copy somewhere you can reach it. Used within these limits, the vault does one job well: it makes sure no renewal date is ever a surprise.

Questions people ask

What insurance documents should I keep together?

Keep each policy's main document, its renewal notice and the schedule that lists what is covered, plus the contact number for claims. Keep them by policy, not by insurer, so one policy's papers are always in one place — and note in the vault where the originals are kept.

How do I track renewal dates for several policies?

Add each policy to the vault with its renewal date. The list sorts itself by date, and everything due within sixty days appears in the renewing soon section, so the nearest dates are always at the top.

Is my information stored online?

No. Everything stays in this browser on this device, and nothing is uploaded or sent anywhere. Copy the list regularly so the record survives a cleared browser or a new device.

What should I keep with each policy?

The policy number, the insurer's name, the renewal date and the claims contact number. The vault records all four, and the original document should be kept somewhere safe alongside a note of where it is.

Can I print the vault checklist?

Yes. Tap the copy button to copy the whole vault as plain text, paste it into any document or note, and print it from there. A printed copy is a useful backup to keep with the original documents.

What should I do if I lose a policy document?

Contact the insurer using the number in the vault and ask for a replacement copy. Give them the policy number from the vault. The vault cannot replace a document, but it keeps the details you need to ask for one.
