How the generator works

The generator holds 372 real baby names, each with its meaning, origin and gender. You set four things: boy or girl (or both), a starting letter, a style, and an optional meaning keyword. The generator then shows the first ten matching names, in alphabetical order, with the total number of matches underneath. Every name has a Copy button and a Save button. Copy puts the name and its meaning on your clipboard; Save keeps it in a favourites list on your own device.

Start broad and narrow down. If you set boy, any letter and any style, you will see the first ten of 195 boy names. That is a lot to choose from, so add a style to cut the list to a size you can think about. Classic gives you names that have been used for centuries. Modern gives you names that feel current. Unique gives you names few other children will share. Nature gives you names from trees, flowers, animals and the sky. Vintage gives you names that were popular a hundred years ago and are coming back.

The meaning keyword is the most useful filter of all, and the one most people miss. Type a word like light, lion or star, and the generator shows only names whose meaning contains that word. It is a good way to find a name with a meaning you love, even if you have never heard the name before. Leave the keyword blank if you want to browse by sound and style instead.

One more tip: use the generator together. Two people sitting with one list will find common ground faster than two people with two separate lists. Each of you saves the names you like, and the overlap between your two favourites lists is your shortlist. If you are doing this on your own, the same trick works with a friend or a parent — anyone whose taste you trust. A name that two people both love is a stronger choice than a name only one person loves.

What the five styles mean

Classic names have been in steady use for a very long time. James, Elizabeth, William and Anna are classic: everyone knows them, everyone can spell them, and they never sound dated. A classic name is the safest choice, and for many families that is exactly the point. The risk is only that the name is common — but as the hub page explains, even the most popular name today belongs to roughly one baby in a hundred.

Modern names feel current. Some are genuinely new, and some are old names that have come back with a new sound. Harper, Luna, Mason and Aria are modern: short, bright, and easy to say. Modern names suit parents who want a name that sounds like today rather than yesterday.

Unique names are the ones few other children will share. Astrid, Atticus, Wren and Caspian-style names fall here — names that stand out in a class list. A unique name is a gift if the child likes standing out, and a burden if they do not, so think about the child as well as the name.

Nature names come from the world outside: trees, flowers, animals, the sea and the sky. Willow, Rowan, Aurora and River are nature names. They tend to sound gentle and open, and they pair well with almost any surname.

Vintage names were popular a hundred years ago and are coming back now. Eleanor, Hazel, Theodore and Felix are vintage: names your great-grandparents would recognise, worn by babies again. Vintage names feel both old and fresh, which is why so many of them are climbing the lists.

Styles overlap, and that is part of the fun. A name can be both nature and vintage, like Hazel, or both classic and unique, like Atticus. The generator tags each name with every style it fits, so a name appears under each of its styles. If you are torn between two styles, try them one at a time and compare the lists — the names that appear in both are often the ones that feel most like yours. And if none of the five styles feels right, leave the style filter blank and browse by letter or meaning instead. The style is a guide, not a rule.

How to narrow a long list

A list of eighty names is not a shortlist — it is a phone book. The way to narrow it is to cut in rounds. First, cross out every name you would not actually use. Be honest: a name you like in theory but would never say out loud is not a real candidate. That round alone usually cuts the list by half.

Second, say the survivors out loud with the surname. Some names that look good on a screen sound wrong spoken, and some names that look plain come alive when you hear them. Say each one three times, as if you were calling the child in for dinner. The names that still feel good after the third try are your real shortlist.

Third, live with the shortlist for a few days. Write the names on a piece of paper and leave it on the kitchen table. The name you keep coming back to is usually the right one. If two names keep tying, ask yourself which one you would rather hear every day for the next twenty years. That question settles most ties.

Finally, agree on a veto rule before you start. Each of you gets to veto any name, no questions asked, no argument needed. A veto is not a rejection of the other person's taste — it is a quick way to clear the names that one of you cannot live with. With a veto rule in place, the shortlist that survives is one you both genuinely like, and neither of you has to give in on a name you dislike. Most couples find that the veto rule turns a stressful decision into a short one.

Meanings: what to check before you decide

Meanings deserve a last check before you decide, because a name's meaning is the one thing about it that never changes. A name can rise and fall in fashion, but its meaning stays the same. If the meaning matters to you, pick a name whose meaning you would be happy to explain to the child one day.

Be careful with meanings you find on random websites. Many name sites copy each other, and errors spread. The meanings on this page come from standard name dictionaries, and where a meaning is genuinely uncertain the page says so instead of inventing one. If a meaning looks surprising, check it against a second dictionary before you believe it.

Also check the meaning in any other language your family speaks. A name can mean one thing in English and something quite different in another language, and a child who grows up bilingual will hear both. A quick check now saves an awkward discovery later. And remember that a name's meaning is a story, not a destiny: a child named Grace is not obliged to be graceful, and a child named Felix is not guaranteed good luck.
