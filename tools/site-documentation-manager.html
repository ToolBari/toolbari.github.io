What this organizer does and who it is for

A construction project produces paper from the first day: permits, drawings, photos, certificates, approvals, and the revised versions of all of them. Lose track of which drawing is the latest, or discover at handover that a certificate is missing, and the project stalls while everyone searches. This organizer keeps the list straight: every document recorded with its type, its date and its status, in one searchable place.

It is built for anyone running a build — a homeowner managing their own project, a small contractor, or a site manager who wants to know what is missing before it matters. You do not need any special system to use it. You only need to record each document when it arrives, and mark its status honestly.

What it will not do is hold your files. This page stores only the names and details you type — never the drawings, permits or photos themselves. Keep the files on your own device or in your own folders, and use this list as the index that tells you what you have, what is pending, and what is missing.

How to use the organizer on your phone or computer

Add one document at a time. Type the name — Building permit, Ground floor plan, Electrical certificate — then pick the type, the date and the status. Received means you have it. Pending means you are waiting for it. Expired means its date has passed. Missing means you know you need it and do not have it.

The list shows everything you have recorded, with the status as a coloured label. The search box filters by name as you type, and the type filter shows only one kind of document at a time. The flags at the top tell you which required document types — permit, drawing, certificate — have no received document recorded yet.

Your list is saved only in this browser, so it is still here when you come back. Nothing is uploaded or shared. The copy button copies the whole list as plain text, which you can paste into a message or keep with your project papers. Each row has a delete button, and the clear button removes everything.

The documents every build needs

Every build needs a permit or approval of some kind — the paper that says the work is allowed. Without it, the work can be stopped, fined or ordered to be undone. Record the permit the day it arrives, note its expiry date if it has one, and check that it covers the work you are actually doing.

Drawings are the second essential: the plans the builders work from. A build usually has several — site plan, floor plans, elevations, structural drawings — and each one may be revised more than once. Record every version with its date, and always know which version is the latest, because building from an old drawing is one of the most expensive mistakes a project can make.

Certificates come at the end of each stage: electrical certificates, plumbing certificates, completion certificates. They prove the work was done properly and inspected, and you will need them for handover, for insurance and for selling the property later. Photos are the fourth kind: progress photos, photos of hidden work before it is covered, and photos of problems. They prove what happened when nobody remembers.

Guarantees and manuals arrive with the equipment: the boiler, the windows, the kitchen appliances, anything with a warranty. They are easy to lose because they arrive in boxes and get filed nowhere, but they are the papers you need when something breaks. Record them like any other document, with the date and the length of the guarantee, so you know what is covered and for how long.

Keeping drawings and permits straight

The golden rule is one list, one date, one status. Every drawing on the list has the date it was issued, and the latest date wins. When a revised drawing arrives, add it as a new entry with the new date — do not delete the old one, because the old version is the record of what was built before the change.

Permits need the same care. A permit has a number, a date and often an expiry, and the work it covers must match the work being done. If the design changes after the permit is issued, check whether the permit still covers it before building — a changed design can need a changed permit.

Name your entries the way you would look for them later. Ground floor plan, revision 3, 12 March is findable; Plan is not. The search box only helps if the names are clear, so write names that a stranger could understand — because the person searching this list in six months may be you, with no memory of what you meant.

Share the list with the people who need it. The builder, the architect and the owner should all be working from the same list of documents, or one of them will be working from an old drawing. Copy the list and send it whenever a new version arrives — a shared list is the cheapest insurance a project can have.

The handover file

When a build finishes, the owner should receive a handover file: the complete set of documents for the finished building. It usually holds the permits, the final as-built drawings, the certificates for each stage, the guarantees for equipment, and the manuals for anything installed. A build without a handover file is a build whose paperwork is unfinished.

This organizer helps you build the handover file as you go, instead of hunting for papers at the end. Every document you record is one item already in the file. The missing-document flags tell you what is still needed, so nothing is discovered missing on the day of handover.

At handover, copy the list and check it against the actual files one by one. The list says what should be there; the files are what is actually there. Only when the two match is the handover file complete. Keep a copy of the list with the files — it is the index that makes the file usable.

A worked example: a small house extension

Imagine a small house extension. The list starts with the building permit, received on 3 February, and the site plan and ground floor plan, both received the same week. The structural drawing arrives later, on 20 February, and is recorded with its date. Two progress photos are recorded in March, and the electrical certificate arrives in May.

In April a revised ground floor plan arrives — revision 2, dated 8 April. It is added as a new entry, and the old revision stays on the list with its earlier date. Anyone checking the list can see at a glance that revision 2 is the latest, and the builder works from revision 2, not revision 1.

By handover, the list holds the permit, three drawings, two photos and one certificate, with nothing flagged as missing. The owner receives the files with the copied list as the index. Nothing was hunted for at the last minute, because every document was recorded the day it arrived.

Common mistakes that lose documents

The first mistake is a permit on a phone photo and nowhere else. A photo of a permit is not the permit, and a phone that is lost, broken or replaced takes the photo with it. Keep the original file or paper somewhere safe, and record it in the list the day it arrives.

The second mistake is no record of which drawing is the latest. When two versions of a drawing circulate and nobody knows which one is current, the builder may work from the wrong one. Date every version, keep the old ones, and always check the date before building from a drawing.

The third mistake is treating the list as the file. This organizer records names and details — it does not hold your documents. If the files themselves are scattered across phones, emails and paper folders, the list cannot save them. Keep the files in one place, keep the list up to date, and check the two against each other before handover.

Questions people ask

What documents does a build need?

A permit or approval, drawings for each stage, certificates for electrical and plumbing work, and photos of progress and hidden work. The exact set depends on the job, but every build needs at least a permit, a drawing and a certificate of some kind.

Does this store my actual files?

No. This page stores only the names and details you type — the document name, type, date and status. Your actual files stay wherever you keep them. Use this list as the index that tells you what you have and what is missing.

How do I know which drawing is latest?

Every drawing on the list has the date it was issued, and the latest date wins. Add revised drawings as new entries with their new dates, and keep the old versions as a record of what was built before the change.

What is a handover file?

The complete set of documents for a finished building: permits, final as-built drawings, certificates, guarantees and manuals. It is handed to the owner when the build finishes, and this organizer helps you build it as you go.

Can I track expiry dates?

Yes — record the date a document expires in the date field and mark its status as Expired when it passes. The tracker counts expired documents in its summary, so you can see at a glance what needs renewing.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. Your list is saved only in this browser using local storage. Nothing is uploaded, synced or shared, and clearing your browser data will remove it — so copy the list if you want a lasting record.
