What this tracker does and who it is for

Most small workplaces run on training that nobody tracks properly. Someone did first aid two years ago — or was it three? The fire safety refresher was due last month, or next month, and the certificate is in a folder nobody has opened since. The training happened, the knowledge is there, but the dates are scattered across certificates, emails, and memory. When a client asks, an inspector visits, or an incident happens, the question is always the same: who is trained, and can you prove it?

This tracker answers the first half of that question. Add each staff member and each training they have done, with the completion date and the refresher interval, and the tracker works out when the refresher is due. It sorts the list so the most urgent comes first, flags everything overdue, and shows what is due within thirty days. The whole team fits on one page, and the list copies out as plain text.

It is built for small teams — a workshop, a care home, a restaurant, a small office — where one person keeps the training list and everyone else relies on it. It tracks dates only: it does not certify anyone, it does not check certificates, and it does not prove compliance. What it does is make sure no refresher date is ever a surprise, which is the part of compliance that most often goes wrong.

How to use the tracker, step by step

Start by gathering the training records — certificates, course confirmations, or the notes from the last review — and add them one at a time. Type the staff member's name and the training name, enter the date the training was completed, and choose the refresher interval: how often the training must be repeated. If a course is one-off with no refresher, choose that option and the tracker will list it without a due date.

Once a record is added it appears in the list, sorted by due date, with its status beside it: current, due soon, or overdue. When someone completes a refresher, delete the old record and add a new one with the new completion date — the due date moves forward by the interval, and the record stays true. When someone leaves, delete their records so the list shows only the people who work here now.

One record per person per training is the rule: if Ayesha has done both first aid and fire safety, she gets two records, each with its own completion date and interval. That way each training is tracked on its own cycle, and a refresher due for one course never hides behind a current record for another. The list is sorted by due date, so the most urgent record — whoever it belongs to — is always at the top.

Read the overdue section every time you open the page. Anything there needs booking now, not next month. Use the copy button to take the whole list as plain text for your records, and copy it regularly — the tracker lives in this browser only, so a copy is your backup. On a phone the whole flow works by touch: type, tap, read.

What the output means

Each record shows the staff member, the training, the completion date, and the next refresher date, worked out by adding the interval to the completion date. A twelve-month refresher completed on 10 March is due on 10 March the following year; a six-month refresher completed on 5 June is due on 5 December. The status chip tells you where each record stands: current means the refresher is more than thirty days away, due soon means it falls within thirty days, and overdue means the date has passed.

The overdue section lists only the records that need action now, nearest due date first. An overdue record does not mean the person is untrained — it means the refresher is late, and the gap should be closed as soon as possible. Book the course, and when it is done, replace the record with the new completion date so the tracker resets.

The summary line counts the records, the overdue items, and the items due within thirty days. Those three numbers are the whole picture: if overdue is zero and due soon is small, the team is in good shape. If overdue is growing, the tracker is telling you that training has slipped — and the fix is booking courses, not fixing the tracker.

A worked example

Imagine a small restaurant with eight staff. The owner gathers the certificates and adds the records: three people did food hygiene in January with a twelve-month refresher, two did first aid last March with a thirty-six-month refresher, and the whole team did fire safety in September with a twelve-month refresher. It is now February, a year later.

The tracker sorts the list and the picture is clear at once. The food hygiene refreshers are due this month and show as due soon. The first aid refreshers are still current, with more than a year left. The fire safety refreshers are due in September. But one record stands out: a new starter who did food hygiene fourteen months ago and whose refresher is two months overdue — the certificate was filed and forgotten.

The owner books the overdue refresher and the three due-soon refreshers in one session, updates the records with the new completion dates, and copies the list for the file. The whole review took fifteen minutes, and the gap that would have been found by an inspector was found by the tracker instead. That is the job: not paperwork for its own sake, but finding the gap while there is still time to close it.

Tips for a good result

Add training the day it is completed, while the certificate is in your hand. The completion date is the one fact the whole tracker depends on, and it is easiest to record correctly on the day. If you are not sure of a date, check the certificate — a guessed date makes the due date a guess too, and a guessed due date is worse than no date at all.

Set the refresher interval from the course provider or the legal requirement, not from habit. Different courses repeat on different cycles, and the interval that is right for first aid may be wrong for food hygiene. When the provider's guidance and the law disagree, follow the shorter interval — it is the safer one — and note the reason where the team can see it.

Review the tracker once a month, on the same day you review other lists. Read the overdue section first, then the due-soon items, and book what needs booking. Copy the list after each review and keep it with the business records. And keep the certificates themselves safe: the tracker records the dates, but the certificate is the proof, and only the original proves anything.

Limits of the tracker — what it cannot do

This tracker records dates; it does not certify training and it does not prove compliance. A record on this page is not evidence that training happened — the certificate is the evidence, and the tracker only notes the date on it. If a regulator, a client, or an insurer asks for proof, show them the certificate, not this list.

It does not know what training your workplace is legally required to provide. Requirements differ by country, by industry, and by role, and they change. Check local legal requirements for your business, check what your insurer expects, and check what your clients require. The tracker cannot tell you that a training is missing — it can only track the training you have told it about.

It also does not send reminders, book courses, or check that a refresher actually happened. It lives in this browser and does nothing while the page is closed, and if the browser data is cleared the list is gone — copy it regularly. Used within these limits, the tracker does one job well: it keeps the dates straight so that training never slips by accident.

Questions people ask

What training should a small workplace track?

Track the training your work depends on: safety training, first aid, fire safety, food hygiene where it applies, and any course a client or insurer requires. If a certificate has an expiry or refresher date, it belongs in the tracker.

How do I know when refresher training is due?

Add the completion date and the refresher interval, and the tracker works out the due date. Anything due within thirty days shows as due soon, and anything past its date shows as overdue.

Can I track training for a whole team?

Yes. Add one record per person per training, and the list sorts itself by due date. The overdue section shows the whole team's gaps in one place.

Does this certify that someone is trained?

No. This tracker records dates only. It does not certify anyone and does not prove compliance — the certificate from the training provider is the proof.

Can I print the compliance list?

Yes. Tap the copy button to copy the whole list as plain text, paste it into any document or note, and print it from there.

What training is legally required in my country?

That depends on your country, industry and role, and the answer changes. Check local legal requirements, your insurer's terms, and any client requirements — this page cannot state another country's rules.
