What gratuity is

Gratuity is a lump sum an employer pays when an employee leaves after long service — on retirement, resignation, or death. It is a thank-you written into law: the Payment of Gratuity Act makes it a legal right for employees of covered establishments, not a favour the employer can choose to give or withhold. It is paid once, at the end of the employment, and it is meant to cushion the years that follow.

The right to gratuity begins after five years of continuous service with the same employer. Leave before five years and there is no gratuity — that is the single most important rule, and the one that catches people who resign at four and a half years. The one exception is death or disablement in service, where gratuity is payable even before five years, because the service ended through no fault of the employee.

Gratuity is also tax-free up to 20 lakh. The amount above 20 lakh is taxable, but it is still paid to you — the 20 lakh figure is a tax-exemption ceiling, not a cap on the gratuity itself. Someone whose formula gives 21,63,462 receives the full amount, and pays tax only on the 1,63,462 above the ceiling. This calculator shows both parts so there is no confusion.

The formula, step by step

The standard formula is simple: 15 days of wages for every completed year of service. In numbers, that is 15 divided by 26, multiplied by your last drawn monthly salary, multiplied by the number of years counted. The 26 is the number of working days in a month — a month has 26 working days after the four Sundays are taken out — so 15/26 of a month's salary is the pay for 15 days.

The years counted are not always the years you served. A year counts in full if you served more than six months of it. So 8 years and 4 months counts as 8 years, but 5 years and 8 months counts as 6 years — those two extra months past the half-year mark are worth a full year's gratuity. The tool applies this rule automatically and shows the counted years in its output, so you can see exactly what happened.

One more detail: the salary used is the last drawn salary — the basic plus DA in your final month, not an average over your career. A promotion or a pay rise in your last year raises your gratuity, because the whole calculation runs on the final figure. That is also why the gratuity figure is worth checking before you resign: leaving a month after a pay rise can be worth more than leaving a month before it.

Worked example: 8 years of service

Take a last drawn salary of 60,000 a month and 8 years and 4 months of service. The 4 months are under six, so 8 years count. The formula gives 15/26 × 60,000 × 8 = 2,76,923. The whole amount is tax-free, because it is under the 20 lakh ceiling:

Taxable portion: ₹0

Now take the same idea with a shorter service and a smaller salary: 40,000 a month and 5 years and 8 months. The 8 months are over six, so 6 years count — one more than the completed years — and the formula gives 15/26 × 40,000 × 6 = 1,38,462:

Last drawn salary (basic + DA): ₹40,000 per month
Service entered: 5 years 8 months
Service counted for gratuity: 6 years
Gratuity payable: ₹1,38,462
Tax-free portion (up to ₹20,00,000): ₹1,38,462
Taxable portion: ₹0

The rounding rule matters here: without it, the gratuity would have been 1,15,385 for 5 years. The two months past the half-year mark are worth 23,077. If you are close to the half-year mark, it is worth knowing exactly where you stand before you decide when to leave.

Worked example: 25 years and the tax ceiling

Take a last drawn salary of 1,50,000 a month and 25 years of service. The formula gives 15/26 × 1,50,000 × 25 = 21,63,462. That is above the 20 lakh tax-exemption ceiling, so the split matters:

Last drawn salary (basic + DA): ₹1,50,000 per month
Service entered: 25 years 0 months
Service counted for gratuity: 25 years
Gratuity payable: ₹21,63,462
Tax-free portion (up to ₹20,00,000): ₹20,00,000
Taxable portion: ₹1,63,462

You receive the full 21,63,462. The first 20,00,000 is tax-free; the remaining 1,63,462 is added to your taxable income for the year. The ceiling is about tax, not about payment — nobody loses gratuity because the formula gives more than 20 lakh.

Long service is where gratuity becomes serious money. At 25 years, the gratuity is roughly a year's salary — 25 years × 15 days is 375 days, just over a year's pay. That is the point of the scheme: a long career ends with a meaningful sum, not a token.

When gratuity is not payable

Gratuity is not payable before five years of continuous service, except on death or disablement. If you resign at 3 years and 2 months, there is no gratuity — and the calculator says so plainly, while showing what the formula would give at five years with the same salary, so you can see what is at stake:

Last drawn salary (basic + DA): ₹50,000 per month
Service entered: 3 years 2 months
Service counted for gratuity: 3 years
Gratuity is not payable yet — it is payable after 5 years of continuous service with the same employer.
At 5 years with the same salary, the formula gives: ₹1,44,231

The five-year rule has a sharp edge: someone who resigns at 4 years and 11 months gets nothing, while someone who stays one more month gets the full five-year amount. If you are thinking of leaving and you are close to five years, the gratuity is worth putting in the calculation — it may be worth more than a month or two of waiting.

Gratuity can also be forfeited, in full or in part, if an employee is dismissed for misconduct that caused damage or loss to the employer, or for an offence involving moral turpitude. That is a matter for the employer's disciplinary process and the law, not for this calculator — but it is worth knowing that the right to gratuity is strong, not absolute.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is entering the gross salary instead of basic plus DA. The gross figure includes HRA and other allowances that the formula does not use, so it overstates the gratuity — sometimes by a lot. Check your salary slip, find the basic and the DA, and enter only those.

The second mistake is counting service with a previous employer. Gratuity is paid by each employer for the service with that employer. If you served 6 years at one company and 4 at the next, you get gratuity from the first and nothing from the second — you cannot add the two together.

The third mistake is ignoring the six-month rounding rule. Someone with 8 years and 7 months who counts 8 years is short-changing themselves by a full year's gratuity. The tool applies the rule, but it is worth knowing it exists, because it affects the timing of a resignation.

The fourth mistake is treating the 20 lakh ceiling as a cap on the payment. It is a tax-exemption ceiling only. A gratuity above 20 lakh is still paid in full; only the part above 20 lakh is taxed. And the fifth mistake is leaving before five years without realising what it costs — check the figure before you resign, not after.

Questions people ask

How is gratuity calculated?

The standard formula is 15/26 × last drawn monthly salary (basic + DA) × years of service counted. A year counts in full if you served more than six months of it. Enter your figures in the calculator above and it does the maths.

How many years do I need for gratuity?

Five years of continuous service with the same employer. Leave before five years and no gratuity is payable — except on death or disablement in service, where it is payable even before five years.

Is gratuity taxable?

Gratuity is tax-free up to 20 lakh. The amount above 20 lakh is added to your taxable income for the year. The 20 lakh figure is a tax-exemption ceiling, not a cap — you still receive the full gratuity.

What salary is used for the gratuity calculation?

Your last drawn monthly salary — basic pay plus dearness allowance only. HRA, special allowances, bonuses and the gross figure on your salary slip are not included in the formula.

Does service with a previous employer count?

No. Gratuity is paid by each employer for the service with that employer. You cannot add service from different employers together — each employer's gratuity is calculated separately.

What if I resign just before completing 5 years?

You get no gratuity. The five-year rule is strict: resign at 4 years and 11 months and there is nothing, stay one more month and the full five-year amount is payable. Check the figure before you decide when to leave.
