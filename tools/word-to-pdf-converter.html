How to use the tool

This tool turns text you type or paste into a clean, printable document, and then saves it as a PDF using your browser. It is made for the everyday jobs that need a tidy PDF in a hurry: a letter, a notice, a set of notes, a homework page, a simple agreement, or a page of instructions. You do not need a Word file, a word processor, or any software at all. You type the words, the tool shapes them into a document, and your browser does the saving.

Using it takes four steps. First, type a title for your document in the title box. The title appears at the top of the document in large, bold text. Second, type or paste your text into the big text box. You can write it directly, or paste text from anywhere, such as a message, a note, or another document. Third, choose a font size. Normal suits most documents, small fits more words on a page, and large makes a notice or a sign easy to read at a distance. Fourth, tap Show my document to see the preview, check that it looks right, and then tap Save as PDF. Your browser opens its print screen, and you choose Save as PDF as the destination.

The text box understands three simple marks. A line that starts with a hash and a space becomes a heading, which is useful for breaking a longer document into sections. Words wrapped in two stars become bold, which is useful for names, dates, prices in words, and anything the reader must not miss. A blank line between blocks of text starts a new paragraph, which keeps the document readable instead of running everything together. You do not need to learn anything else. Write naturally, leave blank lines between paragraphs, and the tool does the rest.

How print-to-PDF works

Print-to-PDF is a feature built into every modern browser. When you print a web page, your browser offers Save as PDF as one of the printer choices, and instead of sending the page to a printer, it saves it as a PDF file on your device. This tool uses that feature, and it is honest about it: the Save as PDF button simply opens your browser's print screen, and the browser makes the PDF. There is no server involved, no upload, and no hidden step. The PDF you get is a real PDF file, which you can email, share, upload to a form, or print later.

On a computer, the steps are the same in every browser. Tap Save as PDF, and the print screen appears. In the destination or printer list, choose Save as PDF. Check the preview on the right side of the screen, which shows exactly what the PDF will look like. If the document is longer than one page, the preview shows the page breaks, so you can see where one page ends and the next begins. Then save the file and give it a name you will remember. On a phone, the steps are nearly the same. Tap Save as PDF, and your phone shows its print screen. Choose Save as PDF as the printer, or share the printout to a PDF app, depending on your phone. The wording differs between Android and iPhone, but the idea is the same: the phone turns the document into a PDF and saves it in your files or downloads.

One thing worth knowing is that only the document preview is printed. The rest of this page, the buttons, the boxes and the guide text, stays out of the PDF, because the page is styled so that only the preview prints. That means the PDF you save is a clean document with just your title and your text, which is what you want when you send it to someone else. If the print preview ever shows more than the document, check that you tapped Show my document first, so the preview is built before you print.

Converting a real Word file: two free methods

This tool works with text you type or paste. It does not open a Word file, because reading a Word file needs a word processor, and a web page cannot do that job honestly. If you already have a document saved as a Word file and you want it as a PDF, there are two free methods, and both are quick.

The first method is to use a word processor on your computer. Open the Word file in Microsoft Word, if you have it, or in the free word processor LibreOffice, which opens Word files without any trouble. Once the file is open, use the Export or Save As command and choose PDF as the format. The word processor turns the whole document, with its headings, tables and images, into a PDF that looks the same as the original. This is the best method when the document has tables, pictures or careful formatting, because the word processor keeps all of it. It is also the safest method for private documents, because the file never leaves your computer.

The second method is to use Google Docs in your browser. Upload the Word file to Google Drive, open it with Google Docs, and then download it as a PDF from the File menu. This method needs no software installed, and it works on any computer or phone with a browser. It is a good choice when you are away from your own computer, or when you already keep your files in Google Drive. It does involve uploading the file to a service, so it is not the right choice for private papers. For ordinary documents, though, it is quick and reliable, and the PDF it makes keeps the headings and the layout of the original.

If your Word file is on your phone, both methods still work. A phone can open the file in a word processor app and export it as a PDF, or upload it to Google Drive and download the PDF from there. Many phones also offer a print-to-PDF option from inside the word processor app, which does the same job. Whichever method you choose, open the finished PDF once and check it before you send it, especially the tables and the last page.

Tips for a clean, readable PDF

A few habits make the difference between a tidy PDF and a messy one. First, keep paragraphs short. A paragraph of three or four lines is easy to read on screen and on paper, while a wall of text puts readers off. Leave a blank line between paragraphs, and the document breathes. Second, use headings for longer documents. A heading every few paragraphs tells the reader what is coming, and it makes the document easy to scan. Third, use bold sparingly. Bold words draw the eye, so bold the names, the dates and the key points, and leave the rest plain. If everything is bold, nothing stands out.

Fourth, choose the font size for the reader, not for yourself. Normal suits letters, notes and homework. Large suits notices, signs and anything read at a distance. Small suits a long document you want to fit on fewer pages, but do not go small just to save paper if the reader will struggle to read it. Fifth, always check the preview before you save. The preview shows exactly what the PDF will contain, including where the headings fall and where the page breaks land. If a heading sits alone at the bottom of a page, add a blank line or move a paragraph so it sits with its text. A one-minute check in the preview saves a second PDF and a second email.

What it can do is turn plain text into a clean, honest PDF in a few minutes, with no signup, no upload and no software, and for a letter, a notice, a set of notes or a simple page, that is all you need.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is pasting text without checking the layout. Pasted text often carries extra blank lines, odd spacing, or lines that run together, and the preview shows these problems clearly. Always tap Show my document and read the preview before you save, and fix the spacing in the text box if the preview looks wrong. The second mistake is choosing a tiny font to fit everything on one page. A cramped document is hard to read, and a second page is not a failure. Choose the size that suits the reader, and let the document take the pages it needs. The third mistake is forgetting the title. A document without a title looks unfinished, and the reader has to guess what it is. A short title takes a few seconds and makes the PDF look complete.

Another common mistake is saving the PDF without looking at the print preview. The print screen shows exactly what will be saved, including the page breaks, and a quick glance catches a heading alone at the bottom of a page or a paragraph split in an awkward place. People also forget where the PDF was saved, because different browsers and phones save downloads in different places. After saving, open the file once from your downloads or files app to confirm it is really there, and give it a clear name, such as the title of the document, so you can find it later. Finally, remember that this tool does not save your text. If you close the page, the text is gone, so save the PDF, or copy the text somewhere safe, before you leave.
