Working Days Calculator — Count Business Days Between Two Dates

Count the working days between two dates, without doing the calendar maths by hand. Pick the start and end dates, choose whether weekends count, add your holidays, and the calculator gives the total plus a short week-by-week summary.

Holidays are the ones you enter — the tool cannot know your company's holiday list, so add every holiday yourself.

How to use the working days calculator

Pick the start date and the end date, choose whether weekends count, and add any holidays one per line in YYYY-MM-DD form — for example, 2026-01-15. Press Calculate and the tool shows the total working days, the total calendar days, how many weekend days and holidays were taken out, and a short summary of working days by week. The Copy button copies the whole result as plain text.

Both the start date and the end date count if they are working days. A range from Monday to Friday is five working days, not four — the start day is a day of work too. If you only want the days between two dates without counting one of the ends, move that end by a day before you calculate.

The holiday list is yours to fill in. The tool has no built-in holiday calendar, because holidays differ by country, state, company and religion, and any built-in list would be wrong for most people. Type each holiday on its own line, in the form YYYY-MM-DD, and the tool skips it — but only if it falls inside your range and on a day that would otherwise count. A holiday that falls on a Saturday is already excluded by the weekend rule, so it is not counted twice.

The week-by-week summary groups the working days by the Monday of each week, so you can see at a glance which weeks are full and which are short. For very long ranges the summary shows the first twelve weeks and tells you how many more there are.

What counts as a working day

A working day is a day you would normally work. For most office jobs that means Monday to Friday, excluding public holidays. For shops, hospitals, factories and many other workplaces, the working week is different — Saturday may be a working day, or the week may run on shifts. This calculator lets you choose: exclude weekends for a Monday-to-Friday week, or count every day and take out only the holidays you list.

The definition matters because the same two dates can give very different answers. Three calendar weeks contain 21 days, but only 15 working days on a Monday-to-Friday week — and fewer if a holiday falls inside. A deadline quoted in working days is always longer in calendar days than it sounds, and that gap is where most scheduling mistakes begin.

Half days are not part of this calculator. If your workplace has half days — a Saturday morning, say, or the day before a festival — count them as whole days here and adjust the result yourself, or list them as holidays and add a half day back in your head. The tool keeps to whole days because that is how most leave and deadline rules are written.

One more thing to settle before you calculate: whose holidays? Your company's list, your client's list, or the public list for your state? A project deadline depends on the days your team actually works; a leave request depends on your employer's calendar; a delivery promise depends on the courier's working days. Pick the list that matches the question you are asking, and enter those dates.

Notice periods

Working days matter most when the count has consequences, and notice periods are the classic case. A one-month notice period and a 30-working-day notice period are not the same thing: 30 working days is six full weeks, while a calendar month is four and a bit. If your contract says 30 working days, count them properly — this calculator does it in seconds — and do not assume the last day is a month after the first.

The same applies to probation periods, payment terms and delivery promises that are written in working days. "Payment within 10 working days" from a Monday means the money is due two Fridays later, not the following Thursday. "Delivery in 5 working days" ordered on a Friday means the next Friday at the earliest. Whenever a number of working days is promised or owed, convert it to a date with a calculator, not by guessing.

One caution: the end date of a notice period is usually the last working day, and the first day of the notice is usually the day after you give notice, not the day itself. Different contracts word this differently, so check yours. This calculator counts both ends as working days, which is the right default for most counts — but if your contract counts differently, adjust the start date by a day.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is counting the start day twice — once as the end of the previous period and once as the start of the new one. A range from Monday to Friday is five days. If you count Monday as day zero and Friday as day five, you get six, which is wrong. This calculator counts each calendar day once, inclusive of both ends, which is the standard way.

The second mistake is forgetting holidays. A three-week project with two holidays in it is not 15 working days but 13, and a leave plan that ignores a holiday wastes a leave day. Enter every holiday in the range, every time — the tool cannot know them for you.

The third mistake is assuming weekends are always excluded. If your team works Saturdays, or if the deadline is a delivery promise from a courier that works seven days a week, excluding weekends understates the working days. Choose the weekend setting that matches the people whose days you are counting, not the setting you used last time.

The fourth mistake is counting in calendar days when the rule is written in working days, or the other way round. "Two weeks" and "ten working days" sound similar, but they are different promises. Read the wording, then calculate in the units the wording uses.

Questions people ask

Does the calculator count the start and end dates?

Yes — both the start date and the end date count if they are working days. A range from Monday to Friday is five working days. If you want to exclude one end, move that date by a day before calculating.

How do I add holidays?

Type each holiday on its own line in the holiday box, in YYYY-MM-DD form — for example, 2026-01-15. The tool skips holidays that fall inside your range on days that would otherwise count. A holiday on a weekend is not counted twice.

What is the difference between working days and calendar days?

Calendar days are every day on the calendar, including weekends and holidays. Working days are the days you would normally work — usually Monday to Friday, excluding holidays. Three calendar weeks are 21 days but only 15 working days.

Can I count Saturdays as working days?

Yes. Choose No on the weekends question and the tool counts every day, taking out only the holidays you list. If you work alternate Saturdays, list the non-working Saturdays as holidays instead.

How are the working days grouped in the summary?

The summary groups working days by the week starting each Monday, so you can see which weeks are full and which are short. For very long ranges it shows the first twelve weeks and tells you how many more there are.

Can I use this for a notice period?

Yes — enter your first and last days and the calculator counts the working days between them. Check your contract first: some count the day notice is given, others start the next day. Adjust the start date by a day if your contract counts differently.
