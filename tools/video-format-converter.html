How to use this guide

A video format is the container a video file lives in, and the container decides where the video will play. MP4 plays almost everywhere, WebM is built for the web, and MOV is the format Apple devices prefer. Most of the time, the problem is not the video itself but the container it is in: the video is fine, and the player or the app simply does not accept that container.

The formats explained in plain words

MP4 is the format to use when you are not sure. It plays on Android phones, iPhones, Windows computers, Macs, smart TVs, and inside WhatsApp, YouTube, Facebook and most other apps. The files are a sensible size, the quality is good, and almost every video tool can open an MP4 file. If a friend sends you a video that will not play, converting it to MP4 fixes the problem in most cases.

WebM is an open format built for the web. Websites use it because it gives good quality at a small file size, which means faster loading pages and less data for the visitor. Most modern browsers play WebM without extra software. The weakness is Apple: older iPhones and some Apple apps do not play WebM, so it is a poor choice for sharing with people who use Apple devices.

MOV is Apple's own format, and it is the format an iPhone records in when it is set to the highest quality. MOV files keep excellent quality, which is why video editors like them, but the files are often large, and many Android phones and Windows programs struggle with them. If you edit video on a Mac, MOV is natural. If you share video with everyone, MP4 is safer.

One more idea helps everything else make sense: a format is a container, and the codec is the method used to squeeze the video inside it. MP4 usually holds video squeezed with a codec called H.264, which is why it plays everywhere. A file can be an MP4 and still refuse to play if it uses a codec the player does not know.

Method 1: Use a free online converter

An online converter is the fastest way to convert a video when you only need to do it once or twice. You open the converter in your browser, choose your video file, pick the format you want, and download the converted file. There is nothing to install, and it works on any computer or phone with a browser. Most free converters handle the common conversions, such as MOV to MP4 or MP4 to WebM, in a few minutes.

  1. Open a free online converter in your browser and find the video converter tool.
  2. Choose your video file from your device, or drag it onto the page on a computer.
  3. Pick the output format, such as MP4, and the quality setting if the converter offers one.
  4. Start the conversion, wait for it to finish, and download the converted file.
  5. Play the downloaded file all the way through before you delete the original.

Online converters are best for ordinary videos: a clip for a friend, a school project, a video for a website, or anything that is not private. Their free plans have limits, such as a maximum file size or a number of conversions per day. A large video also takes a while to upload on a slow connection, so use Wi-Fi for big files. For private videos, use the desktop method below instead, because the file never leaves your computer.

Method 2: Use a free desktop app

If you convert videos often, or if the video is private, a free desktop app is the better method. The file stays on your own computer, there is no upload wait, there is no daily limit, and you get far more control over the quality and the file size. The best-known free choice is HandBrake, a free and open-source video converter for Windows, Mac and Linux.

  1. Install HandBrake from its official website and open it.
  2. Choose your video file, or drag it into the HandBrake window.
  3. Pick a preset, such as Fast 1080p, or choose MP4 as the format.
  4. Press Start, wait for the conversion to finish, and find the new file beside the original.

HandBrake looks technical at first, but the presets do the hard work for you. A preset is a ready-made set of settings, and the ones named for web or for phones cover most jobs. You only need the advanced settings if you are chasing a particular file size or quality level. The conversion takes a few minutes for a short clip and longer for a long video. Because everything happens on your own machine, this is the method to choose for private videos and for batches of files.

Method 3: Convert on your phone

Converting on a phone is straightforward, and you do not need a computer for it. The simplest way is to open a free online converter in your phone browser and follow the same steps as Method 1. The converter pages are designed for touch, and they let you choose a video from your phone storage, convert it, and download the result. This works on both Android and iPhone, and it is the quickest route for a single file.

If you convert videos on your phone often, a dedicated converter app is more comfortable than a browser. Free converter apps for Android and iPhone convert between the common formats on the phone itself, and some of them also trim a video or change its size at the same time. Check the app's reviews and its permissions before you install it, and prefer an app that converts on the phone rather than uploading your video to a server. One practical tip: convert on Wi-Fi rather than mobile data when the file is large, because uploading a big video on a slow connection takes a long time and can fail halfway through.

What conversion loses

Converting a video almost always loses a little quality, because the video is squeezed again into the new format. A single conversion at a sensible quality setting loses so little that most people cannot see the difference. The damage comes from converting again and again: each round squeezes the video a little more, and after three or four rounds the picture looks soft, the colours wash out, and blocky patches appear in fast-moving scenes. Always convert from the original file, never from a file that has already been converted.

Subtitles and extra audio tracks are the other things conversion can lose. Some converters drop subtitles silently, and some keep only the first audio track when a video has two. If your video has subtitles you need, check that the converter keeps them, or keep the subtitle file separate and add it again afterwards. Sound quality can also drop on a low audio setting, so if the voices matter, choose a quality preset rather than the smallest file size.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is converting the same video several times. Each conversion loses a little quality, so converting an MP4 to WebM, then to MOV, then back to MP4 leaves a video that looks noticeably worse than the original. Convert once, from the original file, and keep the original safe. The second mistake is trusting a shady converter site with a private video. Free converters are convenient, but your file travels to their server, and for private recordings, a desktop app that works offline is the safer choice.

Another common mistake is choosing a format for the wrong reason, such as picking MOV because it sounds professional when the video is meant for WhatsApp. The format should follow the destination, not the reputation. People also forget to check the converted file before deleting the original, and then discover too late that the sound is missing or the picture is cut off. Play the whole converted file once, check the sound, and only then delete the original.

Questions people ask

Which format should I use for WhatsApp?

MP4. WhatsApp plays MP4 files directly, and the files are a sensible size for sending over a phone connection. If your video is in MOV or another format, convert it to MP4 first, and keep the file size modest so it sends quickly.

Will converting make my video smaller?

Sometimes. Converting to the same quality keeps the size about the same, while converting to a lower quality setting or a more efficient format makes the file smaller. WebM is often smaller than MP4 for web use, but the biggest factor is the quality setting you choose, not the format alone.

Can I convert MOV to MP4 on my phone?

Yes. Open a free online converter in your phone browser, choose the MOV file, pick MP4 as the output, and download the result. A free converter app on your phone can do the same job without a browser. Convert on Wi-Fi if the file is large.

Why won't my converted video play?

The player probably does not support the codec inside the file, even if it accepts the container. Try a different player, or convert again with a common codec such as H.264 in an MP4 container. If the file is very small or stops partway through, the conversion may have failed, so convert again from the original.

Is it safe to upload my video to a converter?

For ordinary videos, such as a clip for friends or a school project, a well-known free converter is fine. For private videos, such as family recordings, use a desktop app on your own computer instead, so the file never leaves your device.

What is the difference between MP4 and WebM?

MP4 is the universal format: it plays on almost every phone, computer and app. WebM is an open format built for the web: it gives good quality at a smaller file size, but some Apple devices and apps do not play it. Use MP4 for sharing and WebM for a website.
