How to use the tool

Capturing an idea takes seconds: type it, give it a tag, and tap capture. The date is optional — add it when the idea belongs to a day, leave it blank when it does not. That is the whole job at the moment of inspiration: do not stop to organise, polish or judge the idea. The point of a capture tool is to be faster than forgetting, and this one is.

Later, when you have time, come back and find what you saved. Type a word in the search box to search your ideas, or tap a tag button to see everything with that tag. Tap Open on an idea to read it in full, with its tag and date. Delete ideas that no longer matter, and copy the list to keep a backup.

This tool captures your own ideas — it does not invent new ones. An idea generator makes up ideas for you; this tool catches the ones you already had, before they disappear. That is a different job, and it is the job this page does. If you want a machine to suggest ideas, you want a different tool.

Everything stays in this browser only, on this device. There is no account, no server, and no sharing — your ideas are yours alone. Copy the list with the copy button to keep a backup somewhere safe, because clearing your browser data removes your ideas.

The 10-second capture habit

An idea you do not capture within seconds is an idea you will probably lose. A thought that is not written down fades fast, and the feeling of "I will remember it later" is one of the least reliable feelings a mind produces. The 10-second rule is simple — if an idea matters, get it into the tool within ten seconds of having it.

Capture the raw thought, not the polished version. "Herb garden by the kitchen window" is enough; you do not need the full plan, the budget and the plant list at the moment of capture. The raw thought is the seed, and seeds are small. Polishing can wait for the review; the job right now is to not lose the seed.

Keep the tool within reach. On your phone, bookmark this page on your home screen, so capturing an idea is one tap away. The easier capture is, the more you will do it — and the more you capture, the more you have to work with later. A capture tool that takes five taps to open is a tool you will not open.

Do not judge ideas at capture time. The idea that sounds silly in the moment may be the best one you have all week, and the idea that sounds brilliant may turn out to be nothing. Judging is the review's job. Capture's job is to catch everything and let the review sort it out.

Make the habit small enough to keep. Ten seconds, one idea, one tag — that is all a capture needs to be. A habit that takes ten seconds is a habit you will keep on a busy day, and busy days are exactly when the best ideas arrive, because your mind is working on something else.

Tagging so you can find it later

A tag is how you find an idea again. When you tag an idea "garden", you are making a promise to your future self: when I want my garden ideas, I will look under "garden". The tag is not a description of the idea — it is the word you will search for when you want it back.

Use few tags, and use them every time. Five or six broad tags — garden, work, home, learning, cooking, travel — will cover most of a life, and a short list of tags you always use beats a long list of tags you half-remember. Every idea should have a tag; an idea with no tag is an idea you can only find by scrolling, which means an idea you will not find.

Tag with the word you would search for, not the word that describes the idea best. If you would search for "garden" when you want that herb idea, tag it "garden" — even if "herbs" is more precise. The tag serves the search, and the search serves you. Precision is the enemy here; consistency is the friend.

Let your tags grow from use. Start with the obvious ones, and add a new tag only when you find yourself capturing ideas that do not fit any tag you have. A tag list that grows slowly, from real ideas, stays useful. A tag list planned in advance, with twenty neat categories, is a filing system nobody will use.

Resist the urge to tag an idea five ways. One tag per idea is the rule that works — it forces you to decide what the idea is really about, and it keeps the tag buttons readable. An idea with five tags is an idea filed everywhere, which is the same as filed nowhere.

Reviewing ideas weekly

Capture without review is hoarding. A list of two hundred unreviewed ideas is not a treasure chest, it is a junk drawer — and like a junk drawer, it gets harder to open the fuller it gets. The review is what turns capture into something useful, and the review needs a habit, not a mood.

Once a week is the rhythm that works for most people. Pick a regular time — Sunday morning, Friday afternoon, whenever your week has a quiet half hour — and go through your ideas one by one. For each one, ask three questions: does it still matter, what is the next step, and is the tag still right.

Most ideas will not survive the review, and that is fine. An idea that seemed exciting on Tuesday and dull on Sunday was probably never an idea at all — it was a mood wearing an idea's clothes. Delete it without guilt. The review's job is not to keep everything; it is to find the few ideas worth acting on.

The review is also where connections appear. Two ideas captured days apart may turn out to be the same project in disguise, or two halves of one plan. You will only see that when you look at your ideas together, which is exactly what the review does. Capture collects the pieces; review sees the picture.

End every review with one action. Pick the single idea that matters most, and do the smallest next step on it — send the message, buy the seed packet, write the first paragraph. A review that ends with an action is a review that paid for itself; a review that ends with a longer list is just more hoarding.

What this tool cannot do

This tool cannot think for you. It captures ideas, tags them and lists them, but it cannot tell you which idea matters, which one to act on, or which one to drop. Those are judgement calls, and judgement is the part of thinking that no tool can do for you — and should not try to.

It cannot invent ideas. A generator makes up new ideas; this tool catches yours. If you are short of ideas, this page will not help — it can only keep the ones you already have. That is deliberate: the tool respects your thinking instead of replacing it.

It cannot sync between your phone and your computer. An idea captured on your phone stays on your phone, and one captured on your computer stays there. If you capture on two devices, you have two lists, and this page will not pretend otherwise. Use the copy button to bring a list from one device to the other.

It cannot remind you to review. There are no notifications, no emails and no nudges — this page has no account and no server, so it cannot reach you when you are not looking at it. The review habit is yours to build, and the best reminder is a regular time in your week, not an app.

It cannot keep your ideas safe from a cleared browser. Everything lives in this browser's storage, and clearing your browser data removes it. Copy your list regularly, and paste it somewhere safe.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is saving an idea with no tag. An untagged idea is an idea you can only find by scrolling through everything, and scrolling through everything is a job nobody does. Tag every idea, even if the tag is only "misc" — a rough tag beats no tag, every time.

The second is 200 ideas and never one review. Capture feels productive, and it is — but only when the review turns capture into action. Two hundred unreviewed ideas are not two hundred opportunities; they are two hundred things you have not decided about. Review weekly, or stop capturing until you do.

The third is polishing at capture time. If you write a paragraph for every idea, you will capture fewer ideas — and the ones you do capture will be the ones you had time to polish, not the ones that mattered most. Capture the seed. Polish it in the review.

The fourth is too many tags. Twenty tags for thirty ideas is not organisation, it is scattering — each tag holds one or two ideas, and you will never remember which tag you used. Few tags, used every time, beat many tags used once.

The fifth is keeping everything. An idea that no longer matters is clutter, and clutter makes the ideas that do matter harder to find. Delete freely. If an idea was worth keeping, it will come back — and if it does not come back, it was not worth keeping.

The sixth is treating the list as the work. A list of ideas is not a list of achievements — it is a list of possibilities, and possibilities become real only when you act on them. The review should end with one action, however small, or the list is just a museum of things you once thought.

Ideas captured here stay in this browser only — they do not sync, they do not back up, and nobody else can see them. A captured idea is not a done idea: the tool keeps your thoughts safe, but only a regular review turns them into anything.

Questions people ask

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. Your ideas stay in this browser only, on your own device, and this page does not connect to any server. If you clear your browser data, your ideas will be removed, so use the copy button to keep a backup.

How is this different from an idea generator?

An idea generator invents new ideas for you. This tool does the opposite: it captures the ideas you already had, before they disappear, and helps you organise and review them. It never makes anything up — every idea in your list is one you wrote yourself.

How many ideas can I save?

There is no fixed limit — your browser's storage is the only constraint, and for an ordinary idea list you will not reach it. Hundreds of ideas are comfortable; the challenge is reviewing them, not storing them.

How do I find an old idea?

Type a word in the search box to search your ideas, or tap a tag button to see everything with that tag. That is why every idea should have a tag: a tagged idea is one search away, and an untagged one is lost in the list.

What tags should I use?

Few, broad tags that match the words you would search for — garden, work, home, learning, cooking. Use the same tags every time, one tag per idea, and add a new tag only when your ideas genuinely do not fit any tag you have.

Will my ideas survive if I clear my browser data?

No. Your ideas live in this browser's storage, and clearing your browser data removes them. Copy your list regularly with the copy button, and paste it somewhere safe, so a cleared browser never costs you your ideas.
