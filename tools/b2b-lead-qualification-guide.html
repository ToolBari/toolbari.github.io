What this tool does and who it is for

Every business collects leads: names from a trade show, enquiries from the website, introductions from a client. The list grows, and the question is always the same — which ones are worth chasing? Chasing all of them wastes the week; chasing the wrong ones wastes the month. Qualification is the discipline of sorting the list before you start, and this tool does the sorting.

Score each lead on four questions: budget, need, timing, and decision-maker. Each question scores from zero to three, and the total out of twelve tells you where the lead stands. Ten to twelve is hot — follow up first. Seven to nine is warm — follow up this week. Four to six is lukewarm — keep in touch. Zero to three is cold — low priority. The ranked list shows the best leads at the top, so the day's first call is always the right one.

It is built for small businesses and anyone who sells to other businesses without a sales team — a founder doing their own sales, a freelancer choosing which enquiries to pursue, a small firm with more leads than time. The scores are your own judgement, recorded honestly and added up fairly. No lead data is collected or stored online, and no contact details are asked for: the tool scores companies, not people.

How to use the tool, step by step

Start with the company name — just the name, nothing else. The tool does not ask for contact details, phone numbers, or names of individuals, because it does not need them: qualification is about the company and the opportunity, not about the person you spoke to.

Then answer the four questions honestly. Budget: can they afford what you sell, and is the money confirmed? Need: do they need it, and how badly? Timing: when do they want it — now, soon, or someday? Decision-maker: are you talking to the person who can say yes, or to someone who has to ask? Each answer scores zero to three, and the tool adds them up. Be honest: a generous score helps nobody, because it puts a weak lead at the top of the list and pushes a strong one down.

Tap Score this lead and the lead joins the ranked list, sorted by score with the highest first. Work the list from the top: the hot leads first, then the warm ones. When a lead's situation changes — the budget is confirmed, the timing moves up — delete the old score and re-score it with the new facts. Copy the list regularly: the leads live in this browser only, and the copy is your backup.

What the output means

Each lead shows its score out of twelve and its band. A score of ten or more means the lead is strong on most of the four questions: the money is there, the need is real, the timing is soon, and you are talking to the decider. That is a lead to follow up first, today, before anything else. A score of seven to nine is a solid lead with a gap — perhaps the budget needs approval, or the timing is a few months out — and it deserves a follow-up this week.

Four to six means the lead has something going for it but is missing too much to chase hard. Keep in touch — a short message, a useful note, a check-in next month — and re-score it when something changes. Zero to three means the lead is not worth chasing now: no budget, no clear need, no timeline, or no access to the decider. It stays on the list in case the facts change, but it does not earn your time today.

The summary line counts the leads and the hot ones. That count is the shape of your week: if there are three hot leads, those are Monday's calls; if there are none, the week's job is to find better leads, not to chase cold ones harder. The ranked list is the plan, and the plan is only as good as the scores — which is why honest scoring matters more than anything else on this page.

A worked example

Imagine a small software firm with three leads this week. The first is a retailer whose budget is confirmed, whose need is urgent, who wants the work within weeks, and whose owner is the person on the call: budget 3, need 3, timing 3, decision-maker 3 — a score of 12, hot. The second is a manufacturer with a clear need and a likely budget, but the decision sits with a committee and the timeline is a few months out: budget 2, need 2, timing 2, decision-maker 1 — a score of 7, warm. The third is a startup that likes the idea but has no budget and no timeline: budget 0, need 1, timing 0, decision-maker 2 — a score of 3, cold.

The ranked list puts the retailer first, the manufacturer second, and the startup last. The firm calls the retailer on Monday morning, sends the manufacturer a proposal on Tuesday, and sends the startup a short note to keep in touch. By Friday the retailer has signed, the manufacturer is considering the proposal, and the startup is still on the list for next quarter.

Without the scores, the week would have looked different: the startup is enthusiastic and easy to talk to, the manufacturer is slow to reply, and the retailer is busy and hard to reach. Enthusiasm is not qualification, and the tool's job is to keep the two apart. The retailer was the best lead all along — the score just made it obvious.

Tips for a good result

Score on facts, not on feelings. A lead you like is not a lead with a budget, and a lead that is hard to reach may still be the best one on the list. If you do not know the answer to a question, score it zero — unknown is not a maybe, it is a gap, and the score should show it. You can always re-score when you find out.

Re-score when the facts change. A warm lead whose budget is confirmed becomes hot; a hot lead whose timeline slips becomes warm. The list is a living thing, and a score from last month may be wrong today. Delete and re-score rather than guessing at an adjustment — the four questions take a minute, and a fresh score is worth more than a patched one.

Work the list from the top and stop when the hot and warm leads are done. The cold leads will still be there next week, and chasing them today costs the time a hot lead needs. If the list has no hot leads, that is information too: the week's job is finding better leads, not working harder on weak ones.

Limits of the tool — what it cannot do

This tool adds up your judgement; it does not make the judgement for you. A score is only as good as the answers behind it, and honest answers take a real conversation with the lead. The tool cannot tell you whether a budget is real, whether a need is urgent, or whether the person you are talking to can actually decide — only the conversation can do that.

A high score is not a prediction of a sale. It means the lead looks promising on the four questions you answered, nothing more. Deals fall through for reasons no score captures: a change of management, a budget cut, a competitor's offer, a decision to do nothing. The score tells you where to spend your time; it does not tell you what will happen.

The tool does not store contact details, send messages, or remind you to follow up. It keeps a ranked list in this browser, and the follow-up is your job. And if the browser data is cleared, the list is gone — copy it regularly. Used within these limits, the tool does one job well: it stops the weak leads from eating the week that the strong leads deserve.

Questions people ask

What is lead qualification?

Sorting leads by how likely they are to buy, before you spend time on them. This tool scores four things — budget, need, timing and decision-maker — and ranks the leads so the best ones come first.

What makes a business lead worth chasing?

A confirmed budget, a real need, a near timeline, and access to the person who decides. A lead strong on most of these is worth chasing; a lead weak on all of them is not.

How should I score budget and timing?

Score what you know, honestly. A confirmed budget scores 3; no budget or an unknown budget scores 0. Timing now scores 3; no timeline scores 0. Unknown is a gap, not a maybe.

What is a decision-maker and why does it matter?

The person who can say yes to the purchase. Talking to the decision-maker scores 3; talking to someone who must ask scores less, because every extra step slows the deal and adds a chance of no.

How many leads should I follow up first?

Start with the hot leads — scores of 10 to 12 — then the warm ones. If there are no hot leads, spend the time finding better leads rather than chasing cold ones harder.

Does this store my leads online?

No. Leads are saved in this browser only, and nothing is uploaded or sent anywhere. No contact details are collected at all — only the company name and the four scores.
