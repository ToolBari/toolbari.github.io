What Retro Bounce Ball is

Retro Bounce Ball is a platform game in the style of the red-ball games people played on old phones in the 2000s. You control a small red ball that bounces all the time, on its own, without ever stopping. Your job is to steer it across 10 levels of platforms, gaps, spikes and moving platforms, collect the gems along the way, and reach the flag at the end of each level. There is no download, no signup and no account. The game opens in your browser, you tap Start, and the ball is bouncing within a second.

The whole game is built on one simple idea: the bounce never stops. You cannot make the ball stand still, and you cannot make it jump on command from a standstill — you can only steer it left and right, and time a jump so it lands exactly when the ball touches the ground. That one rule turns a simple ball into a game of rhythm and timing. A level you fail three times suddenly clicks on the fourth try, when your thumbs learn the bounce.

If you remember the red-ball platformer from old phones, the shape of this game will feel familiar at once: the cheerful colours, the gems floating above the gaps, the flag waiting at the far end. This is an original game written for this page, inspired by the classics of that era — not the old game itself, and not affiliated with any original publisher. The levels, the physics and the controls are all new, built for a phone screen first and a keyboard second.

How to play on your phone

Playing on your phone is the way this game was designed to be played. Tap Start Playing and the ball starts bouncing at once. Two big buttons sit at the bottom-left of the game area: LEFT and RIGHT, each 100 pixels tall, made to be held down with your left thumb. Hold RIGHT and the ball rolls right; hold LEFT and it rolls back. The JUMP button, just as big, sits at the bottom-right for your right thumb. Tap it just as the ball lands and the ball leaps much higher than its normal bounce — high enough to reach the raised platforms and the gems floating above the gaps.

You can also steer by swiping. Drag your finger left or right across the playfield and the ball steers in that direction for a short while. Swiping is handy when your thumbs are busy, or when you want a quick nudge without holding a button down. The two methods work together: hold a button for steady movement, swipe for a quick correction.

The Pause button sits at the top-right of the game area and stays visible the whole time you play. Tap it and the game freezes exactly where it is — the ball hangs in mid-air — so you can answer a message or take a breath without losing a life. The page never scrolls while you play: the playfield and the buttons are built so your thumbs stay on the game, not on the page behind it. Everything works in the Chrome browser on Android, and the game needs no install, no permissions and no storage beyond your best score and your unlocked levels.

How to play on a desktop

On a desktop or laptop the game plays just as well with a keyboard. The left and right arrow keys — or A and D — steer the ball, and Space, the up arrow or W makes it jump. The same timing rule applies: press jump just as the ball lands and you get the highest jump in the game. Press P at any time to pause, and M to turn the sound on or off. Sound is off by default, so the game starts silent and stays silent until you ask for bleeps.

If you prefer a mouse, you can hold the on-screen LEFT and RIGHT buttons with the mouse, and click JUMP — the buttons work with a mouse exactly as they do with a finger. The keyboard is the faster way to play once the levels get busy, because you can steer and jump at the same time without looking down at the screen.

The game also runs well on a school or work Chromebook, since it is only a web page with a canvas inside it. There is nothing to install, nothing to sign into, and no file to download. If the browser can open this page, it can run the game.

Gems, spikes, pits and moving platforms

Every level is built from the same four ingredients, mixed differently each time. Gems are the yellow diamonds floating above gaps and on top of platforms. Each one is worth 50 points, and they are almost always placed where the jump is hardest — above a wide gap, or at the top of a high bounce. You do not need to collect every gem to finish a level, but a run that collects them all scores far higher, and the best score is the real goal once you have finished all ten levels.

Spikes are the grey triangles sitting on top of some platforms from level 2 onward. Touch one and you lose a life — there is no health bar and no second chance on the same life. The spikes are always visible and always in the same place on a given level, so every death teaches you exactly where not to land. Falling into a pit costs a life too: the gaps between platforms are the oldest enemy in platform games, and this one is no exception. When you lose a life, you restart the level from the beginning with your gems reset, and you have three lives per run.

Moving platforms appear from level 4 onward. They slide slowly up and down, and they carry the ball with them when you land on one — ride one up and you can reach a gem or a platform that a normal bounce cannot reach. The trick is to land on a moving platform while it is rising, not while it is falling, and to jump off at the top of its travel. A moving platform is never the only way across a gap: every level can be finished on the still platforms alone, so the movers are a shortcut and a gem route, not a trap.

The 10 levels

The game has 10 levels, and each one is longer and busier than the last. Level 1 is a gentle introduction: wide platforms, small gaps, no spikes at all, and gems placed where a normal bounce can reach them. It exists to teach your thumbs the bounce rhythm, and most players clear it on the first or second try. Level 2 adds spikes on the wider platforms. Level 3 widens the gaps and starts staggering the platform heights, so you have to jump up as well as across.

Level 4 introduces moving platforms. Level 5 packs more spikes onto the platforms you would most like to land on. Levels 6 to 8 combine everything — wider gaps, higher platforms, spikes on the landing spots, moving platforms as the only way to some gems — and the levels get longer, so a single mistake costs more of your run. Levels 9 and 10 are the real test: long courses where a steady rhythm matters more than fast reflexes, and where one careless jump can undo a minute of careful play.

Clearing a level unlocks the next one, and your unlocked levels are saved on your own device — so you can close the page, come back tomorrow, and start from the level you reached. The level buttons under the game show which levels are open: tap any unlocked level to start your run there. Your best score is saved the same way, on your own device, and it is never sent anywhere.

Five tips that actually work

1. Learn the bounce rhythm before you rush. Spend the first level just watching the ball. It bounces at a fixed height and a fixed pace. Once you can feel when it will land, your jumps will land on time without thinking.

2. Jump as the ball lands, not before. The highest jump comes from pressing JUMP in the short moment when the ball touches the ground. Press too early and you get a small hop; press too late and the ball has already bounced away. Watch the ball, not the button.

3. Look ahead, not at the ball. Your eyes should be on the next platform and the next gap, not on the ball itself. The ball is predictable; the level ahead is where the decisions are. A quick look ahead tells you whether to jump now or wait one more bounce.

4. Take the gem routes only when you are sure. Gems sit above the hardest jumps for a reason. On a first run through a new level, ignore the gems and learn the safe route. On the second run, take the gem that sits over the gap you already know how to cross.

5. Slow down near spikes. The ball moves at a fixed speed, but you choose when to hold the button. Let go of RIGHT a bounce early, land short of a spike, and jump over it from a standstill bounce instead of flying into it at full speed.

Common mistakes

Jumping too early. The most common mistake in the game. A jump pressed while the ball is still falling does nothing — the jump only works in the short moment when the ball is on the ground. Wait for the landing, then press.

Hugging spikes. New players steer straight along a platform and land on the spike sitting in the middle of it. Spikes are always visible: if a platform has one, jump over it from the edge, or take the platform above or below instead.

Holding RIGHT forever. The ball only needs steering — it bounces on its own. Holding RIGHT through a whole level works until the first spike, the first narrow platform, or the first gap that needs a timed jump. Let go of the button and let the ball bounce in place while you think.

Chasing every gem. A gem above a wide gap is worth 50 points. A life is worth the whole run. On a level you have not finished yet, the flag matters more than any gem. Collect gems on runs where you already know the level.

Forgetting the level select. You do not have to start from level 1 every time. If level 7 is where your runs end, tap 7 in the level row and practise it directly. The levels you have unlocked stay unlocked.

Questions people ask

Is Retro Bounce Ball free?

Yes. Retro Bounce Ball is completely free to play in your browser. There is no download, no signup, no account and no payment at any point — you tap Start and you play.

How many levels are there?

There are 10 levels, each longer and harder than the last. Level 1 has no spikes at all, moving platforms appear from level 4, and levels 9 and 10 combine everything the game has taught you. Clearing a level unlocks the next one.

How do I jump higher?

The ball bounces on its own, but a jump timed to the landing goes much higher. Tap JUMP — or press Space — just as the ball touches the ground, and the ball leaps high enough to reach raised platforms and gems. Jump too early and you get a small hop instead.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes — the game was built for a phone first. Hold the big LEFT and RIGHT buttons to steer, tap the big JUMP button to jump, or swipe left and right on the playfield to steer. The Pause button stays visible at the top-right, and the page never scrolls while you play.

Where is my progress saved?

Your best score and your unlocked levels are saved on your own device, in your browser's own storage. Nobody else can see them, and they are never sent to any server. Clearing your browser data will remove them, and playing in a different browser starts fresh.

What happens when I hit a spike?

You lose a life and restart the level from the beginning, with the gems on that level reset. You have 3 lives per run — lose all three and the run ends. Spikes are always in the same place on a level, so each death shows you exactly where not to land next time.
