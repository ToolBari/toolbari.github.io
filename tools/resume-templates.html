Which template for whom
Chronological is the standard choice, and the right one for most people. Your jobs appear newest first, each with achievement bullets. Pick it when your work history is steady and your last job is close to the job you want. Hiring managers know this shape best, which is a reason to use it.
Functional leads with skills instead of jobs. Pick it when your skills matter more than your job titles — for example after a career break, or when your jobs were very different from each other. Your skills get full sections with proof, and your work history appears as a short list at the end.
Fresher is for a first job. It puts education and projects first, because those are a new graduate's strongest proof, and it treats part-time work, internships and volunteering as real experience — which they are.
Career Change is for moving into a new field. It leads with the skills that carry over, explains the move in one honest line, and gives space to any experience in the new field, even unpaid. It does not hide the change — it makes the case for it.
Student is for internships, placements and part-time work while you are still studying. It is short, it puts education first, and it has a line for your availability — the thing a part-time employer needs to know first.
If you are unsure, start with chronological. It is the shape most employers expect, and it is the hardest to get wrong.
Chronological vs functional
The two most-used templates differ in one thing: what the reader meets first. In a chronological resume, the reader meets your most recent job. In a functional resume, the reader meets your strongest skill. That first impression shapes everything that follows.
Chronological works best when your history tells a clear story: each job building on the last, in the same kind of work. The reader can see the path at a glance, and the dates prove it. Most hiring managers prefer it, partly because it is familiar and partly because it is honest — gaps and short stays are visible, and there is nowhere to hide them.
Functional works best when your history does not tell that story. Maybe you took three years away to care for family. Maybe your jobs were in different fields and the thread between them is a skill, not a job title. A functional resume lets that skill lead, with proof from several jobs grouped under it. The work history still appears — a good functional resume never hides it — but it appears briefly, at the end.
One honest note: some recruiters are suspicious of functional resumes, because they have seen them used to hide gaps. If you choose functional, keep the work history section complete and honest, with real dates. A template that hides something will cost you the interview when the truth comes out.

Making the template yours
A template is a starting point, not a finished resume. The first thing to do is replace every bracketed line with your own details — and then delete any section that does not apply to you. An empty section, or one filled with vague filler, is worse than no section at all.
Next, tailor it. Read the job description, find the skills and duties that repeat, and make sure your resume names them — in the employer's own words, where they are true for you. Change the summary for each job. Reorder your bullets so the achievement that fits this job comes first under each role. In the second quarter of 2026, tailored resumes reached interviews at 5.71 percent, against 3.09 percent for generic ones. The template gives you the shape; tailoring gives you the fit.
Then check the numbers. Every bullet should carry a number or a scale: how many people, how often, how big, how much. If a bullet has neither, ask yourself what actually changed because of you — and if nothing changed, cut the bullet.
Finally, read the finished resume out loud. Your ear catches what your eye misses: repeated words, awkward sentences, claims that sound bigger than the truth. Fix them, run the resume through the ATS checker with the job description, and then send it.
Mistakes to avoid
Leaving the brackets in. A resume that still says [Your name] or [Skill 1] tells the reader you did not finish the job. Fill in every line or delete it.
Copying the filled example. The examples show what good looks like — they are not your resume. Copy the structure, not the words. An interviewer will ask about every line, and a borrowed line has no story behind it.
Using the wrong template. A fresher using the chronological template looks thin; an experienced worker using the fresher template looks like they are hiding something. Pick the template that matches your history.
Adding fancy formatting. The templates are plain on purpose. Do not add tables, columns, graphics or unusual fonts when you fill them in — applicant tracking systems read plain resumes best.
Sending the same resume everywhere. Tailor the summary and the skills to each job. The same true history, arranged so the right parts are easy to find, is what gets interviews.
Claiming what you cannot prove. Only claim what you can back up in an interview. A resume that overstates the truth will cost you the job when it is found out.


Questions people ask


Are these resume templates free?
Yes. All five templates are free to use, with no account and no sign-up. Copy the structure, fill it in, and send it.
Which template should I use?
Chronological for most people — it is the shape employers expect. Functional after a break or a mixed history, fresher for a first job, career-change for a new field, student for internships and part-time work.
Will these templates pass an ATS?
Yes — they are one column, plain text, standard headings, no tables or graphics. For a full check, paste your filled resume into the ATS Resume Checker with the job description.
Can I change the template after I fill it in?
Yes. The structure is a starting point. Move sections, add a line, cut a section that does not apply — as long as the result stays plain and honest, it will work.
How long should my resume be?
One page if you can, especially early in your career. Two pages are fine with many years of experience, as long as the second page holds real achievements.
Should I use the filled example as my resume?
No. The examples show what good looks like — they are not your resume. Copy the structure and fill it with your own true details, or an interviewer will ask about a line you cannot explain.
