What this tool does

An email signature is the short block at the bottom of your emails that tells the reader who you are and how to reach you. A good signature has your name, your job, your company and one or two ways to contact you, and nothing else. A bad signature is a long mess of images, quotes and links that nobody reads and that looks unprofessional on a phone.

This page builds a clean signature for you. Type your name, job title, company, phone number, email address and website into the boxes at the top, pick one of three styles, and a preview appears below as you type. When you are happy with it, tap Copy HTML to copy the formatted version, or Copy plain text to copy the simple text version. Paste the result into your email app once, and every email you send after that carries your signature.

Everything you type stays in your browser. The signature is built on your own device, nothing is sent to a server, and nothing is stored anywhere. When you close the page, your details are gone. That is why there is no signup and no account: the tool does not need to remember you, because the signature lives in your email app once you paste it there, not on this page.

How to use it

On a phone, tap each box in turn and type your details. Your name is the only box that really matters. Every other box is optional, and anything you leave empty is left out of the signature cleanly, with no blank lines and no stray separators. If you do not have a company, leave the company box empty. If you do not want your phone number in your signature, leave that box empty too. The preview shows you exactly what the finished signature will look like.

After the contact boxes, pick a style. Simple is a plain text block with your name in bold. The photo style adds a round placeholder with your initials beside your details, which looks smart when you do not have a photo to use. The social style adds links to your pages, using the page addresses you type into the three social boxes below the style picker. If you leave the social boxes empty, the social style simply shows no links.

On a desktop computer the steps are the same, and you can tab from box to box with the keyboard. The preview updates with every keystroke, so you can watch the signature take shape as you type. When it looks right, copy it. Copy HTML gives you the formatted version with bold text and the initials circle, which is the version most people want. Copy plain text gives you the same words with no formatting, which is useful for email apps that do not accept formatted signatures.

What a good signature has

A good signature answers three questions in a few seconds: who are you, what do you do, and how do I reach you. Your name comes first, in bold, because it is the most important word in the block. Your job title and company come next, on the same line, separated by a simple bar. Then come your contact details: a phone number and an email address are usually enough. A website address is worth adding if you have one. That is the whole signature.

What a good signature must not have is almost as important. It must not have a large image, because a large image makes every email slow to load and often shows as a broken box on the reader's phone. It must not have a funny quote, a line of wisdom or a joke, because a joke in a work email is a risk that gains you nothing. It must not have eight social links, because nobody clicks the eighth link and a wall of icons looks desperate. Two or three links at most, and only to pages you actually keep up to date.

Keep the colours calm. A signature is not a poster. The styles on this page use one dark colour for text and one green for the initials circle, and that is enough. If you want your signature to match your company colours, you can change the colour after you paste it into your email app, but keep it to one accent colour and keep the text dark and readable.

The three styles

The simple style is the safest choice and the one most people should pick. It is a plain block of text with your name in bold and your details below it, one per line. It works in every email app, on every phone, and it never breaks. If you are not sure which style to choose, choose simple. A plain signature that always works is better than a fancy one that sometimes shows as a mess.

The photo style adds a round placeholder beside your details, with your initials in white on a green circle. It is for people who want a face to their name but do not have a photo to use, or who do not want a photo in their emails. The circle is built from text and colour, not from an image file, so it loads instantly and never shows as a broken picture. If you later want a real photo, your email app can usually add one, but the initials circle is a clean stand-in that looks professional on its own.

The social style adds links to your pages below your contact details. Type the address of each page into the social boxes, and the signature shows the page names as links, separated by bars. Only add pages you actually use. A link to a page that has not been updated in two years does more harm than good. If you have no social pages for work, do not use this style. The simple style with your phone number and email address is all you need.

Adding your signature to Gmail

Once you have copied your signature, adding it to Gmail takes about a minute. Open Gmail on a computer and open the settings, which you reach from the gear icon near the top right. In the settings, find the section for signatures. Create a new signature and give it a name, such as Work. Then click inside the signature box and paste. The formatted signature appears in the box. If you copied the plain text version, the words appear without formatting, and you can bold your name yourself with the formatting buttons.

Below the signature box, Gmail lets you choose when the signature is used. Set it to appear on new emails, and decide whether you want it on replies too. Many people keep it on new emails only, because a reply already carries the conversation and a full signature on every reply feels heavy. Save your changes at the bottom of the settings page, then send yourself a test email to see how the signature looks on a phone.

If the paste does not keep the formatting, try copying again and pasting with the normal paste command, not the paste-without-formatting command. The formatted version needs the normal paste to carry its bold text and colours with it. If it still looks wrong, use the plain text version and add the bold yourself. A plain signature that looks right is better than a formatted one that looks broken.

Adding your signature to Outlook

In Outlook, the steps are similar but the menus have different names. Open Outlook and find the settings or options area, then look for the mail settings and the signatures section. Create a new signature, give it a name, and paste your copied signature into the editing box. Outlook keeps the formatting from the HTML version, including the bold name and the initials circle if you picked the photo style.

Outlook also lets you choose which signature to use for new messages and which for replies, and you can keep more than one signature and switch between them. If you use Outlook on both a computer and a phone, add the signature on the computer first, because the phone app has fewer signature options. Send yourself a test email and open it on your phone to check that the signature looks the way you want it to.

One thing to know about Outlook: it sometimes adds extra space around a pasted signature. If your signature looks too tall, click inside it and remove the empty lines. The signature built on this page has no blank lines in it, so any extra space comes from the paste, and a quick tidy in the editing box fixes it.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is a giant image. A photo of yourself at full size, a company logo as large as the email itself, or a banner advertising a sale all make your emails look like spam. Keep images out of your signature entirely. The initials circle on this page gives you a visual mark without any of the problems of an image.

The second mistake is too many links. A signature with a link to every page you have ever made tells the reader nothing, because nobody will click through them all. Pick the one or two pages that matter and leave the rest out. The third mistake is a funny quote. Humour in a signature is a gamble: what is funny to you may read as unprofessional to a client who does not know you yet. Leave the quotes out.

The fourth mistake is an out-of-date signature. People change jobs, change numbers and change companies, and their signature keeps showing the old details for months. Check your signature every few months, and rebuild it here whenever something changes. The fifth mistake is forgetting the test email. Always send yourself one test message after setting up a signature, and open it on your phone, because that is where most of your readers will see it.

It does not store your details, so if you close the page you will need to type them again. It does not check whether your phone number or website address is correct, so read the preview carefully before you copy it.

The signature also shows your email address as plain text, not as a clickable link. That is deliberate. A plain text address works in every email app, never breaks, and can be copied by anyone who needs it. The social style does include links, but only to the page addresses you type in yourself. The generator never adds a link you did not ask for, and it never changes the words you typed.
