What this is

This page is a barcode and QR scanner that runs entirely in your browser. There is no app to install, no account to create, and nothing to pay for. You open the page, tap the scan button, point your camera at a code, and the page shows you the text hidden inside it. That text might be a web address, a few lines of plain writing, a product number, or the details of a ticket or a menu. Whatever the code holds, this page simply reads it out and lets you copy it.

A QR code is the square pattern of black and white blocks you see on posters, tables, tickets, and packaging. A barcode is the set of vertical stripes you see on almost everything sold in a shop. Both are just ways of writing information in a shape a camera can read quickly, and this page reads both kinds.

Everything happens on your own device. The camera image never leaves your phone or computer, and nothing you scan is saved or sent anywhere. If you need to make a code of your own instead of reading one, try ToolBari's own QR code generator, which turns any text or web address into a QR code you can print or share.

How to use it

Using the scanner takes less than a minute. On a phone, open this page in your browser and tap the big Scan button. Your browser will ask for permission to use the camera — say yes, because the scanner cannot see anything without it. The camera view appears on the page. Hold your phone so the code fills the middle of the frame, keep your hand steady, and wait a moment. When the code is recognised, the camera stops and the decoded text appears on the page, with a button to copy it.

On a desktop or laptop, the same steps work if your computer has a webcam. Tap Scan, allow the camera, and hold the printed code up in front of the webcam. It helps to hold the code still and flat, a hand's width away from the lens, and to keep the room well lit.

If you change your mind while scanning, tap the Stop button. The camera switches off at once and nothing is kept. If the camera permission was denied by mistake, allow the camera in your browser settings and try again — the page will ask once more.

Which browsers work — and which do not

This scanner uses the BarcodeDetector that is built into some browsers. It works in Chrome on Android, where that detector is part of the browser. Other browsers vary: some have the same built-in detector and some do not, and the situation changes as browsers are updated, so this page checks for the detector every time it loads rather than guessing from the browser's name.

If your browser does not have the detector, this page tells you so plainly, right where the scanner would be. It does not show a fake camera view, a fake loading bar, or a made-up result. A scanner that pretends to read a code it cannot read would be worse than useless, because you would trust an answer that was never real.

There is a simple fallback. The camera app on most modern phones already scans QR codes on its own: open the camera, point it at the code, and a small notice appears with the link or text. That works even when this page cannot scan, and it needs no extra app either. Older phones and older browsers may not support either route, and in that case the honest answer is that scanning needs a newer browser or a newer phone.

Tips for a clean scan

Most failed scans are not the scanner's fault — they are about light, distance, and movement. A few small habits make a big difference.

Good light comes first. Scan in a bright room or in daylight, and avoid pointing the camera into a strong light behind the code, which turns the code into a dark shape the camera cannot read. If you are scanning in the evening, switch a lamp on and point it at the code, not at the camera.

Fill the frame. Move the phone closer until the code takes up most of the camera view, but not so close that the edges are cut off. The whole code, including the quiet white border around it, needs to be visible at once.

Hold still. A shaky hand blurs the fine detail the scanner needs. Rest your elbows on a table if you can, or hold the phone with both hands, and give the scanner a second or two to settle. If the code is on a screen, turn the screen brightness up and tilt the phone slightly to avoid glare. If the code is printed on curved packaging, flatten it with your hand if you can. And if the code itself is torn, faded, or smudged, no scanner can read the missing parts — a clean print is the one thing this page cannot provide for you.

What QR codes and barcodes are, in plain words

A barcode is a number written as stripes. Each stripe's width stands for a digit, and a scanner reads the pattern of wide and narrow stripes and turns it back into the number. That is why the barcode on a tin of beans is just a long number: the number identifies the product, and the shop's till looks the number up. The barcode itself holds no price, no name, and no secret — only the number.

A QR code works the same way, but in two directions instead of one. Its grid of black and white squares can hold much more: a web address, a few sentences of text, a Wi-Fi password, or the details of a ticket. The three large squares in the corners help the camera find the code and work out which way up it is, which is why a QR code can be read at an angle.

Both kinds of code are only containers. Reading one is like opening an envelope: the scanner shows you what is inside, and then it is up to you to decide what to do with it. A QR code that holds a web address is only as trustworthy as the address itself, so if a code appears somewhere unexpected, read it first and think before you open the link.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is scanning in the dark. A camera needs light to see the difference between black and white, and in a dim room the code becomes a grey blur. Move into better light before you blame the scanner.

The second is distance. Holding the phone a full arm's length from a small code makes the pattern too tiny to read, and pressing the phone right against the code cuts the edges off. A hand's width away is a good starting point, then move in or out slowly until the code fills the frame.

The third is movement — waving the phone around in the hope that the scanner will catch the code in passing. It will not. Hold the phone still and let the scanner work.

The fourth is expecting the page to work on a browser that does not support it. If this page tells you your browser cannot scan, no amount of tapping will change that. Use the camera app on your phone instead, or open the page in a browser that has the built-in detector.

The last mistake is trusting a code blindly. A printed code can hold any web address at all, including one that leads somewhere you would not choose to go. This page only shows you the text — it never opens a link for you — so take a moment to read what the code says before you act on it.

What it cannot do

Scanning uses your browser's built-in detector (Chrome on Android). If your browser does not support it, this page says so — it never fakes a decoded result.

This page cannot scan without a camera, and it cannot scan if you do not allow the camera. It cannot read a code that is torn, faded, or hidden behind glare, and it cannot guess the missing parts of a damaged code. It cannot scan a photo from your gallery — it reads only what the live camera sees — so to scan a code on your screen you need a second device, or you can read the code's text another way.

It cannot tell you whether a link inside a code is safe. It shows you the text exactly as the code holds it, and the judgement is yours. It cannot open the link for you, fill in a form, or connect to Wi-Fi on its own. It cannot store your scans, so if you need a result later, copy it before you leave the page.

And it cannot do any of this in a browser without the built-in detector. That limit is not something this page can work around, and it will not pretend otherwise. When the detector is missing, the page says so, and the fallback is your phone's own camera app — which, for QR codes, works just as well.

Questions people ask

Is this scanner really free?

Yes. It runs entirely in your browser, there is nothing to install, no account to create, and no charge of any kind. The only thing it needs is a camera and a browser with the built-in detector.

Why does the page say my browser does not support scanning?

The scanner needs the BarcodeDetector that is built into some browsers, and it works in Chrome on Android. If your browser does not have it, this page tells you so plainly instead of faking a scan. You can still scan a QR code with your phone's camera app, which reads codes on its own.

Does this page save or send what I scan?

No. Scanning happens entirely on your device, and nothing you scan is sent anywhere or stored anywhere. When you close the page, the result is gone, so copy any text you want to keep.

What kinds of codes can it read?

It reads QR codes and the most common barcodes: EAN-13, EAN-8, Code 128, Code 39, UPC-A, and UPC-E. If a code uses a different format, the scanner will simply not recognise it.

Why will the camera not start?

The usual reason is that camera permission was denied, so allow the camera in your browser settings and try again. The camera may also be in use by another app, or the device may have no camera at all. If the problem continues, try your phone's camera app instead.

Can I scan a code from a photo in my gallery?

No. This page scans only through the live camera, so it cannot read a code from a saved photo. To scan a code shown on your screen, use a second device, or open the code's web address another way.
