How to use this guide

A PDF is meant to look the same everywhere, which is why it is such a good format for sharing, and also why it feels so hard to change. The good news is that the four jobs most people actually need — fixing a typo, adding a note, filling in a form, and signing — are all free to do, and none of them needs a paid app. This guide covers each of those four jobs step by step, for phone and for desktop, and it is honest about what free editors cannot do, so you do not waste time on a job that needs a different tool.

This page does not edit your files, because a web page on its own cannot change a PDF file. What it does is show you the free editors that can, with the steps for each job, and a picker that recommends the right free method for your job and your device. Start with the picker: choose your job and your device, and it will name a free method and show its first three steps.

Every method below is free. If an editor asks you to pay before it lets you download the result, you have reached the wrong tool: close it and try one of the free ones named here. And before you edit anything, save a copy of the original file. Editing a copy costs nothing and saves you from losing the only version you have.

The four editing jobs

Job 1 — fix a typo. A wrong word, a wrong date, or a misspelled name is the most common reason to edit a PDF. On a desktop, open the file in a free editor such as PDF24 Tools, click the text you want to change, and type the correction. On a phone, open a free online editor such as Sejda in your browser, upload the file, tap the word, and fix it. The change is small, so it usually looks clean. One thing to watch: if the new word is longer than the old one, it may spill over the words next to it, because a PDF does not reflow text the way a word processor does. If that happens, shorten the replacement or split it across the space you have.

Job 2 — add text or a note. Sometimes you need to add words to a page: a note in the margin, a missing line, or a short comment for the reader. Every free editor has a text box tool for this. Open the file, choose the text tool, click where the note should go, and type. On a desktop, PDF24 Tools and LibreOffice Draw both handle this well. On a phone, the Adobe Acrobat online editor has a comment tool that works the same way. Keep added text short, and place it where there is empty space, so it does not cover the words that are already there. If you need to add a whole paragraph, the PDF is the wrong place for it: edit the original document instead, if you have it, and make a fresh PDF.

Job 3 — fill in a form. Forms are the job free editors handle best. Many PDF forms have fields you can click straight into: tap a field, type your answer, and move to the next one. If the form has no fields, use the text box tool to type your answers on top of the lines. On a phone, the Adobe Acrobat online editor is a good choice for forms, because its fields are easy to tap. On a desktop, Sejda works well. Fill in every field, then read the whole form once before you save it, because a missed field is the most common form mistake. The section below on filling forms properly has the full routine.

Job 4 — add a signature. Signing a PDF is easier than most people expect. Every free editor has a sign tool: you draw your signature with a finger or a mouse, or type your name and let the tool turn it into a written-style signature, then you place it on the signature line. On a phone, drawing with your finger is often the easiest way, and the Adobe Acrobat online editor handles it well. On a desktop, PDF24 Tools and Sejda both have sign tools. The section below on signing a PDF on a phone walks through the steps in detail.

There is one more route worth knowing. If the PDF is mostly text and you need to change a lot of it, you can open it in Google Docs through Google Drive, edit it there like a normal document, and download it as a PDF again. This works best for simple documents: the layout may shift a little, so check the result before you send it.

Filling in forms properly

Forms deserve a little care, because a filled form is usually sent to someone else, and a mistake on it looks careless at best. Start by saving a copy of the blank form, so you can start again if you make a mess of it. Then open the copy in your editor and work through the fields in order, top to bottom, so you do not skip one. If a field is too small for your answer, shorten the answer rather than letting the text spill outside the box.

Dates and numbers need extra attention, because a wrong date on a form can cause real trouble. Type dates in the format the form asks for, and read numbers back twice before you move on. If the form asks for a signature, leave that until last, after every other field is filled and checked. When the form is complete, read it once from the top, as if you were the person receiving it. Then save it under a clear name, such as the form name plus your name, so the person who receives it can tell whose form it is.

One more habit is worth building: keep the blank original. If the form is rejected and you need to fill it in again, starting from the blank copy is much faster than starting from a filled one and clearing every field by hand.

Signing a PDF on a phone

Signing on a phone is the easiest way to sign, because you can draw your signature with your finger. Open a free online editor in your phone browser, such as the Adobe Acrobat online editor, and upload the PDF. Find the sign tool, which is usually marked with a pen icon, and choose the option to draw a signature. Draw your signature on the screen with your finger, taking your time: a slow, careful signature looks far better than a rushed one. If it looks wrong, clear it and draw it again. There is no limit on tries.

Once the signature looks right, place it on the signature line. Most editors let you drag it into place and resize it by pulling a corner. Make it about the same size as the line it sits on: a signature that is far too big looks careless, and one that is far too small is hard to see. If the document needs a date next to the signature, add it with the text tool while you are there. Then download the signed file and open it to check that the signature sits where it should. Save the signed file under a new name, so you can tell it apart from the unsigned original.

What free editors cannot do

Free editors are good at small changes, and it helps to know where they stop. They cannot reflow a whole document: if you add a long paragraph to a page, the words below it will not move down to make room, because a PDF fixes every word in place. They cannot edit a scanned page at all, because a scan is a picture of a page, not real text. If you open a scanned PDF in an editor and cannot click on the words, the page is a scan, and you need OCR first: our PDF OCR and Text Extractor guide explains how OCR reads the words in a scanned page.

Free editors also struggle with pictures. You can usually move or delete an image, but replacing one with a new image at the same size and position is fiddly, and the result often looks slightly off. If a picture needs changing, it is usually better to change it in the original document and make a fresh PDF. And if your PDF needs a whole new layout, a PDF editor is the wrong tool entirely: that is a job for the app that made the document in the first place.

Common mistakes

Editing the only copy. Always save a copy first and edit the copy. If something goes wrong — a wrong edit, a file that will not save, a signature in the wrong place — the original is still there, and you can start again without losing anything.

Using a shady site for private documents. Not every free PDF site is careful with your files. Stick to well-known editors, and for anything private, such as a contract or a medical record, use a free desktop app so the file never leaves your computer. If a site looks rough, asks for payment, or shows you pages of adverts before it lets you work, close it and use a better-known one.

Trying to edit a scanned page. If you cannot click on the words in a PDF, the page is a scan, and no editor will let you change the text. Run it through OCR first, or ask whoever sent it for the original document. Typing a correction on top of a scan with a text box can work for one word, but it looks like a patch, because it is one.

Adding too much text to a page. A PDF does not reflow, so a long addition will cover the words below it or spill off the page. Keep additions short, and if you need to add a paragraph, edit the original document instead.

Forgetting to check the result. Always open the edited file and read it before you send it. Check that the change is where you meant it to be, that nothing is covered up, and that the file still opens cleanly. A minute of checking saves an embarrassing correction later.

Questions people ask

Can I edit a PDF for free without signing up?

Yes. Several free online editors let you fix text, add notes, fill forms and sign in your browser without an account. This guide shows the free methods for phone and desktop, step by step.

Can I edit a PDF on my phone?

Yes. Open a free online PDF editor in your phone browser, upload the file, make your change, and download the result. For a signature, the phone is often the easiest device, because you can draw it with your finger.

Why can I not edit the text in my PDF?

The page may be a scanned image, not real text. If the PDF is a photo of a page, there is no text to edit until you run it through OCR, which reads the words in the image. Our PDF OCR guide explains how to do that.

Is it safe to upload a private PDF to a free online editor?

For ordinary files, a well-known online editor is usually fine. For private files such as contracts or medical records, use a free desktop app instead, so the file never leaves your computer.

Can I change the layout of a whole PDF for free?

Not really. Free editors are good at small changes: a typo, a note, a filled form, a signature. Reflowing a whole document, moving pictures around, or redesigning pages is a job for a proper document app, not a PDF editor.

Should I edit the only copy of my PDF?

No. Always save a copy first and edit the copy. If something goes wrong, the original is still there, and you can start again without losing anything.
