How affiliate commission really works

Affiliate marketing is simple at its core. You recommend a product, someone clicks your special link, and if they buy, the seller pays you a share of the sale. That share is your commission. It is usually a percentage of the price, so a 10 percent rate on a product that sells for 50 pays you 5 on each sale. Some programmes pay a fixed amount per sale instead, which is simpler to predict but does not grow when the buyer spends more.

The link is what connects the sale to you. When someone clicks it, the seller's site records that the visit came from you, usually with a small file called a cookie stored in the buyer's browser. If the buyer purchases within the programme's cookie window, the sale counts as yours and you earn the commission. If they buy after the window has closed, the sale still happens, but you earn nothing from it.

What counts as a sale also matters. Most programmes pay only on completed, paid orders. Some pay when the buyer signs up for a free trial. A few pay per click or per lead, but those are the exception, not the rule. Read the terms of any programme you join, because the definition of a sale, the cookie window, and the return rule are the three things that decide what you actually get paid.

A worked example

Take a product that sells for 50, with a commission rate of 10 percent. You earn 5 on every sale. Suppose 1,000 people click your link in a month, and 2 percent of them buy. That is 20 sales. Twenty sales at 5 each comes to 100 a month. Those are the numbers this calculator starts with, so press the button and check them for yourself.

Now suppose you want to earn 200 a month from the same product. You need twice as many sales, which means twice as many clicks: 2,000 clicks a month at the same conversion rate. Or you could keep the same traffic and promote a product that pays twice as much per sale. Either route reaches the same target, and the calculator shows you both, because you can change any number and see the result straight away.

The example also shows why the conversion rate matters more than most beginners think. If your conversion rate rises from 2 percent to 3 percent, your sales rise from 20 to 30 a month, and your earnings rise from 100 to 150, without a single extra click. Improving the page your link points to, or recommending a product your audience actually wants, is often worth more than chasing more traffic.

Picking products worth promoting

A high price is not always better. An expensive product pays more per sale, but fewer people buy it, and the conversion rate is often lower. A cheap product pays less per sale, but it sells more easily, and a small commission on many sales can beat a large commission on almost none. Judge a product by what it earns you in a month, which is the number this calculator gives you, not by the size of a single commission.

Trust matters more than price. Your audience buys because they trust your recommendation, and that trust is easy to lose. Promote products you have actually used, or products you have researched well enough to describe honestly. A product that disappoints the buyer costs you more than the refund. It costs you the next recommendation too, because the buyer will not trust it.

Look at the whole programme, not just the rate. A programme with a long cookie window, a low return rate, and clear terms can pay better than a programme with a higher rate and a one-day window. Check how and when you get paid, what the minimum payout is, and whether the programme is known for paying on time. A generous rate that never reaches your account is worth nothing.

What the cookie window really means

The cookie window is the number of days after a click during which a sale still counts as yours. Common windows are 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, and 90 days, and the difference between them is enormous. With a 24-hour window, only buyers who decide on the same day count. With a 30-day window, a buyer who thinks about it for three weeks still earns you a commission.

The window matters most for expensive products, because people take longer to decide on a big purchase. Nobody buys a costly item on impulse from a single click. They compare, they read reviews, and they come back days later. If your cookie window is short, most of those careful buyers will buy outside it, and you will have done the recommending while someone else gets the sale.

Some programmes also have a last-click rule, which means the commission goes to the last affiliate link the buyer clicked before purchasing, not the first. If a buyer clicks your link and then clicks someone else's review link a week later, the other affiliate gets the commission. There is nothing you can do about that rule, but it is worth knowing, because it explains why some sales you expected never appear in your earnings.

Returns, refunds and lost commission

Most programmes cancel the commission when an order is returned or refunded. That is fair, because the seller did not keep the money either, but it means your earnings are only certain once the return period has passed. Some programmes hold your payments for 30 days or more for exactly this reason, so the money you see in your dashboard is not always the money you will actually receive.

Return rates differ wildly by product type. Clothing and shoes are returned often, because sizes and fits vary. Digital products are rarely returned, because there is nothing to send back. A product with a 20 percent return rate quietly cuts your earnings by a fifth, which can turn a product that looked profitable into one that barely pays. If you can find out the return rate before you promote a product, do so, and if you cannot, assume that some of your sales will come back.

The best defence against returns is honest recommending. Buyers return products that did not match what they were promised. If you describe a product accurately, including its flaws, the people who buy it are the people who actually want it, and far fewer of them will send it back.

How long does it take to earn?

Affiliate income is slow at the start. In the first weeks, you may earn nothing at all, because your audience is small and your links are new. That is normal, and it is not a sign that the method has failed. The earnings grow as your audience grows, as your pages rank in search results, and as older recommendations keep bringing in clicks long after you wrote them.

The honest way to think about it is in months, not days. Give a product three months of genuine recommending before you judge it. If it earns nothing in three months despite real traffic, the product or the audience is wrong, and it is time to try something else. If it earns a little, it may be worth more effort, because small earnings in the first months often grow as your content reaches more people.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is trusting a rate without reading the terms. A headline rate of 30 percent sounds wonderful until you discover it applies only to the first purchase, or only to one product in the range, or only if you sell a certain number each month. Read the terms before you promote anything, and check the cookie window and the return rule while you are there.

The second mistake is promoting too many products at once. A page full of links earns less than a page with one honest recommendation, because the reader cannot tell which product you actually believe in. Pick a few products that fit your audience, write about them properly, and let the rest go.

The third mistake is chasing traffic instead of conversions. Ten thousand clicks that buy nothing earn nothing. A hundred clicks from people who trust you and want the product can earn more than a thousand clicks from strangers who clicked by accident. Spend your effort on the audience and the recommendation, and the clicks will follow.

The fourth mistake is forgetting that affiliate income is uneven. Some months are good, some are quiet, and a programme can change its rates or close without warning. Treat affiliate earnings as one income stream among several, not as a salary you can count on, and never spend money you have not yet been paid.

Questions people ask

What is a good commission rate?

It depends on the product. Digital products often pay 20 to 50 percent, while physical products usually pay much less, often between 1 and 10 percent. A high rate on a cheap product can still pay less than a low rate on an expensive one, so judge a product by what you earn per sale, not by the rate alone.

How does the cookie window affect my earnings?

The cookie window is the number of days after a click during which a sale still counts as yours. If the window is 30 days and the buyer buys on day 20, you earn the commission. If the window is only 24 hours and the buyer waits a week, you earn nothing. A longer window usually means more of your clicks turn into paid sales.

Do I earn if the buyer returns the product?

Usually not. Most programmes cancel the commission when an order is returned or refunded, and some hold your earnings for a while to make sure returns are counted first. That is why a product with a high return rate can look good on paper and pay badly in practice. Always check the return rule in the programme terms.

How many clicks do I need to earn $100?

It depends on your earnings per sale and your conversion rate. If you earn 5 per sale and 2 out of every 100 clicks buy, you need 1,000 clicks to reach 100. Type your own price, rate and conversion rate into the calculator, set the target to 100, and it works out the exact number of clicks for you.

Fixed fee or percentage — which is better?

A percentage pays more when the price is high, and a fixed fee pays the same whatever the buyer spends. If you promote expensive products, a percentage usually wins. If you promote cheap products, a fixed fee can pay more per sale than a small percentage. Work out both with the calculator before you choose.

Do I need a website to do affiliate marketing?

No. Many affiliates start with a social media page, a video channel or a newsletter instead of a website. What matters is an audience that trusts your recommendations. A website helps over time, because it keeps working while you sleep, but it is not required to make your first sale.
