How the organizer works

This organizer is a simple place to keep the books you have read, each with your own one-paragraph summary and a rating from 1 to 5. You add a book by typing its title, the author's name, your summary and your rating, then pressing the button. The book joins your list, which you can search by title, author or summary, and filter by rating.

Everything is stored in your own browser, on your own device. There is no account, no sign-up, and nothing is sent to a server. Your books are there when you come back to the page, and they leave when you clear your browser data or press the clear button.

You can copy the books shown on screen to paste into a note or a message, or export them as a plain text file to keep somewhere safe. The export lists each book with its author, rating and summary, in the order you added them.

What makes a good summary

A good summary is one paragraph in your own words. Say what the book is about in two or three sentences, then say what you took from it. The second part matters more than the first: a summary you can use later is one that reminds you why the book was worth your time.

Avoid copying the back cover or a review. The point of a summary is to fix the book in your own memory, and copying does not do that. Write it as if you were telling a friend whether to read it: what it is, what it does well, and what it left you thinking about.

Keep it short. A paragraph of fifty to a hundred words is plenty. If you find yourself writing an essay, you are probably retelling the plot — step back and write the point of the book instead.

Who it is for

This organizer is for readers who want to remember what they read: students keeping track of course books, book club members comparing notes, parents building a reading list for their children, and anyone who has ever finished a book and forgotten it a month later.

It suits a small personal library best. If you read a book or two a month, the list stays manageable and the search stays fast. If you read much more, use the search and the rating filter to find the books that mattered, and export the list now and then as a backup.

The organizer is not a book shop, a review site or a social network. It does not recommend books, show other people's ratings, or link to anywhere you can buy a book. It is a private notebook for your own reading.

How to use it step by step

Start by adding a book you have recently finished. Type the title, the author's name if you know it, your one-paragraph summary, and a rating from 1 to 5. Press the button and the book appears in your list below.

To find a book later, type a word in the search box — a title, an author's name, or a word from your summary. The list narrows as you type. To see only your favourites, use the rating filter to show books rated 4 or higher, or 5 only.

To remove a book, press its Delete button. To start over, press Clear all books — this removes everything, so export your list first if you want to keep it. To keep a copy outside the browser, press Export to download a plain text file.

Reading with a purpose

A list of books you have read becomes more useful when you read with a purpose. Before starting a book, decide what you want from it: a skill, a story, a new way of thinking, or simple enjoyment. When you finish, your summary writes itself, because you already know what you were looking for.

Your ratings are a guide to what to read next. If the books you rated 5 share an author, a genre or a subject, that is a signal worth following. If a type of book keeps getting a 2, stop forcing yourself to finish it — life is short and the list of books worth reading is long.

Re-reading is worth a place in the list too. A book you loved at twenty reads differently at forty, and a second summary next to the first shows how your thinking has changed. The organizer does not stop you adding the same title twice, so a re-read can have its own entry and its own rating.

Finally, use the list to set a gentle goal. A book a month is twelve books a year, and twelve summaries is a record you will be glad to have. The number matters less than the habit: finish a book, write a paragraph, add it to the list.

Mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is writing a summary that is only plot. A plot summary tells you what happened, but it does not tell you what the book meant to you. Add one sentence about what you took from the book, and the summary becomes useful months later.

Another mistake is rating everything 5. If every book is a 5, the rating filter is useless. Be honest: a 3 means you liked it, a 4 means you would recommend it, and a 5 means it stayed with you. The ratings are yours, so use the full range.

A third mistake is forgetting that the list lives in one browser. If you clear your browser data, the books go with it. Export the list now and then, especially after adding several books, so you always have a copy outside the browser.

Finally, do not paste summaries you copied from elsewhere. The organizer is for your own words, and your own words are what make the list worth keeping. A copied paragraph tells you nothing about what you thought.

Keeping your list useful

A reading list stays useful if you tend it a little. Add a book soon after finishing it, while the summary is fresh. A summary written a month later is thinner and harder to write, and the book's details fade fast.

Once in a while, read through your list. The books you rated 5 will remind you what you love, and the books you rated 2 will remind you what to avoid. Patterns appear: a favourite author, a genre that always works, a type of book that never does.

Share the list with people you trust. A short summary and an honest rating are more useful than a star rating on a shop page, because they come from someone the reader knows. Copy the shown books into a message and let your friends see what you have been reading.
