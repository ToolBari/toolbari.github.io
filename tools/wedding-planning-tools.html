What this page does

Planning a wedding means holding four different lists in your head at once. There is the money: how much you have, how much each part will cost, and how much is left. There is the guest list: who is coming, who has confirmed, and who still needs an answer. There is the checklist: the venue, the food, the clothes, the photos, and the small jobs that are easy to forget until the last week. And there is the date itself, counting down whether you are ready or not. Most couples keep these lists in different places, and the pieces slowly stop matching each other.

This page puts all four jobs in one place. It is a free wedding planning tool that runs entirely in your browser. There is nothing to install, no account to create, and no signup form standing between you and your plan. You open the page, type in your numbers and names, and everything is saved on your device, so you can come back tomorrow and pick up where you left off. The budget planner keeps your spending honest, the guest list keeps your headcount honest, the checklist keeps your timing honest, and the countdown keeps all of it moving.

How to use it on your phone or computer

Open this page in any web browser, on a phone, a tablet, or a computer. The layout is the same everywhere: the budget planner first, then the guest list, then the checklist, then the countdown, with a copy button at the bottom that turns the whole plan into a short text summary you can paste into a message or a note. On a phone everything sits in one column, and the buttons are large enough to tap comfortably. On a computer the same page is wider and easier to type into, which suits a long guest list.

Work in short sessions rather than trying to finish everything at once. Add the total budget first, then the categories, then the guests, then the tasks, and set the date whenever you know it. Saving is automatic: every change is stored in your browser the moment you make it, so there is no save button to remember. If two people are planning together, keep the master copy on one person's device and share the copied summary with the other, because the page does not sync between devices. That one habit prevents most of the confusion that comes from two versions of the same plan.

Start with the budget

The budget is the number everything else follows. Before you look at venues or count guests, agree on one total number between everyone who is paying. It does not have to be exact, but it does have to be one number that everyone accepts, because every decision after this one is measured against it. Type that number into the total budget box at the top of the page. All amounts on this page are plain numbers with no currency attached, so use whatever currency you use in real life and keep it the same all the way through.

Next, add the categories that will take the money: the venue, the food, the clothes, the photos, the music, the decorations, the transport, and anything else your wedding will need. For each one, enter the amount you plan to spend and the amount you have spent so far. The page shows what is left in each category, and the totals at the bottom show what you have spent overall and what is left of the whole budget. If a category goes over its planned amount, the page says so plainly, in words, so an overspend is never hidden inside a total.

A budget is not a guess you make once and forget. Check it every week as you book things, and update the spent amounts as money actually goes out. When one category goes over, the honest fix is to take the difference from another category. A plan you can see is a plan you can adjust, while a plan in your head quietly grows until the week before the wedding.

The guest list

The guest list is where weddings grow. Every name adds a seat, a meal, and a place at a table, so every name also adds to the budget and to the size of the hall you need. That is why the list deserves a calm decision early, before invitations go out and the numbers become hard to change. Start with the people who must be there: close family and the friends you could not imagine the day without. Then add the people you would love to have there. Only after that, and only if the hall and the budget allow it, add the people it would be nice to invite.

Decide together who makes the final call on the list. When two families are involved, agree on how many places each side gets, and let each side choose its own names within that number. It is a small piece of fairness that prevents a large amount of argument. Add each name to the guest list on this page, and tick the box when they confirm. The count at the bottom shows the total and the confirmed number side by side, so you always know how many answers are still missing. Before you send anything, compare the total with the size of the hall: a list that is bigger than the room is a problem that only gets harder to solve.

The checklist, in the right order

A checklist only helps if the jobs are in the right order. The venue comes first, because it decides the date, the guest count, the layout of the day, and a large part of the budget. Until the venue is booked, most other decisions are guesses. Food comes second: the caterer, the menu, and the cake, because good caterers book up early and the menu shapes the whole day. After those two, come the things that take time to make or order, such as clothes, invitations, and the photographer.

The small things come last, and that is not a criticism of them. Decorations, favours, the music for the evening, and the transport on the day all matter, but none of them can be finished properly until the big decisions are made. Add each task to the checklist with a due date, and check it off when it is done. The count at the bottom shows how many tasks are done out of the total, which is a fairer picture than a feeling. A task without a date is a wish, not a plan, so give every task a date, even if the date is only a rough guess to start with.

Work through the list from the top, and resist the urge to start with the easy jobs. Ticking off ten small tasks feels good, but it does not move the wedding forward if the venue is still unbooked. When a task is done, check it off straight away, so the list stays up to date.

The countdown

Pick your wedding date and the page counts the days left. The countdown turns a vague future into a single number, and that number is useful in two ways. First, it tells you how much time you really have, which is often less than it feels like. Second, it lets you work backwards: if there are ninety days left and the venue is not booked, the next week has one clear job in it. If the date changes, pick the new date and the countdown updates straight away, while your budget, guests, and tasks stay exactly as they were.

If the date you enter is in the past, the page says it has already passed. It never shows a negative number of days. When the day itself arrives, the countdown says the wedding is today, and the plan it holds becomes a record of how the day was put together.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is having no budget at all. Without one total number to measure against, every choice is made on its own, and the total only appears at the end, when it is too late to change. The second mistake is the guest list outgrowing the room: inviting 500 people to a hall that holds 200, and discovering the problem only when the replies come in. The third is leaving everything to the last month, when the good suppliers are booked and every small decision feels urgent.

Smaller mistakes add up too. Forgetting the small costs, such as transport and the little extras on the day, can quietly eat the gap between the budget and the spending. Letting the guest list grow without checking the hall turns a seating plan into a puzzle with no solution. Booking things in the wrong order, such as ordering decorations before the venue is chosen, means paying twice when the first choice does not fit the room. Writing the plan down, in one place, on this page, fixes all of these at once.

What this page cannot do

This page plans and counts. It does not book venues, send invitations, or pay anyone, and it does not talk to the venue, the caterer, or the photographer on your behalf. Those are jobs for you and the people you hire, and this page keeps the numbers for those jobs in order. It also does not remind you of anything: there are no notifications and no alarms, so the checklist only works if you open the page and look at it. And because everything is saved only on this device, it cannot show your plan to anyone else unless you share the copied summary with them.

Those limits are deliberate. What this page does instead is one honest job: it keeps your budget, your guests, your tasks, and your days left in one place, saved on your device, ready whenever you open it.

Can I use this page on my phone?

Yes. The page is made to work on a phone as well as on a computer. The buttons are large, the lists are easy to scroll, and everything you type is saved on your phone as you go.

What happens if my wedding date changes?

Pick the new date in the countdown section and the number of days left updates straight away. Your budget, guest list and checklist stay exactly as they were, so nothing else needs to be typed again.

Does this page book venues or send invitations?

No. This page plans and counts: it tracks your budget, your guests, your tasks and your countdown. Booking a venue, ordering food and sending invitations are jobs for you, the venue and the caterer, and this page keeps the numbers for those jobs in order.
