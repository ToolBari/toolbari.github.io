What this planner does and who it is for

In a small team, leave is usually tracked by memory, by message, or by a notebook that only one person understands. That works until two people book the same week, until someone asks how many days they have left and nobody knows, or until the end of the year arrives and the records do not add up. The planner on this page replaces all of that with one list: the team, their yearly allowances, every leave record, and the overlaps that need attention.

Add each team member with their yearly leave allowance, then record leave as it is taken: who, what type, and which dates. The planner works out each balance — allowance minus days used — and flags any dates when two people are off at once. The whole team fits on one screen, and the summary copies out as plain text for the records.

It is built for small teams — a shop, a studio, a small office, a family business — where one person keeps the leave list and everyone else asks them how it stands. It is a planning tool, not a payroll or HR system: it does not approve leave, it does not pay anyone, and it does not connect to anything. It keeps the numbers straight so the conversations about leave can be short and fair.

How to use the planner, step by step

Start by adding the team. For each person, type their name and their yearly leave allowance in days — the number their contract or local law gives them for the year. The allowance is the starting point for every balance on the page, so set it carefully: a wrong allowance makes every balance wrong. If someone works part-time, set the allowance to the days they actually get, not the full-time number.

Then record leave as it happens. Choose the person, pick the leave type, and enter the start and end dates. The planner counts the days inclusively — a Monday to Friday record is five days — and subtracts them from the person's balance. Sick leave, parental leave and unpaid leave are recorded the same way, with the type shown beside each record so the pattern is visible.

Read the balances and the overlaps. A balance near zero means the person has little leave left; a negative balance means they have taken more than their allowance, which may be fine if it was agreed, but it should be a decision, not an accident. The overlaps section shows every pair of people off on the same dates — check cover for each one. Copy the summary regularly: the planner lives in this browser only, and the copy is your backup.

What the output means

The team balances list shows, for each person, the allowance, the days used, and the balance left. The balance is the number that matters most: it is what the person can still take this year. A balance that falls below zero is shown clearly, because it usually means a record was entered twice or an allowance was set too low — or that extra leave was agreed and should be noted somewhere the team can see it.

The leave records list shows every record in date order, with the person, the type, and the dates. It is the audit trail: if a balance looks wrong, the records show why. Delete a record if it was entered by mistake, and the balance corrects itself at once. Records are never locked and never need approval on this page — approval happens in your business, and the planner records the result.

The overlaps section is the early warning. Two people off at once may be fine — a quiet week, a small team with cover — or it may leave a role uncovered. The planner does not decide; it shows the clash and leaves the decision to you. The summary line counts the team, the records, and the overlaps, so a glance at the bottom of the tool tells you whether anything needs attention.

A worked example

Imagine a small shop with four people: Ayesha, Karim, Maria, and Tom. Each has a yearly allowance of 20 days. It is June, and the summer holidays are coming. Ayesha asks for 3 to 12 July, Karim asks for 8 to 15 July, Maria asks for 20 to 24 July, and Tom asks for 3 to 7 July. The owner adds all four records to the planner.

The balances update at once: Ayesha has used 10 days and has 10 left, Karim has used 8 and has 12 left, Maria has used 5 and has 15 left, Tom has used 5 and has 15 left. The overlaps section flags two clashes: Ayesha and Karim are both off from 8 to 12 July, and Ayesha and Tom are both off from 3 to 7 July. The shop cannot run with two people away in the same week, so the owner talks to the team: Karim moves his leave to the following week, and the clash clears.

By December the records tell the year's story at a glance: who took what, who still has days left, and whether anyone needs a reminder to use their remaining leave before the year ends. Nothing was kept in anyone's head, nothing was lost in a message thread, and the one page held the whole picture.

Tips for a good result

Record leave the day it is agreed, not the day it starts. A record made early is a record that prevents clashes; a record made late only explains them. Ask the team to give as much notice as they can, and make the planner the single place where leave is recorded — if some leave lives in messages and some in the planner, the balances cannot be trusted.

Set allowances from the contract and local law, not from habit. Part-time staff, new starters who joined mid-year, and anyone whose contract changed all need their own number. When in doubt, check the contract and check local law — the planner does the arithmetic, but the allowance is a decision only a person can make.

Review the planner once a month and once more before the busy seasons. Read the overlaps, check the low balances, and remind anyone with many days left to plan their time off. Copy the summary after each review and keep it with the business records. And when the year turns over, clear the old records and set the new allowances — a fresh year deserves a fresh list.

Finally, treat the planner as the team's shared memory, not as a manager's private list. Let everyone see the balances and the overlaps — transparency removes most arguments about leave before they start. When people can see the picture for themselves, they plan around each other instead of competing for the same weeks.

Limits of the planner — what it cannot do

This planner tracks numbers; it does not approve leave, enforce rules, or replace the official record. Approval is a conversation in your business, and the planner records the result of that conversation. It does not know whether a request should be approved, whether a clash matters, or whether a balance is fair — those are decisions for the people involved.

It does not connect to payroll, to any HR system, or to any calendar. It does not send reminders, and it cannot tell anyone that their leave is coming up. It counts calendar days inclusively and does not know about weekends, public holidays, or half days unless you record them in a way your team agrees on. Keep the official record wherever your business keeps its records, and treat this planner as the working list it is.

Leave rules differ by country and by contract: how much leave people get, what happens to unused days, and what counts as sick or parental leave are all set by law and contract, not by this page. Check local law before you set allowances, and get advice for anything that affects pay or employment. The planner does the arithmetic; the rules are yours to get right.

Questions people ask

What types of leave should a small team track?

Track annual leave first, then sick, parental and unpaid leave. Recording the type beside each record shows the pattern over the year and keeps the balances honest.

How do I work out a leave balance?

Balance is the yearly allowance minus the days used. The planner counts each record inclusively — Monday to Friday is five days — and updates the balance as soon as a record is added or deleted.

Can I see when two people are off at once?

Yes. The overlaps section lists every pair of people whose leave dates cross, with the shared dates, so you can check cover before the week arrives.

Does this connect to payroll?

No. This is a planning tool only. It does not connect to payroll, to any HR system, or to any calendar, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Can I print the leave calendar?

Yes. Tap the copy button to copy the whole summary as plain text, paste it into any document or note, and print it from there.

What leave rules should I check in local law?

How much leave people are entitled to, what happens to unused days, and how sick and parental leave work. These differ by country and by contract, so check before you set allowances.
