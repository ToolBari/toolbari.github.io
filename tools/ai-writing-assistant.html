What this writing assistant does

This page is a writing helper that reworks text you paste in. Pick one of five tasks — rewrite, make shorter, make formal, make simple, or fix tone — and the helper applies clear written rules to your words: it swaps common words for stronger ones, cuts filler, expands contractions, replaces hard words with plain ones, or softens harsh wording. The result appears in the second box, ready to copy.

The important thing to know is how it works. This is a rule-based helper, not a full AI model. It does not understand your sentences the way a person does. It matches words and phrases against a list of rules and changes them, one at a time. That makes it fast, private and predictable: the same text always gives the same result, and nothing you type ever leaves your browser. It also means the helper has limits, and this page states them plainly instead of hiding them.

Think of it as a first-pass editor. It is good at the mechanical jobs — trimming a long paragraph, making a casual message sound right for work, turning stiff formal wording into plain English. It is not good at the thinking jobs: choosing what to say, checking whether something is true, or writing in your voice. Those are still yours, and the helper works best when you treat its output as a draft to read and fix, not as a finished answer.

How to use it

Using the helper takes less than a minute. First, paste or type your text into the top box. A paragraph works best — one to ten sentences. You can paste more, but very long text is harder to check, and checking matters, so work through a long piece one paragraph at a time.

Second, pick a task from the list. Rewrite gives you fresh wording for the same ideas. Make shorter cuts filler words and long phrases. Make formal expands contractions and swaps casual words for formal ones. Make simple replaces hard words with plain ones. Fix tone softens harsh words and calms down shouting and repeated exclamation marks.

Third, tap the button and read the result in the lower box. Then copy it with the copy button, or keep editing it by hand — the lower box is yours to change. On a phone, the whole flow works by touch: the boxes are wide, the buttons are big, and nothing needs a keyboard shortcut. On a desktop, the same page works with a mouse and keyboard, and pasting with the usual shortcut works in both boxes.

The five tasks, explained

Each task does one job, and knowing which one to pick saves you time. Here they are, with a short before-and-after example for each.

  • Rewrite. Swaps common words for stronger ones and tightens a few long phrases. Before: "The task is very important." After: "The task is extremely significant." Use it when a sentence feels flat and you want fresh wording without changing the meaning.
  • Make shorter. Removes filler words like very, really, just and actually, and replaces long phrases with short ones. Before: "I came here in order to help you with your work." After: "I came here to help you with your work." Use it when a paragraph rambles and you want the point to land faster.
  • Make formal. Expands contractions and swaps casual words for formal ones. Before: "Don't forget to tell the kids, thanks!" After: "Do not forget to tell the children, thank you!" Use it for job applications, school work and messages to people you do not know well.
  • Make simple. Replaces hard words with plain ones. Before: "Please utilise this tool to commence your work." After: "Please use this tool to start your work." Use it when your reader may not know formal English, or when you want a message anyone can read quickly.
  • Fix tone. Softens harsh words, calms shouting and trims repeated exclamation marks. Before: "This idea is STUPID and terrible!!!" After: "This idea is unwise and disappointing!" Use it before you send a message written in anger — it will not fix the argument, but it will stop the words making it worse.

You can run the tasks one after another. A common chain is Make shorter, then Rewrite, then a final read by hand. Each pass changes the text a little, so read the result after each step instead of running all five at once and hoping for the best.

What it cannot do

This helper does not understand meaning. It cannot tell whether your argument makes sense, whether your facts are right, or whether your tone fits your reader in a deeper way than word choice. It will happily rewrite a wrong sentence into a smoother wrong sentence. It cannot write new ideas for you, and it cannot check grammar beyond the small mechanical fixes its rules happen to make. If you need a full AI writer that drafts, reasons and rewrites at a deeper level, the free AI writers ChatGPT and Claude are the well-known options — both offer free use in the browser, and both ask you to make a free account.

It also cannot fix a sentence that has no clear meaning to begin with. If you paste a confused paragraph, you will get a shorter or more formal confused paragraph. The fix for that is not a tool — it is deciding what you want to say, in one plain sentence, and then writing it out. The helper can polish that sentence. It cannot write it for you.

Finally, it cannot make your writing sound like you. Rule-based rewriting pushes every sentence toward the same middle ground: correct, plain, a little generic. That is fine for a first draft. But the sentences people remember have a voice in them — your example, your phrasing, the detail only you know. Put those back in after the helper has done its pass.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is pasting a whole essay and expecting magic. A rule-based helper works one word at a time. On a long text it makes hundreds of small changes, and you cannot check them all, so errors slip through — a swapped word that changes the meaning, a cut filler that was doing real work, a formal phrase that sounds stiff. Work one paragraph at a time and read each result.

The second mistake is accepting every suggestion. The helper's version is not better by default — it is just different. Sometimes the original word was the right one. If a change sounds wrong, put the original back. You are the editor; the helper is the assistant.

The third mistake is using the wrong task for the job. Making a message formal does not make it polite — tone is a different job, and there is a task for it. Making text shorter does not make it clearer — sometimes the long version was clear and the short version is abrupt. Match the task to the problem you actually have.

The fourth mistake is forgetting the reader. A formal rewrite for a friend sounds cold. A simplified rewrite for a professor sounds careless. Before you pick a task, picture the person who will read the result, and choose the version they would want to receive.

Writing better on your own

Tools help, but the habits underneath them matter more. Read your sentences aloud before you send them — your ear catches what your eye misses. Cut one word in three from any first draft; most sentences survive, and the ones that do not were carrying filler. Use short sentences for important points and longer ones for explanation. Put the main point first, then the detail, so a busy reader gets the message even if they stop halfway.

Keep a small list of words you overuse. Most people have three or four — very, really, just, actually, basically — and seeing them on a list makes them easier to cut. Write the way you speak when you are explaining something to a friend: plain, direct, one idea at a time. Then use this helper for the pass you would rather not do by hand, and finish with one careful read of your own. That combination — your thinking, the helper's speed, your final read — is what good writing looks like.

Questions people ask

Is this a real AI model?

No. This helper reworks your text with clear written rules — word swaps, filler removal and tone fixes — and it runs entirely in your browser. It is honest about that. For deep rewriting by a full AI model, the guide on this page links to free AI writers you can use.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. Paste or type your text, pick a task and tap the button. The buttons are big, the text boxes are wide, and nothing needs installing.

Can it fix my grammar?

It can fix a few common things, like double spaces and some repeated words, when you use the rewrite and tone tasks. It is not a grammar checker, though — it does not understand your sentences, so read the result once before you use it.

Will it rewrite a whole essay?

It will rework whatever text you paste, long or short, but pasting a whole essay and expecting a perfect rewrite is asking too much of a rule-based helper. Work one paragraph at a time, and keep your own ideas in charge.

Is my text sent anywhere?

No. Everything happens in your browser on your own device. Your words are never uploaded, stored or sent to a server by this page.

Which task should I use first?

If your text feels long and rambling, start with Make shorter. If it feels too casual for its reader, try Make formal. If you are stuck on wording, Rewrite gives you a fresh version to work from.
