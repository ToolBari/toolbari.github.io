What this builder does

This builder makes checklists that save. You create a checklist, add items to it, tick them off with large checkboxes as you finish them, and the list is still there when you come back tomorrow. You can keep several checklists at once — one for packing, one for shopping, one for moving house — and switch between them. Items can be moved up and down to set the order, deleted when they no longer matter, and the progress bar shows how much of the list is done.

Four starter templates are built in: packing, shopping, moving and daily routine. Each one is a sensible starting list that you can use as it is or change to fit your own trip, shop, move or day. Starting from a template is faster than starting from a blank page, and it reminds you of the items people most often forget — the phone charger, the toilet paper, the first-night bag.

Everything is saved in your browser, on your device. There is no account, no sign-up and no cloud: the lists live in your browser's storage and nowhere else. The export button downloads a checklist as a plain text file, the copy button copies it as text, and the print button prints it. Those three buttons are how a list moves from this device to anywhere else.

How to use it on your phone

Start by picking a template or creating a new checklist. Tap one of the four template buttons to load a ready-made list, or type a name in the new checklist box and tap Create checklist. The checklist selector at the top lists all your checklists; tap it to switch between them. The delete button removes the checklist you are looking at, so use it carefully.

To add an item, type it in the add-item box and tap Add item. Items appear in the list with a large checkbox on the left. Tap the checkbox to tick an item off — the text is crossed out and the progress bar moves. Tap it again to untick it. The small arrow buttons move an item up or down, and the delete button removes it. The order you set is the order the list keeps.

The progress bar and the line above the list show how many items are done out of the total, as a count and a percentage. When everything is ticked off, the bar is full. The download button saves the checklist as a text file with ticked and unticked items marked, the copy button copies the same text to your clipboard, and the print button opens your browser's print view. The page works by touch alone, with large tap targets throughout.

How to write a checklist that actually gets used

A good checklist is short, specific and ordered. Short means ten to twenty items at most: a list of fifty items is not a checklist, it is a project plan, and nobody finishes it. If your list keeps growing past twenty items, split it into two lists — "before the trip" and "packing day", for example — and finish one before starting the other.

Specific means each item names one concrete thing to do. "Pack clothes" is vague; "pack 3 shirts, 2 pairs of trousers, pyjamas" is specific, and you know when it is done. "Sort out the kitchen" is vague; "empty the fridge, clean the oven, pack the kettle last" is a list you can actually work through. The test for an item is simple: could someone else tell whether it was done? If not, rewrite it.

Ordered means the items run in the order you will do them. Put the first thing first and the last thing last, and put the thing you always forget near the top where you will see it. Use the up and down buttons to fix the order as you go: a list in the right order feels like a path, while a list in random order feels like a pile. And tick things off as you finish them, not at the end: the progress bar is a small reward each time, and small rewards keep you going.

Worked templates: packing, shopping, moving

The packing template covers the twelve things people most often forget: passport and tickets, phone charger, toiletries bag, toothbrush, a change of clothes, comfortable shoes, a jacket or raincoat, medicines, sunglasses, a book or headphones, a travel adapter and a water bottle. Add your own items for the trip — swimwear for a beach holiday, warm layers for a cold one — and tick things off as they go into the bag, not before.

The shopping template is a weekly shop in ten items: bread, milk, eggs, rice, vegetables, fruit, cooking oil, tea or coffee, soap and toilet paper. It is deliberately short: a shopping list works best when it holds only what you need this week, and the items are ordered roughly as you walk around a shop. Add the things your household needs and delete the ones it does not, and the list becomes yours.

The moving template is twelve items in the order a move actually happens: pack boxes and tape, label every box, cancel or move the utilities, change your address with the bank, clean the old home, book a moving van, pack a first-night bag, charge your phone, keep keys and documents together, take photos of the old home, check every room twice, and hand over the keys. The first-night bag matters most: it holds what you need on the first night in the new home, so you are not opening ten boxes to find a toothbrush.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is the fifty-item list. A list that long is demoralising before you start, and the important items hide among the trivial ones. Keep lists short, and if a job is genuinely big, split it into two or three lists with clear names. The second mistake is vague items: "get organised", "sort things out" and "prepare" are not items, because nobody can tell when they are done. Rewrite every vague item into a concrete action.

The third mistake is no order. A list in random order forces you to read the whole list every time to find the next thing to do, and the urgent items get done last by accident. Put the list in the order you will do it, and move items as plans change. The fourth mistake is never ticking things off: a list you do not update becomes stale within days, and a stale list is worse than no list, because you stop trusting it. Tick items off as you finish them, and delete items that no longer matter.

The fifth mistake is keeping the list only in your head alongside the written one. If an item is not on the list, it does not exist as far as the list is concerned, and it will be forgotten at the worst moment. When you think of something, add it straight away, even if it is small. The list is your memory: trust it by keeping it complete.

Keeping your checklists safe

Your checklists live in your browser's storage on this device. That is private — nobody else can see them, and nothing is sent anywhere — but it also means they do not follow you to another phone or computer, and they disappear if you clear your browser data or uninstall the browser. The export and copy buttons exist for exactly this reason: download a text copy of a list you care about, or copy it into a note, and it survives whatever happens to the browser.

A good habit is to export a list when you finish it, or when it holds something you will want again: a packing list that worked well is worth keeping for the next trip, and a moving list is worth keeping for the next move. The exported text marks ticked items, so the copy shows what was done and what was not. Print is useful too: a printed list on the fridge works when the phone is charging, and a pen ticks boxes just as well as a finger.

If you share a device with family, remember that anyone using the same browser can see the checklists. They are not locked or password-protected, because this builder has no accounts at all. Do not put passwords, account numbers or anything private in a checklist; a checklist is for tasks, and private information belongs somewhere safer.

What this builder cannot do

This builder cannot sync your lists to another device, remind you at a set time, or share a list with someone else in real time. It has no notifications, no calendar and no accounts, by design: it is a simple list keeper, not a task management service. If you need reminders, set one on your phone's clock or calendar app and put the list's name in it.

It also cannot recover a deleted list. Deleting a checklist removes it immediately, and there is no undo and no recycle bin, so export a copy first if the list matters. And it cannot hold very large lists well: a list of hundreds of items will slow the page down and is almost certainly a list that should have been split into smaller ones. Keep lists short, export the ones that matter, and let the builder do the simple job it is made for.

Questions people ask

Where are my checklists saved?

In your browser, on this device only. They are stored in the browser's local storage, which keeps them between visits on the same phone or computer. Nothing is sent to a server and nothing is synced anywhere, so your lists are private but tied to this device.

Can I use this on more than one device?

Not directly: a list saved on your phone does not appear on your computer. To move a list, use the download button to save it as a text file, or the copy button to copy it as text, and send the file or text to your other device. You can then re-create the list there, or just use the text copy.

How do I print my checklist?

Tap the Print button, which opens your browser's print view with the current checklist. You can print it on paper or save it as a PDF, depending on your device. A printed list works well on a fridge or in a bag when your phone is charging.

Can I share a checklist with someone?

Yes, as text. The copy button copies the whole checklist, with ticked and unticked items marked, so you can paste it into a message or an email. The download button saves the same text as a file you can send. The other person gets a copy, not a live shared list.

How many items should a checklist have?

Ten to twenty is a good size. Shorter lists get finished; lists of fifty items feel endless and the important items hide among the trivial ones. If a job is genuinely big, split it into two or three lists with clear names, such as before the trip and packing day.

Why do my checklists never get finished?

Usually because the items are vague, the list is too long, or nothing gets ticked off as you go. Rewrite vague items into concrete actions, keep the list short, put the items in the order you will do them, and tick each one off the moment it is done. The progress bar is a small reward each time.
