What this tool does and who it is for

A healthcare credential tracker is a list of the licences, certificates and registrations that staff hold, with the date each one expires. You add a staff member, pick the credential type, and enter the expiry date. The page counts the days left, marks each credential as ok, expiring soon or expired, and sorts the list so the nearest expiry is first.

This page is made for small clinics, dental practices, care homes and any small healthcare team that needs to know whose licence renews next. It is also useful for a practice manager who has just taken over and needs to build a list of what the team holds and when each credential runs out.

The tracker covers the credentials a small healthcare team usually holds: a professional licence, a registration, a first aid certificate, a basic life support certificate, a specialist certificate, and a background check. Each entry is one credential for one person, so a staff member with three credentials gets three entries.

Everything stays in your browser. There is no account, no server, and no one else who can see your list. The copy button turns the renewal list into plain text you can paste into a document, save in a note, or print for a staff meeting. The list is there when you come back, because it is saved on your device.

How to use it, step by step

Start by adding your first credential. Type the staff member's name, pick the credential type, and enter the expiry date from the certificate itself, not from memory. Press Add credential, and the entry appears in your list with the days left and a status: ok, expiring soon, or expired.

Work through the list from the top. It is sorted by expiry date, nearest first, so the renewal that needs starting soonest is always first. When a credential is renewed, remove the old entry and add a fresh one with the new expiry date, so the list always reflects the real dates.

Use the copy button to take the renewal list with you. It writes out every credential, its holder, its expiry date and the days left, as plain text. Paste it into a document for a staff meeting, or print it and pin it up in the office. The clear button empties the list, so only use it when you are ready to start fresh.

  • Add one entry per credential, so a person with three credentials gets three entries
  • Start a renewal as soon as a credential shows expiring soon, because renewals take time
  • Check the list at the start of each month, and more often if renewals are near

What the output means

The days left is the count from today to the expiry date. A positive number means the credential is still valid. Zero means it expires today. A negative number means it has expired, and the person should not work under it until it is renewed. The status follows from that count: expiring soon means the expiry falls within the next 60 days.

Sixty days is a practical warning window, not a rule. Many renewals need forms, fees, training hours or a check that takes weeks to come back, so a credential that expires in 60 days is a credential to start renewing now. If your regulator needs longer, start earlier; the page shows the days, and you decide when to act.

The order of the list is the most practical part. Because the nearest expiry sits at the top, a practice manager can see in seconds whose renewal to start next, without opening a spreadsheet or a filing cabinet. That is the whole point of the tool: not the maths, but the order it puts your credentials in.

Nothing on this page checks whether a credential is real. The tracker holds the dates you enter and counts the days; it never contacts a register, never verifies a licence, and never proves anything about a person's qualifications. Verification is a separate job, done with the official register or regulator.

A worked example

Take a small clinic with three staff members. Ana holds a professional licence expiring on 10 December and a first aid certificate expiring on 5 February. Ben holds a registration expiring on 20 November. Carla holds a specialist certificate expiring on 15 March next year. Each credential goes into the tracker as its own entry, so there are four entries in total.

Suppose today is 1 October. Ben's registration has 50 days left, so it shows expiring soon: the renewal should start now. Ana's licence has 70 days left, so it is still ok, but it will be expiring soon within days. Her first aid certificate has 127 days left, and Carla's certificate has months left, so both can wait.

The list sorts itself by expiry date, so Ben sits at the top, then Ana's licence, then her first aid certificate, then Carla. The practice manager can see at a glance that Ben's renewal is the job for this week, and Ana's licence is the job for next week.

Now suppose Ben renews, and his new registration expires on 20 November next year. The old entry is removed and a fresh one is added with the new date. The list re-sorts, and Ana's licence moves to the top. The record stays honest because it follows the real expiry dates.

Tips for a good result

Start renewals early. A renewal that needs a training course, a background check or a regulator's approval can take weeks or months. The 60-day warning is a minimum; for anything that involves a third party, start at 90 days or more. A renewal started early can wait; a renewal started late cannot be hurried.

Keep the certificate with the record. The tracker holds the date, but the certificate is the proof. Keep the original or a certified copy in the staff file, and note where it is kept. If a regulator asks, the date in a browser is not proof; the certificate is.

Check credentials when someone joins, not only when they expire. A new starter's licence should be checked with the official register on day one, and the expiry date entered in the tracker at the same time. That way the first renewal is already on the list.

Keep a note of how each renewal works, not just when it is due. Some renewals need a form and a fee, some need training hours, and some need a check that takes weeks to come back. A short note per credential type, kept in your own files, means the next renewal starts with a plan instead of a puzzle. When a renewal is done, update the note with anything you learned, so the next person to do it starts ahead.

Agree who owns the list. A tracker that belongs to everyone is checked by no one. Give one person the job of checking the list each month and starting the renewals, and give a second person the job of checking when the first is away. Two named people are enough; a whole team with no named owner is not.

  • Use the full name on the certificate, so entries match the paperwork
  • Note the renewal steps for each credential type in your own files, so the next renewal is not a fresh puzzle
  • Keep a printed copy of the renewal list in the office, where the manager can see it
  • Review the whole list once a quarter, and remove staff who have left

Limits of the tool — what it cannot do

This tracker cannot verify a licence or certificate. It never contacts a register, a regulator or a training body, and it cannot tell you whether a credential is real, current or valid. It counts days from the expiry dates you enter, and it trusts those dates completely.

It cannot remind you by itself. The page shows the list when you open it, and it does not send messages, emails or alerts. If nobody opens the page, nobody sees the warning. Build the monthly check into a routine, and do not rely on the tool to chase you.

It cannot keep your records safe for you. Your list lives in this browser only, which means it is lost if the browser data is cleared or you switch devices. Use the copy button regularly and keep the text somewhere safe. For official records, keep the certificates in the staff files as well.

This tracker does NOT verify any licence or certificate against a real register: it only reminds you of the expiry dates you enter. Always check credentials with the official register or regulator, and follow your local rules. This page is a date tracker, not legal or medical advice.

Questions people ask

What credentials should a clinic track for each staff member?

Track the professional licence or registration, any specialist certificates, first aid and basic life support certificates, and any background check with an expiry date. Each credential is its own entry, so a staff member with three credentials gets three entries, each with its own expiry date.

How far ahead should I start a renewal?

Start as soon as a credential shows expiring soon, which this page marks at 60 days before expiry. Many renewals need forms, fees, training hours or a check that takes weeks, so 60 days is a minimum. For anything that involves a regulator or a third party, start at 90 days or more.

Can I track more than one person?

Yes. Add as many people and as many credentials as you need, one entry at a time. The list sorts itself by expiry date, nearest first, so the renewal due soonest is always at the top, whoever it belongs to.

Does this check a real licence register?

No. This tracker does not verify any licence or certificate against a real register. It only reminds you of the expiry dates you enter. Always check credentials with the official register or regulator, especially when someone joins the team.

Can I print the renewal list?

Yes. Press the copy button to turn the list into plain text, then paste it into any document or note and print it from there. The copied text includes every credential, its holder, its expiry date and the days left, so the printed page is a full renewal list.

What happens if a licence expires?

The person should not work under that licence until it is renewed. An expired licence can mean a person cannot legally do their job, and the clinic can be in trouble for letting them. If a licence has expired, stop the affected work, start the renewal at once, and check your local rules for what else is needed.
