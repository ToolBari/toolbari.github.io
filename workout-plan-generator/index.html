What each goal's week actually looks like

The generator is honest and simple: it does not invent a new plan on every click. Each goal has one fixed, sensible week, written for home training:

  • General fitness: two 20-minute walks plus stretching, two bodyweight-basics days, and built-in rest days.
  • Strength: push-ups, squats and planks on Monday; squats, lunges and planks on Wednesday; a full-body bodyweight day on Friday — rest between sessions.
  • Cardio: brisk walks of 20, 20 and 30 minutes across the week, with rest days between them.

Pick Intermediate and the same week gains one extra instruction: increase time or repetitions gradually. That is the whole difference — the page and the tool both say so plainly.

Please read: this is general information only, not medical advice. Stop if you feel pain. If you have a health condition, an injury, are pregnant, or have not exercised in a long time, talk to a doctor before starting any new exercise routine.

Questions people ask

What goals can the Workout Plan Generator build for?

Three goals: General fitness, Strength and Cardio. For each goal you also choose a level — Beginner or Intermediate — and the generator returns a fixed 7-day week built for that combination.

Do I need gym equipment for these plans?

No. Every plan the generator produces is bodyweight and walking based — push-ups, squats, lunges, planks, brisk walks and stretching. There is no equipment, gym or weights version in the tool.

What changes if I pick Intermediate instead of Beginner?

The 7 days stay the same; the plan adds one instruction: increase time or repetitions gradually. Beginner plans carry no such note.

Is this workout plan medical advice?

No. It is general information only, not medical advice. Stop if you feel pain, and talk to a doctor before starting if you have a health condition, an injury, or you are pregnant.
