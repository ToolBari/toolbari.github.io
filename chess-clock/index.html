The clock measures real elapsed time, so switching tabs does not pause it. Reloading or closing the page ends the game.

How a chess clock works

A chess clock is two countdown clocks joined together: only one runs at a time. The player whose turn it is has the running clock, and pressing the button stops their clock and starts the opponent's. The increment, often called Fischer increment, adds a few seconds to your clock after each move you make, so a player who moves quickly can build time back.

When a clock reaches zero, the game ends on time and the page shows a flag-fall alert naming the player who ran out of time. The names, base time and increment are set before the game starts, and the Start button becomes a Pause button while a game is running.

Setting a game

Enter the two names, then the base time in minutes: 10 minutes each is a common friendly game, 5 minutes is a fast game, and longer times suit slower play. The increment is in seconds per move and can be 0 for no increment. Press Start and Player 1's clock runs first.

Tap the big button of the player who just moved to switch the clocks. The increment is added to the clock of the player who moved, then the other player's clock starts running. Reset returns both clocks to the base time so a new game can begin. The clock shows minutes and seconds for each player, so 9 minutes 30 seconds appears as 9:30 and a single minute appears as 1:00.

Questions people ask

What does the increment do?

After each move, the increment seconds are added to the clock of the player who just moved.

What happens when a clock reaches zero?

The game stops and a flag-fall message names the player who ran out of time.

Can I pause the game?

Yes. The Start button becomes a Pause button while a game runs. Press it again to resume.

What if I tap the wrong clock?

Tapping your own clock ends your turn. If you tapped by mistake, tap the other clock to switch the turn back; the increment stays added.
