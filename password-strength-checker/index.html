How to make yours stronger

  • Go long: 12 characters or more — length beats clever substitutions.
  • Mix four pools: lowercase, UPPERCASE, digits and symbols (!@#$%).
  • Skip the patterns: no 1234, abcd, qwerty, repeated characters, names or years.
  • Never reuse: one password per site, so one leak cannot unlock everything.

Questions people ask

How does the password strength checker judge my password?

It estimates entropy: the password's length multiplied by the size of its character pool (lowercase, uppercase, digits and symbols). It then lowers the score for patterns attackers try first — common passwords such as "password" score just 8 bits, repeated characters and sequences like 1234 or abcd are penalised too.

Is my password sent to a server or stored?

No. The check runs entirely in your browser with JavaScript on this page — the password you type is never sent over the network and never stored. You can even load the page and disconnect from the internet before typing.

How long should a password be?

At least 12 characters — length matters more than anything else. A 12-character mix of letters, numbers and symbols reaches roughly 79 bits of entropy, which this checker rates Strong; the word "password" rates Very weak at 8 bits because attackers try it first.

What makes a password strong?

Length (12+ characters), a mix of lowercase, uppercase, digits and symbols, no dictionary words or personal details, no sequences or repeats — and a different password on every site. A password generator creates one instantly and removes the guesswork.
