What the one-tap filters set

Each filter button simply moves the six sliders to a fixed recipe — you can fine-tune them afterwards:

  • General: back to neutral (100 / 100 / 100, no effects).
  • Vintage: sepia 60%, softer contrast, faded colour.
  • Black & White: full grayscale, contrast lifted.
  • Bright: brightness 112%, a touch of sepia, richer colour.
  • Vivid Colors: saturation 165%, brighter contrast.

What this editor honestly does not do

No crop, no resize, no text or stickers — only the edits listed above. Need an exact size for a passport or form? Use the photo resizing guide. Smaller file? The image compressor. Different format? The image converter (JPG, PNG, WebP).

Questions people ask

What edits can the ToolBari Photo Editor actually do?

Six sliders — brightness (40–160%), contrast (40–160%), saturation (0–220%), black & white (0–100%), vintage sepia (0–100%) and blur (0–5px) — plus five one-tap filters (General, Vintage, Black & White, Bright, Vivid Colors), rotate 90°, horizontal flip and reset. The edited photo downloads as a JPG.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The editor draws your photo onto a canvas inside your own browser and applies the edits there. Nothing is uploaded, sent or stored — the download is generated on your device.

Can this editor crop or resize a photo?

No — it adjusts, filters, rotates and flips, but it does not crop or change dimensions. For exact sizes, use ToolBari's passport and form photo resizing guide; to shrink the file size, use the image compressor.

What format does the edited photo download in?

A single JPG file named edited-photo.jpg, saved at quality 0.92 — a good balance of looks and file size for sharing and uploading to forms.
