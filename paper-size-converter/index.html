The A-series sizes

The A series starts with A0, a sheet of one square metre, and each smaller size is made by cutting the previous one in half across its longer side. That is why A4 is half an A3 sheet, and why every A size keeps the same shape no matter how big or small it is.

A4 is the everyday size for letters and documents in most of the world, while A3 is often used for posters and drawings and A5 for booklets and small notebooks. A6 is the size of a postcard.

Millimetres, inches and pixels

Millimetres and inches are just two ways of measuring the same sheet: one inch is exactly 25.4 millimetres. Pixels are different, because they depend on how many dots per inch the image is set to. The same A4 sheet is 2480 by 3508 pixels at 300 dots per inch, but only 595 by 842 pixels at 72.

When a printer or a print shop asks for a file at a certain DPI, they are asking how densely the pixels should be packed. Higher DPI means more pixels for the same sheet, which is why print files are much larger than screen images of the same page.

Questions people ask

What size is A4 paper?

A4 is 210 by 297 millimetres, or 8.27 by 11.69 inches. At 300 dots per inch it is 2480 by 3508 pixels.

How do the A sizes relate to each other?

Each A size is half the area of the one above it: fold an A3 sheet in half and you get two A4 sheets, and fold an A4 sheet to get A5.

What DPI should I use for printing?

The converter works with any DPI you enter. Print work is often set up at 300 dots per inch, but your printer or print shop may ask for a different value.

What are Letter, Legal and Tabloid?

They are North American paper sizes. Letter is 8.5 by 11 inches, Legal is 8.5 by 14 inches, and Tabloid is 11 by 17 inches.
