Keyword Density Checker

Paste any text to see its total word count and a table of the most frequent single words and two-word phrases, each with its count and density percentage. You can choose to leave common stop words out of the table.

Keyword density alone does not rank a page, and stuffing keywords into a text hurts readability. Treat this table as a check on what your text says, not as a target to chase.

How density is worked out

The tool counts every word in your text, ignoring case, so Garden, garden and GARDEN are counted as the same word. Density is then worked out as: density = (occurrences / total words) x 100, shown to 2 decimal places. The tables show the top 10 single words and the top 10 two-word phrases, ranked by count, with ties broken alphabetically.

The stop-word checkbox excludes these common words from the tables: the, a, an, and, or, of, to, in, on, for, with, is, are, was, were, be, been, it, this, that. When stop words are excluded, two-word phrases containing a stop word are excluded too. The total word count always counts every word, stop words included, and the result says plainly whether stop words are excluded.

Worked example: a text of 200 words in which the word "garden" appears 10 times gives "garden" a count of 10 and a density of 5.00%. Load the worked example above to see the tool reproduce it.

How to use the table

Read the tables as a mirror of your text. The words at the top are the ones your text is really about, and the two-word phrases show the ideas that keep coming back. If a word you expected to see is missing, or an unimportant word dominates the table, that is a sign to look again at what you wrote. This tool analyses text you paste, which is different from generating keyword ideas.

Questions people ask

What is keyword density?

Keyword density is how often a word or phrase appears in a text, shown as a percentage of the total word count. A word that appears 10 times in 200 words has a density of 5.00%.

Does this tool count words case-sensitively?

No. The tool ignores case, so Garden, garden and GARDEN are counted as the same word.

What are stop words, and why exclude them?

Stop words are common words such as the, and, of and to. Excluding them keeps the table focused on the words that carry the meaning of your text.

Will a high keyword density help my page rank?

No. Keyword density alone does not rank a page, and stuffing keywords into a text hurts readability. Use the table to check what your text is really about, not to chase a target number.
