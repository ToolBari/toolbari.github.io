Moon Phase Calendar

Pick any date to see the Moon's phase name, how much of the Moon is lit, and the Moon's age in days since the last new moon. The tool also shows the dates of the next full moon and the next new moon.

The phase is calculated for noon UTC (12:00) on the date you pick.

This tool uses a mean-cycle approximation: the Moon's real cycle varies slightly, so exact phase instants can differ by a few hours from almanac values. It is not built for eclipse prediction.

How the moon phase is worked out

The tool uses the mean synodic model. It counts the days since a known new moon — 6 January 2000 at 18:14 UTC — and divides by the average length of a lunar cycle, 29.530588853 days. The remainder is the Moon's age in days. Dividing the age by the cycle length gives the phase as a fraction of the cycle, from 0 at new moon to 1 just before the next new moon.

Illumination comes from the formula (1 − cos(2 × π × phase)) / 2, so it is 0 at new moon, half at the quarters and 1 at full moon. The phase name comes from eight equal buckets of the cycle, each one eighth wide, with New Moon centred on phase 0. The Moon drawing above is built from the same numbers: the shape of the lit part follows the phase, with the right side lit while the Moon is waxing and the left side lit while it is waning.

The eight phases of the Moon

The Moon passes through eight named phases in every cycle:

  • New Moon — the Moon sits between Earth and the Sun, so the side facing us is dark.
  • Waxing Crescent — a thin sliver of light appears on the right and grows night by night.
  • First Quarter — half of the Moon's face is lit, on the right.
  • Waxing Gibbous — more than half lit, still growing toward full.
  • Full Moon — the whole face we see is lit.
  • Waning Gibbous — more than half lit, shrinking after full.
  • Last Quarter — half lit, on the left.
  • Waning Crescent — a thin sliver on the left, fading toward the next new moon.

Reading the result

The Moon's age tells you how far through the cycle the date falls: 0 days at new moon, about 14.8 days at full moon, and about 29.5 days just before the next new moon. The illuminated percentage is the share of the Moon's face that appears lit from Earth. The next full moon and next new moon dates are counted forward from your chosen date using the same mean cycle.

Questions people ask

How accurate is this moon phase calendar?

It uses a mean-cycle model, so it is accurate to within a few hours of almanac values for most dates. The real lunar cycle varies slightly in length, so exact phase instants can differ by a few hours. It is good for planning and curiosity, not for eclipse prediction.

What does the Moon's age mean?

The Moon's age is the number of days since the last new moon. It is 0 at new moon, about 7.4 days at first quarter, about 14.8 days at full moon, and about 22.1 days at last quarter, before the cycle ends at about 29.5 days.

Why does the Moon look different every night?

The Sun always lights half of the Moon. As the Moon orbits Earth, the angle between the Sun, the Moon and us changes, so we see a different amount of the lit half. That changing view is what we call the phases of the Moon.

What is the synodic month?

The synodic month is the average time from one new moon to the next, 29.530588853 days. This tool counts days from a known new moon on 6 January 2000 at 18:14 UTC and divides by that average to find the phase for any date.
