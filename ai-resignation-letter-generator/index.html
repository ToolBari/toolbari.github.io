What a resignation letter should include

A resignation letter needs only a few things: a clear statement that you are resigning, your role, your last working day, and a polite closing. A short reason helps but is not required, and you do not need to explain your plans in detail. Keep the tone polite even if you are leaving unhappy, because the letter stays on your record. Offer a handover in one line, thank the reader, and stop there. Short letters are easier to read and harder to regret.

Choosing a tone

Formal wording is the safe choice for most jobs: it is clear, respectful, and leaves no room for misunderstanding. A warm tone fits when you genuinely liked the job and want to say so. A brief tone works when the relationship is plain and the facts are all that matter. Whatever tone you pick, keep it steady and avoid jokes, complaints, or promises you cannot keep. The letter is a record, not a conversation.

Before you send it

Check your employment contract for the notice period and any rules about how to resign. Make sure your last working day matches the notice you owe, and confirm the date with your manager if you are unsure. Keep a copy of the letter for yourself, and note when and how you sent it. If anything in your situation is unusual, such as a dispute or a fixed-term contract, get advice before sending rather than guessing. A careful resignation protects your reference and your final pay.
