Questions people ask

What is BMI?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a screening number from your weight and height: weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. It sorts adults into broad weight ranges, but it does not measure body fat directly.

What are the standard BMI ranges?

The World Health Organization (WHO) adult classification: below 18.5 is underweight, 18.5–24.9 is the normal range, 25.0–29.9 is overweight, and 30.0 or above is obese.

Is a BMI result a medical diagnosis?

No. BMI is a general information and screening measure only. It can mislead for children, pregnant women, older people and very muscular people. For anything about your own health, talk to a doctor.
