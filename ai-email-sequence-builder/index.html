How to use this builder

Pick the goal of the sequence, choose how many emails you want, and say who they are for. Press the button and the tool builds an outline with one block per email: a subject line, a purpose, and three key points.

Use the outline as your writing plan. Write each email from its key points, in your own voice, and keep each email to one idea. When the plan looks right, copy it into your notes or your email tool.

Why sequences work in this order

A good sequence follows a simple arc: welcome, value, trust, ask, reminder. The first email introduces you and sets expectations. The second delivers something useful, so the reader is glad they signed up. The third builds trust with a story or proof.

Only then comes the ask: the offer, the invitation, or the request. Ending with a reminder is polite rather than pushy, because busy readers often mean to act and forget. Skipping straight to the ask is the most common mistake in email sequences.

Writing each email well

Keep the subject line short and honest. Readers decide in seconds whether to open, and a subject that promises something the email does not deliver costs trust. One idea per email keeps the writing focused and the reader's next step clear.

Write the way you speak, and read each email aloud before you send it. If a sentence sounds stiff on the page, it will sound stiff in the inbox. End every email with one clear next step, not three.

Honest limits

This tool plans the shape of a sequence. It cannot write the emails for you, judge whether the timing suits your readers, or know what your audience cares about. The outline is a starting point; the content must come from you.

Email rules differ by country and by platform. Always write only to people who asked to hear from you, include a way to stop, and check the rules that apply where your readers live.

Questions people ask

How many emails should a sequence have?

Three to five is a good range for most goals. Three keeps it tight; five gives room for a story and a reminder. More than five usually means the sequence is trying to do too much.

How far apart should I send them?

Give readers breathing room between emails. Daily emails suit a short launch or a course; a few days apart suits a welcome sequence. Watch how readers respond and adjust.

Can I use this for cold emails?

No. This tool is for people who asked to hear from you. Writing to strangers who never signed up breaks trust and breaks spam rules in many places.

Does this tool send the emails?

No. It builds the plan. You write the full emails and send them through your own email tool, to your own permission-based list.
