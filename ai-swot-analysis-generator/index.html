How to use the SWOT generator

Start by writing one or two sentences about the business or project, then add the goal of the analysis — the decision you are trying to make. A clear goal keeps the grid useful, because a strength only matters in relation to what you are trying to do.

Next, fill in the four boxes. If you are stuck, press "Fill starter entries" to load a set of generic lines in each quadrant, then edit them one by one. Delete anything that does not fit, and add the facts only you know: your costs, your customers, your skills, your market. When the grid looks right, press "Build SWOT text" to see the finished version, then copy it or download it as a text file.

What goes in each quadrant

Strengths and weaknesses are about the inside of the business — things you control, like skills, costs, products and reputation. Opportunities and threats are about the outside — things happening around you, like new demand, new competitors, changing rules or rising costs. Keeping that split clear is what makes the grid work.

Write short, specific lines. "Loyal customers who return every week" is more useful than "good business". A line you could argue about is a good line: it forces a real discussion. If a box stays empty, that is a signal too — it usually means you need more information before deciding.

What this tool cannot do

This tool organises your own thinking; it does not analyse your business. It has no market data, no knowledge of your finances, and no way to judge whether an entry is true. The starter entries are generic lines that fit many small businesses, which is exactly why they must be edited — an unedited grid tells you nothing you did not already know.

Use the finished grid as the start of a conversation, not the end of one. Talk it through with a partner, a colleague or a mentor, and check the outside facts — demand, competitors, costs — against real sources before you commit money or time.

Questions people ask

What is a SWOT analysis?

A SWOT analysis lists the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats of a business or project in four boxes, so the full picture fits on one page. Strengths and weaknesses describe the inside of the business, while opportunities and threats describe the world around it.

Where do the starter entries come from?

They come from built-in template lists stored in this page. They are generic starting points for a typical small business, not findings about your business, and they should be edited or replaced with your own facts.

Can I edit the starter entries?

Yes. Every box is an editable text area. Change, delete or add lines freely, then press "Build SWOT text" again to see the updated grid.

Does this tool save my work?

No. Everything stays in your browser tab and disappears when you leave the page. Copy the finished grid or download it as a text file to keep it.
