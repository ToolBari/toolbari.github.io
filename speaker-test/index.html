Speaker Test

The speaker test plays a tone on your left speaker, then your right speaker, so you can check both channels. It also runs a frequency sweep from 100 Hz to 8000 Hz to help you spot gaps or rattling.

Press a button to play a test tone.
Keep the volume low at first and raise it slowly. Stop at once if a tone hurts your ears or your speakers rattle.

How to check left and right speakers

Press Play left tone and listen: the sound should come only from your left speaker or the left ear of your headphones. Then press Play right tone and check the other side. If you hear a tone on the wrong side, your speakers or headphone cable may be swapped, or your system balance may be set to one side.

If one side is silent, check the cable, the speaker power, and your system sound settings before assuming a speaker is broken. Try the same test with headphones: if both channels work there, the problem is with the speakers or their wiring, not with your device.

What the frequency sweep does

The sweep plays a smooth tone that rises from 100 Hz to 8000 Hz over five seconds. Listen for places where the volume drops away, where the tone vanishes for a moment, or where the speaker buzzes or rattles. A healthy speaker should sound fairly even all the way through, though small laptop speakers are often quiet at the lowest frequencies.

Rattling usually means something near the speaker is vibrating, or the speaker itself is damaged. Try moving objects away from the speaker and run the sweep again. If the rattle follows the same frequency every time, the speaker itself is the likely cause.

Questions people ask

Why do I hear nothing?

Check that your volume is up, the browser tab is not muted, and the right output device is selected in your system sound settings. If the page says your browser does not support Web Audio, try a different browser.

What if the left and right tones sound swapped?

Your speakers may be wired the wrong way round, or your system balance may be reversed. Swap the speaker cables or check the balance setting in your system sound settings, then run the test again.

What is the frequency sweep for?

The sweep plays a tone rising from 100 Hz to 8000 Hz over five seconds. It helps you hear gaps, volume drops and rattling that a single tone would miss.

Can this test damage my speakers?

At normal listening volume, no. Keep the volume low at first and raise it slowly, and stop at once if a tone hurts your ears or your speakers rattle.
