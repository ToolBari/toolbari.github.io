How to use the speedometer

Press Start and allow location access when your browser asks. Keep the page open while you move and the speed updates as new location readings arrive. The distance readout adds up the path you have travelled, and the maximum speed keeps the fastest reading of the session. Press Stop when you are done.

Speed is shown in kilometres per hour and miles per hour. Distance is shown in metres for short trips and kilometres for longer ones.

When the speedometer cannot read

If location permission is denied, the tool says so and does not guess. If there is no signal — indoors, underground or in dense built-up areas — the tool waits and tells you there is no signal yet. Move outside with a clearer view of the sky and try again.

Some devices do not report a speed value. In that case the speed is worked out from the distance and time between location fixes instead, and the reading may jump around more.

Getting the best signal

Location works best outdoors with a clear view of the sky. Tall buildings, tunnels and heavy tree cover can weaken the signal or make readings jump. If the speed looks wrong, wait a few seconds for the signal to settle.

Keep the page open while you travel, since readings only update while the page is in front of you. Closing the page or switching apps may pause the location updates.

Questions people ask

Why does the speedometer ask for my location?

Speed is calculated from how your location changes over time, so the tool cannot work without location access. Your location is never sent anywhere.

Why does it show no signal?

Location signals are weak indoors, underground and in dense built-up areas. Move outside with a clearer view of the sky and try again.

How is distance measured?

The tool adds up the distance between each pair of location fixes along your route, so it follows the path you actually travelled.

Is my location stored or shared?

No. All readings stay in your browser and disappear when you leave the page.
