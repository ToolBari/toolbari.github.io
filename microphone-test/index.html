How to test your microphone

Press Start microphone and allow the permission your browser asks for. The page then shows the name of the microphone your browser is using, and the level meter starts moving as you speak. Speak at your normal volume and watch the bar: it should rise clearly when you talk and fall back when you are quiet.

For a fuller check, press Record 5 seconds, speak for the full five seconds, then press Play recording to hear yourself back. If the playback sounds clear, your microphone is working well for calls and recordings.

What the level meter shows

The meter shows how loud your microphone signal is right now, on a scale from 0 to 100 percent. It is a relative reading, not a decibel measurement, so use it to compare: a quiet room should sit near zero, normal speech should move the bar well into the middle, and shouting should push it high.

If the meter pins at 100 percent while you speak normally, your input is too hot and your voice may sound distorted on calls. Move a little further from the microphone or lower the input volume in your system sound settings. If the meter barely moves, move closer or raise the input volume.

Questions people ask

Is my voice uploaded or recorded?

No. The microphone stream is analysed in your browser and the test recording stays on your device. Nothing is sent to any server.

Why is the meter stuck at zero?

The most common reasons are a denied permission, a muted microphone, or the wrong input device. Check your browser permission, then check your system sound settings to confirm the right microphone is selected and unmuted.

What level should I aim for?

Speaking normally should move the meter well above zero without pinning it at 100 percent all the time. A meter that sits near the middle while you talk is a healthy level for calls.

Why does the device name look generic?

Some browsers hide the real device name until permission is granted, and some show only a general name. Grant permission first, then restart the test to see the full name.
