Link Extractor

Paste any block of text and this tool pulls out every web address it contains. Duplicates are removed, the links are counted, and one button copies the whole list for pasting elsewhere.

    The extractor finds addresses that look like links; it does not open them or check that they work. A link in the list may be broken, mistyped or shortened, so check important links before sharing them.

    What counts as a link

    The extractor looks for addresses starting with http:// or https://, and for addresses starting with www.. It reads through the whole block of text, so links buried in paragraphs, lists or pasted messages are all found. A link at the end of a sentence keeps its trailing full stop out of the address, and closing brackets and quotes are trimmed too.

    Each address appears once in the list, even if it appears many times in the pasted text. The count shows how many unique links were found, not how many times they appeared, so you can see at a glance how many different destinations the text points to.

    Using the list

    The links are listed one per line in the order they first appear in the text. The copy button puts the whole list on your clipboard with one link per line, ready to paste into a document, a spreadsheet or a message. The list itself is plain text, so nothing is opened while you work with it.

    This is handy for pulling the links out of a long article, a chat export or a set of notes. If a link looks odd, check it in your browser before sharing it, because the extractor reads the text as written and cannot tell a mistyped address from a real one. Long lists stay readable because each link sits on its own numbered line.

    Questions people ask

    Which addresses does the extractor find?

    It finds addresses starting with http:// or https:// and addresses starting with www. . It trims trailing punctuation such as full stops and commas, so a link at the end of a sentence is still read correctly.

    How are duplicates handled?

    Each address appears once in the list, even if it appears many times in the pasted text. The count shows how many unique links were found, not how many times they appeared.

    Why is a link missing from the list?

    The extractor only finds complete addresses with a scheme or a www. start. A bare domain such as example.com without www. or a scheme is not picked up, because plain words and domains are hard to tell apart.

    Does the tool check that the links work?

    No. It only reads the text you paste and lists what looks like a link. Nothing is opened or checked, and the text never leaves your browser.
