AI Readability Checker

Paste your text below and this tool scores how easy it is to read, using the Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch-Kincaid Grade formulas. It also shows your sentence, word, and syllable counts, plus plain steps to make the text clearer.

Formulas used: Flesch Reading Ease = 206.835 − 1.015 × (words ÷ sentences) − 84.6 × (syllables ÷ words). Flesch-Kincaid Grade = 0.39 × (words ÷ sentences) + 11.8 × (syllables ÷ words) − 15.59.

Syllables are counted with a vowel-group heuristic: each group of vowels in a word counts as one syllable, a silent final e is dropped when the word has another vowel group, and every word counts at least one syllable.

This tool works fully in your browser and calls no AI service, API, or backend. The scores are estimates from a simple vowel-group syllable count, not a full linguistic analysis. Use them as a guide, not a final judgement, and always read the text yourself.

How to use the readability checker

Paste any text into the box and press the button. The tool counts your words, sentences, and syllables, then works out two scores. Flesch Reading Ease runs from about 0 to 100, where higher means easier to read. Flesch-Kincaid Grade gives the school grade level the text matches, so a grade of 8 means most readers at that level can follow it. Both scores come from the same two averages: words per sentence and syllables per word.

Below the scores you see the working: the counts, the two averages, and the formula with your numbers filled in. That way you can check the result yourself instead of trusting a black box. Shorter sentences and plainer words raise the ease score and lower the grade level. Nothing you paste leaves your browser.

How the syllable count works

Syllables are counted with a simple vowel-group heuristic. The tool looks at each word, counts each group of vowels as one syllable, and drops a silent final e when the word has another vowel group. Every word counts at least one syllable. This rule is fast and works well for most everyday words, but it is only an estimate.

Some words will be miscounted. Words like every can count one syllable too many, and words like simple can count one too few, because English spelling does not always match sound. Names, abbreviations, and numbers add more guesswork. That is why the scores are a guide: use them to compare drafts and spot hard passages, not as a precise measure of reading level.

Worked example, checked by hand

Take this short text: Good sleep helps the body and mind recover. Most adults need seven to nine hours of sleep each night. It has 19 words, 2 sentences, and 24 syllables by the vowel-group rule. The average sentence length is 19 divided by 2, which is 9.5 words. The average syllables per word is 24 divided by 19, which is about 1.26.

Flesch Reading Ease is 206.835 minus 1.015 times 9.5 minus 84.6 times 1.26, which works out to 90.3. That is easy reading. Flesch-Kincaid Grade is 0.39 times 9.5 plus 11.8 times 1.26 minus 15.59, which works out to 3.0. Paste the same text into the tool and you will see the same two scores, because the tool runs the same arithmetic.

Making hard text easier to read

If your score is lower than you want, start with sentence length. Find the longest sentence and split it in two. Then look for long words: a shorter word usually says the same thing with less effort. Replace words that need a dictionary with words a reader already knows. Read the text aloud after each change: if you run out of breath, the sentence is still too long.

Do not chase a perfect score. A score in the 60 to 70 range suits most general writing, and simpler text suits instructions and public notices. Technical writing for experts can sit lower and still be right for its readers. The score is a tool for judgement, not a target to hit at any cost.

Questions people ask

What is a good Flesch Reading Ease score?

Scores of 60 to 70 are usually described as standard reading level and suit most general writing. Scores of 80 and above are easy, and scores below 50 are hard for most readers.

What does the Flesch-Kincaid Grade mean?

It gives the school grade level the text matches. A grade of 8 means a reader at eighth-grade level can usually follow the text. Lower grades mean easier text.

Why are the scores only estimates?

Syllables are counted with a simple vowel-group rule, which miscounts some English words. The scores are a useful guide for comparing drafts, not a precise measure.

Does the tool store my text?

No. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you paste is sent anywhere or stored.
