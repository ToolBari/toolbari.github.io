Color Blindness Simulator

Upload any image and this simulator shows how it may look to someone with protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia or achromatopsia, next to the original. The image is processed in your browser and never uploaded anywhere.

Choose an image first, then press Simulate.

The simulation is a rough approximation of how colors may appear. It is not a medical test and cannot diagnose color vision.

How the simulation works

Every pixel of your image has red, green and blue values. The simulator mixes those three values through a color matrix for the type you pick, so colors that differ only in the affected channels move closer together. Protanopia and deuteranopia both change how reds and greens appear, tritanopia changes blues and yellows, and achromatopsia removes color and leaves brightness only.

The original image stays on the left and the simulation appears on the right, so you can compare them directly. Large images are scaled down to a width of 600 pixels so the page stays fast. You can switch the simulation type and press Simulate again as many times as you like without uploading the image again.

What the simulation is for

Designers use simulations like this to check whether text, charts, buttons and warnings stay readable when colors shift. If two colors in your image look identical in the simulation, people with that type of color vision may not be able to tell them apart either, so the design needs a difference in brightness, shape or labels as well.

The simulation is only an approximation: real color vision varies from person to person, and no matrix can reproduce exactly what another person sees. Use it as a design check, not as a statement about any one person's vision. Try the same image under each of the four types: a chart that survives protanopia may still fail under tritanopia, so checking all four gives a fuller picture.

Questions people ask

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The image is processed in your browser with canvas and never leaves your device.

What is the difference between protanopia and deuteranopia?

Both affect red-green perception. Protanopia relates to red-sensitive cones and deuteranopia to green-sensitive cones, and the matrices mix colors differently for each.

Why does the simulation look different from the original?

The matrix remaps each pixel's color channels, so colors that differ only in the affected channels can look the same in the simulation.

Can this test my own color vision?

No. It only transforms an image. It cannot test or diagnose anyone's vision.
