    How to test your keyboard

    Click anywhere on this page first so the browser sends key presses here, then press keys one by one. Each key lights up green on the on-screen keyboard while you hold it, and the history log below records every press with its key code. Work through the rows from top to bottom so you do not miss any key.

    The counter shows how many keys you have pressed and how many different keys that covers. If you press a key and nothing lights up, try it again firmly, then try the same key in another app to see whether the problem follows the key or the browser.

    What a stuck key looks like

    A stuck key is one that the browser sees pressed but never sees released. On this page, a stuck key stays lit green after you let go, and the stuck-key check names it. Try pressing the key again firmly and releasing it slowly — that often clears it.

    If a key stays stuck, dust or dirt under the key may be holding it down. Turn the keyboard upside down and tap it gently, or use compressed air if you have it. A key that never lights up at all, even in other apps, may be faulty and need a repair or a replacement keyboard.

    Questions people ask

    A key does not light up. Is it broken?

    Not always. Press it firmly, try another browser, and check whether the key works in another app. If it works elsewhere, the browser may be blocking it. If it works nowhere, the key itself may be faulty.

    What does the stuck-key check do?

    It lists every key the browser has seen pressed but not released. A key stays on the list until you release it or press it again to clear it.

    Does this page record what I type?

    It only lists key codes in a log on this page, such as KeyA or Space. Nothing is sent anywhere, and closing the page clears the log.

    Can I test a keyboard on a phone or tablet?

    On-screen keyboards on phones and tablets do not send the same key events, so this tester works best with a physical keyboard on a computer or laptop.
