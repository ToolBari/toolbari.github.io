AI Keyword Cluster Tool

This tool groups a list of keywords into clusters by the words they share, so related keywords sit together. Paste your keywords one per line to see clusters with counts, an ungrouped list, and a copy button for each cluster.

Grouping is by shared words, not search data.

Grouping is by shared words only, not by search volume or ranking data. Two keywords can share a word and still mean different things — check each cluster before you use it. This tool works fully in your browser and calls no AI service, API, or backend.

How to use the keyword cluster tool

Paste your keywords into the box, one per line, and press "Cluster keywords". The tool shows each cluster with its size, the words the keywords share, and a copy button, plus a list of keywords that did not fit any cluster. Duplicate lines are counted once.

Clusters are sorted biggest first. A cluster of two keywords is still a cluster — it tells you those two belong on the same page or in the same group. The ungrouped list is just as useful: those keywords may need their own page, or a different grouping of their own.

How the grouping works

The tool reduces each keyword to stem words: it lowercases everything, drops short filler words such as "for", "the" and "best", and trims common endings — "running" becomes "run", "shoes" becomes "shoe", "studies" becomes "study". Two keywords cluster together when they share at least one stem word, and clusters merge when a keyword links two groups.

That is all the tool does. It knows nothing about search volume, ranking, or what people actually search for, and it cannot tell that two words mean the same thing if they are spelled differently. Treat the clusters as a first sort, not a final answer.

What the clusters are good for

Clusters help you plan pages and sections. Keywords in one cluster usually belong together on one page, because they answer the same need in different words. The shared words shown with each cluster often make a good page title or heading.

They also show gaps. A keyword sitting alone may be a topic you have not covered yet, or a wording nobody else uses. Either way, it is worth a look before you decide where it belongs — or whether it belongs at all.

Use the clusters with the intent of each keyword in mind. A cluster full of question words usually belongs on a guide page, while a cluster full of buying words belongs on a page with a clear next step. Grouping first and sorting by intent second gives you a plan that covers both what people search for and what they want to do.

Questions people ask

What is a keyword cluster?

A keyword cluster is a group of keywords that belong together because they share words and answer the same need. Grouping them helps you plan which keywords go on the same page instead of spreading them thin.

How does the tool decide what clusters together?

It reduces each keyword to stem words and groups keywords that share at least one stem. Clusters merge when one keyword shares words with two groups. Common filler words are ignored.

Why is a keyword ungrouped?

It shares no stem word with any other keyword in the list. That usually means it belongs on its own page, or that it needs different wording to fit a group.

Does it use search data?

No. Grouping is by shared words only. The tool has no search volume, ranking or trend data, and it does not connect to any service.
