The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1) — where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments. This is the standard reducing-balance formula banks use: interest is charged only on what you still owe.

Worked example

A loan of 100,000 at 10% per year for 12 months: r = 10 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.008333. The formula gives an EMI of 8,791.59 per month. Over 12 months you pay back 105,499.06 in total, of which 5,499.06 is interest.

Questions people ask

What is an EMI?

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed amount you pay a lender every month until a loan is repaid. Each payment covers part of the principal and part of the interest.

What is the EMI formula?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments.

Does the EMI include fees and charges?

No. Processing fees, insurance and other charges are usually extra, and lenders apply their own rounding. Treat any calculator result as an estimate and check the lender's own repayment schedule before signing.
