This converter changes the unit of an angle, not the angle itself. A full turn is 360 degrees, 2pi radians or 400 gradians, whichever unit you read it in.

The angle units

Degrees split a full turn into 360 parts and are the everyday unit for angles. Radians measure an angle by the arc it cuts on a circle: a full turn is 2pi radians, so 180 degrees is pi radians and 90 degrees is pi/2 radians. Radians are the natural unit in mathematics, which is why the sine and cosine functions in this site's graph plotter use them. Gradians split a full turn into 400 parts, so a right angle is exactly 100 gradians. Turns count whole rotations, so 1 turn is a full circle and 0.5 turns is a half circle.

Arcminutes and arcseconds are small subdivisions of a degree: one degree is 60 arcminutes and one arcminute is 60 arcseconds, so one degree is 3600 arcseconds. They are used where angles need fine precision, such as navigation and astronomy. The converter shows all six units at once, with radians also given as a multiple of pi when the value is a simple fraction of pi.

Converting by hand

Every conversion goes through the same fact: a full turn is 360 degrees, 2pi radians, 400 gradians, 21600 arcminutes and 1296000 arcseconds. To convert degrees to radians, multiply by pi/180. To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180/pi. The converter shows the factor it used for each unit, so you can follow the arithmetic.

Questions people ask

How many radians are in 180 degrees?

180 degrees is pi radians, about 3.14159. In general, multiply degrees by pi/180 to get radians.

What is a gradian?

A gradian is 1/400 of a full turn, so a right angle is 100 gradians and a full circle is 400 gradians.

How many arcminutes are in a degree?

One degree is 60 arcminutes, and one arcminute is 60 arcseconds, so one degree is 3600 arcseconds.

What is a turn?

A turn is one full rotation: 360 degrees, 2pi radians or 400 gradians. Half a turn is 180 degrees.
