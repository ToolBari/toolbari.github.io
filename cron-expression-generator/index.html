How a cron expression is read

A cron expression has five fields, read left to right: minute, hour, day of the month, month, and day of the week. Each field says when the job runs: a number picks one value, a star means every value, and a slash means every so often — */15 in the minute field means every 15 minutes.

Read the expression 0 9 * * 1 as a sentence: at minute 0, hour 9, any day of the month, any month, on weekday 1. That is every Monday at 9 in the morning. The plain-English summary under the expression does exactly this reading for you.

Common schedules

Most jobs use a handful of patterns. * * * * * runs every minute, 0 * * * * runs at the top of every hour, and 0 0 * * * runs once a day at midnight. Weekly jobs usually fix the weekday, like 0 9 * * 1 for Monday morning.

Monthly jobs fix the day of the month instead: 0 0 1 * * runs on the first of every month at midnight. Start from one of the presets above and adjust a single field to make the schedule yours.

Questions people ask

What do the five fields mean?

From left to right: minute, hour, day of the month, month, and day of the week.

What does the star mean?

A star means every value for that field — for example, a star in the hour field means every hour.

How do I run something every 5 minutes?

Put */5 in the minute field and stars everywhere else, which gives */5 * * * *.

What happens if I set both a day and a weekday?

Most cron systems run the job when either one matches, but some systems differ — check your own system's docs.
