How the cost per share is worked out

The cost per share is the total cost of the animal divided by the number of shares. If an animal costs 700 and it is split into seven shares, each share costs 100. This calculator does that one division and shows its working, so everyone sharing the animal can see the same number.

The total cost is whatever you paid, or whatever you expect to pay, in your own currency. This page never suggests a price, because the cost of an animal varies enormously from place to place and from year to year, and any figure it offered would be wrong for most readers.

The three-way meat split

The well-known division of Qurbani meat is into three equal parts: one part for the family, one part for relatives and friends, and one part for people in need. The split is by weight, so each part is a third of the total meat, whatever the total turns out to be.

Enter the total meat weight in kilograms and the calculator shows all three parts. If the weight does not divide neatly into thirds, the first two parts are rounded to two decimal places and the third part takes the remainder, so the three parts always add back up to the total exactly.

A worked example

Suppose a cow costs 700 and is shared by seven people, and the meat weighs 90 kilograms in total. Enter 700 for the cost, 7 for the shares, and 90 for the weight, and the calculator shows 100 per share, with the meat split into three parts of 30 kilograms each.

Try another: an animal costs 450 with three shares and 60 kilograms of meat. The cost per share is 150, because 450 divided by 3 is 150, and the meat splits into three parts of 20 kilograms each. The tool always shows its working, so you can check the sums yourself.

How many shares does an animal have?

A goat or a sheep counts as one share, for one person or one household. A larger animal, such as a cow, a buffalo, or a camel, can be shared by up to seven people, with each person taking one share. That is why the calculator accepts shares from 1 to 7.

If you are unsure how many shares your animal has, ask the person selling it or your local mosque. The usual arrangement is agreed before the animal is bought, so that everyone knows their share of the cost in advance.

Buying the animal

Buy from a seller you trust, and check the animal before you agree a price. It should be healthy, well-fed, and old enough for Qurbani: a goat or sheep is usually at least a year old, and a cow at least two years. A reputable seller will be happy to answer questions, and a local mosque can usually recommend someone reliable.

In many places the animal is bought at a market or direct from a farmer, and in cities people often arrange Qurbani through a mosque or a charity that handles the purchase and the sacrifice. Whatever route you take, the cost you enter in this calculator should be the full cost, including any slaughter and butchering fees if those are charged separately.

After the sacrifice

Once the animal is dressed, the meat is weighed, and that total weight is the number to enter for the three-way split. Weigh it before the shares are packed, so the thirds are worked out from the real total rather than a guess. A kitchen scale works for small amounts; a hanging scale is easier for a large animal.

Keep the three parts separate as you pack, and label the bags if several families are sharing the animal. The family part can be frozen in portions for the weeks ahead, but the part for people in need should go out promptly, while the meat is fresh. Qurbani is a shared effort, from the buying to the giving, and a little organisation makes the whole day run smoothly.

Planning the cost with others

Sharing an animal works best when the money is agreed early. Decide the shares, work out the cost per share with this calculator, and collect the money before the animal is bought, so nobody is chasing payments on the day of Eid. A short written note of who paid what avoids misunderstandings later.

If the final cost is different from the estimate, run the calculator again with the real figure and settle the difference. A few units either way is normal: animals are priced by the market, and the price on the day is the price that counts.

Sharing the meat fairly

Fair sharing starts with weighing the meat. Once the total weight is known, the three-way split is a matter of thirds, and this calculator gives you the numbers. It also helps to mix the cuts: each of the three parts should have a share of the better cuts and the everyday cuts, not one part with all the best meat.

Pack the shares in separate bags as you go, and label them if several families are involved. The part for people in need is often the most important part of the whole Qurbani, so take care with it: deliver it promptly, while the meat is fresh, to people who will genuinely benefit.

When Qurbani happens

Qurbani takes place during Eid al-Adha, the festival of sacrifice, which in 2027 is expected on Sunday 16 May 2027. The sacrifice is made after the Eid prayer, and the days of Eid that follow are the days for sharing the meat with family, friends, and people in need.

As with all Islamic dates, the confirmed date depends on the sighting of the moon, so Eid al-Adha may move by a day. Plan around the expected date, and confirm it with your local mosque as the time approaches.
