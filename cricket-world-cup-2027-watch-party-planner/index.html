Pick your match day

The tournament runs from Saturday 2 October to Sunday 21 November 2027, so you have seven weeks of match days to choose from. The group stage fills October and the first part of November, the Super 7 follows, and the tournament ends with the semi-finals on 17 and 18 November and the final on 21 November.

The final is the obvious day for the biggest party. It is a Sunday, it is the last match of the tournament, and it is the one everyone will be talking about the next day. The semi-finals are the next best choice: two matches on back-to-back days, in Cape Town and Centurion, with a place in the final at stake in each.

A group match can be just as good a reason for a party, especially if it is your team's biggest game of the group stage. Pick a date inside the tournament window, and the planner will tell you if the date you chose falls outside it — a small check that saves an embarrassing mistake.

How many guests?

The guest count drives everything else in the plan: the seats, the plates, the snacks and the drinks all scale with it. Count everyone who is coming, including children, and remember that the planner adds you to the guest count automatically — the number you enter is the number of guests, not counting yourself.

Think about the room as well as the numbers. A 50-over match lasts most of the day, so everyone needs a proper seat, not a spare chair borrowed at the last minute. If the room is small, a smaller party is a better party: eight comfortable guests beat fifteen squeezed ones.

It is also worth deciding early whether children are coming. A family watch party needs a different plan from an adults-only one: earlier food, a quieter corner for anyone who gets bored, and an end time that works for the youngest guest.

Food and drink quantities

The planner works from the amounts you enter per person. Tell it how many snack servings each person will eat and how many drinks each person will have, and it multiplies by the number of people. Nothing is preset: the amounts are yours, because only you know how hungry your guests are.

The 10 percent extra option is worth keeping on. It rounds the totals up, so you end up with a little more than the exact maths — and running out of food halfway through a long match is worse than having a few servings left over. The planner tells you when the extra is included, so you can turn it off if you prefer the exact numbers.

Think about the shape of the day when you choose the amounts. A 50-over match has two innings with a break between them, so food served in two rounds — something at the start and something at the break — works better than putting everything out at once.

The schedule of the day

A watch party has its own schedule, and it starts before the first ball. Get the screen and sound checked the day before, not on the morning of the match. Lay out the seating, put the drinks somewhere cool, and have the first round of snacks ready before the guests arrive.

The innings break is the natural pause in the day. It is the time to clear plates, put out fresh food, and let people stretch their legs. Plan for it as a proper break rather than a scramble, and the second half of the day will feel relaxed instead of rushed.

Plan the end of the day too. A 50-over match lasts most of the day, so think about when guests will leave, who is clearing up, and what happens to the leftover food. A short tidy-up with everyone's help beats a long one on your own after everyone has gone.

The checklist

The planner prints a checklist with every plan, and it is worth reading through it the day before the party. Screen and sound come first: check them the day before, with the actual device you will watch on, so there are no surprises on the morning.

Seating comes next. Count the seats against the guest list and make sure there is one proper seat per person, with a clear view of the screen. Then the food and drink: snacks and drinks ready before the start, plates and cups counted out, and rubbish bags where people can find them.

The last two items are easy to forget. Phone chargers matter because guests will be checking scores and messages all day, and a plan for the innings break — what food comes out, what gets cleared away — keeps the day running smoothly.

Watching with kids and neighbours

A World Cup final is a family occasion, and children often enjoy a watch party more than adults expect. Give them a job — keeping score, handing out snacks, or choosing the music during the break — and they stay interested far longer than if they are just watching.

Neighbours are worth thinking about too. A long day of cheering carries through walls, so a quick word beforehand goes a long way, and an invitation is better still. A watch party that includes the neighbours is a better party than one that annoys them.

Keep the volume reasonable in the evening, especially if the match runs late. The final is on a Sunday, and not everyone next door will be watching. Goodwill is easy to keep and hard to win back.

If the weather turns

Cricket and rain have a long history together, and a watch party needs a plan for it. If you are watching outdoors, have an indoor backup ready: a room where everyone can move to without losing their seats or their snacks. The match itself may be delayed by rain, and a delay is much easier to enjoy indoors.

Rain can also change the match. A delayed or shortened game is still a game, and the no-result rule means a washed-out match still gives each team a point. If the match you planned the party around is rained off, the party does not have to be: the food, the company and the highlights are still there.

The best backup plan is simply to keep the day flexible. Have the food ready, have the screen working, and let the day take whatever shape the weather and the cricket give it. A relaxed host makes a relaxed party.
