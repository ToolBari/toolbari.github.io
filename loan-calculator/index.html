Loan Calculator

Work out what a loan costs before you commit. Enter the amount, the annual interest rate and the term to see the monthly payment, the total interest, and a full month-by-month repayment schedule.

These figures are estimates from the numbers you enter, using a standard fixed-rate repayment formula. A real lender may add fees or use different terms, so confirm the final figures with the lender before you agree to anything.

How the monthly payment is worked out

The monthly payment comes from the standard repayment formula. The annual rate is first turned into a monthly rate by dividing it by 100 and then by 12. If the monthly rate is r, the amount is P, and the number of monthly payments is n, the payment is P times r times (1 + r) to the power n, divided by (1 + r) to the power n minus 1.

When the rate is zero, there is no interest to pay, so the monthly payment is simply the amount divided by the number of months. The steps shown under the result spell out the numbers used, so you can check the working yourself.

Reading the schedule

Each monthly payment is split into two parts: the interest charged on the balance that month, and the part that reduces what you owe. Early on, most of each payment is interest, because the balance is at its highest. As the balance falls, the interest part shrinks and more of each payment goes toward the amount borrowed.

The schedule table shows this month by month, ending with a zero balance when the loan is fully repaid. The total interest is the difference between everything you pay and the amount you borrowed.

Questions people ask

What is the monthly payment formula?

The monthly payment is the amount times the monthly rate times (1 plus the monthly rate) to the power of the number of payments, divided by (1 plus the monthly rate) to the power of the number of payments minus 1. The monthly rate is the annual rate divided by 100 and then by 12.

What happens with a zero percent rate?

With a zero rate there is no interest, so the monthly payment is simply the amount divided by the number of months, and the total interest shown is zero.

Why is the first payment mostly interest?

Interest is charged on the balance outstanding, which is at its highest at the start. As you pay the balance down, the interest part of each payment falls and the principal part rises.

Does this include fees?

No. The calculator only uses the amount, rate and term you enter. Arrangement fees, insurance and other lender charges are not included, so ask the lender for the full cost.
