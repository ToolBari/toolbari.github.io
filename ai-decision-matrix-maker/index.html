Scores are your judgement, not measurements, so a close result means the choice is genuinely close. This tool works fully in your browser and calls no AI service, API, or backend. It does the arithmetic; the decision is yours.

How to use this maker

Name two to four options, then name two to five criteria and give each one a weight. The weights must add up to 100, so a weight of 50 means that criterion counts for half of the decision. Leave any criterion you do not need blank and it is skipped.

Score each option from 1 to 5 for every criterion, then press the button. The tool shows the weighted total for each option, the working behind it, and the winner. If two options tie, it says so plainly instead of picking one.

How the weighted total is worked out

Each score is multiplied by its criterion's weight as a share of 100, and the results are added. For example, with weights of 50, 30 and 20 and scores of 5, 3 and 4, the total is 5 times 0.5 plus 3 times 0.3 plus 4 times 0.2, which is 2.5 plus 0.9 plus 0.8, which is 4.2 out of 5.

The working is shown for every option, so you can check the arithmetic yourself. A total of 5 means the option scored top marks on every criterion; a total of 1 means it scored the lowest everywhere. Most real options land somewhere in between.

Picking honest weights and scores

Set the weights before you score, and set them by what matters most to you, not by which option you already favour. If one criterion clearly dominates the decision, give it a big weight and say so. The matrix is only as honest as the numbers you put in.

Score every option against the same bar. A 4 for one option should mean the same as a 4 for another, or the totals will mislead you. If you cannot score an option fairly, find out more about it before you decide.

Honest limits

A decision matrix organises your judgement; it does not replace it. If the winner feels wrong, the weights or the scores probably are, and the right move is to fix the inputs rather than to trust the output.

The tool cannot tell you which criteria matter, whether your scores are fair, or whether an option you left out would have won. Use the result as a clear summary of your own thinking, then decide.

Questions people ask

Why must the weights total 100?

Weights are shares of the decision, so they must add up to the whole. A weight of 50 means half the decision, and 100 in total keeps every share honest and comparable.

What do the scores 1 to 5 mean?

1 means poor, 2 means below average, 3 means average, 4 means good, and 5 means excellent. Score every option against the same bar so the totals mean something.

What if two options tie?

The tool tells you it is a tie and names the tied options. A tie means the numbers cannot separate them, so look at the one criterion that matters most to you.

Does this tool make the decision for me?

No. It does the arithmetic on the weights and scores you enter. The judgement behind those numbers, and the final decision, are yours.
