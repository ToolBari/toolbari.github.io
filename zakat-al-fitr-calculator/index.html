What Zakat al-Fitr is

Zakat al-Fitr is a small charity given at the end of Ramadan, before the Eid prayer. One share is given for each person in the household, so that everyone in the community can celebrate Eid with food on the table. It is one of the last acts of Ramadan, and it turns the joy of Eid into something shared.

The share is traditionally a measure of a staple food, such as wheat, barley, dates, or rice, and in many places the cash value of that food is given instead. The amount is modest by design: it is meant to be within reach of every household that can afford it, and to reach the people who need it most.

How the total is worked out

The arithmetic could hardly be simpler: the number of people multiplied by the per-person amount. If there are five people in the household and the per-person amount is twelve, the total is sixty. This calculator does that one multiplication and shows its working, so there is nothing to get wrong.

The only decision is the per-person amount, and that decision is not the calculator's to make. It comes from your local guidance, usually announced by a mosque or an Islamic authority near the end of Ramadan, and it varies from place to place and from year to year.

Where the per-person amount comes from

The per-person amount is set locally, because the price of staple food is different in different places. Your mosque, your Islamic centre, or a scholar you trust will announce the figure for your area, usually in the last week or two of Ramadan. Until that announcement, there is no number to enter, and that is exactly as it should be.

This page never presets an amount, because a preset figure would be wrong for most readers and might be followed by mistake. If you are reading this before the figure is announced, wait for your local guidance, then come back and enter it here. The calculator will be ready whenever you are.

A worked example

Here is an example to show how the calculator works. Suppose your local guidance says the per-person amount is 12, and there are five people in your household: you, your spouse, and three children. Enter 5 for the number of people and 12 for the amount, and the calculator shows a total of 60.

Remember that 12 is only an example number used to show the arithmetic. Your own per-person amount comes from your local guidance and may be higher or lower. The calculator does not care what the number is: it multiplies whatever you enter by the number of people, and shows the result.

Who counts as a person in the household

Count yourself, your spouse, your children, and anyone else you support, such as an elderly parent living with you. The head of the household usually pays for everyone, including young children and anyone who cannot pay for themselves. Each person counts as one share, whatever their age.

If a baby is born before the Eid prayer, most scholars count the new arrival as well, so the household grows by one share. If someone in the household pays for themselves, count only the people you are paying for, and let the other person work out their own total.

Giving through a mosque or charity

Many people give Zakat al-Fitr through their mosque or a charity, which collects it and passes it on to the people who need it. That is a practical choice: the mosque knows who in the area is struggling, and the charity has the means to reach people quickly. If you give this way, check the deadline, because the organisation needs time to distribute what it receives before the Eid prayer.

Keep a simple note of what you gave, when, and for how many people. If you are paying for family members who live elsewhere, such as elderly parents, count them in your total and tell them you have paid on their behalf, so the share is not given twice. Whether the money stays in your town or travels further afield, the purpose is the same: that everyone can celebrate Eid.

When to give it

Zakat al-Fitr is given before the Eid prayer, and that timing is part of the point: it reaches people in time for Eid itself. Many people give it in the last few days of Ramadan, or on the morning of Eid before heading to the prayer. Giving it early is fine; giving it after the prayer misses the purpose.

Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March 2027, depending on the sighting of the moon. If you give through a mosque or a charity, check their deadline, because they need time to pass the money or food on to the people who will receive it.

If you are travelling at Eid, give before you leave, or arrange for someone at home to give on your behalf. The timing matters more than the place: as long as it reaches someone in need before the Eid prayer, the purpose is met.

Food or money?

The traditional form of Zakat al-Fitr is food: a measure of a staple such as wheat, rice, or dates, given to someone in need. In many places today, the cash value of that food is given instead, because it is easier to distribute and lets the recipient choose what they need most.

Scholars differ on whether money is acceptable, and the answer varies by school of thought and by local practice. Follow the guidance of your own mosque or scholar. This calculator works either way: enter the cash value per person if that is what your local guidance gives, and the total is the amount to give.

Whatever form you choose, give it with a clear intention and in good time. The amount is small, but the effect is not: for a family with nothing set aside for Eid, a share of food or its value is the difference between a celebration and an ordinary day.

Questions people ask

How much is Zakat al-Fitr per person?

It is set locally and varies from place to place and year to year, because it follows the price of staple food. Ask your mosque or a scholar you trust for the figure in your area. This calculator never presets an amount.

Who has to pay Zakat al-Fitr?

Every Muslim who can afford it pays one share per person in the household, including children and dependants. The head of the household usually pays for everyone, including those who cannot pay for themselves.

When must it be paid?

Before the Eid prayer. Many people give it in the last few days of Ramadan or on the morning of Eid. Giving it after the prayer misses the purpose, which is to reach people in time for Eid itself.

Can I pay it as money instead of food?

Many scholars allow the cash value of the food to be given instead, and in many places that is the usual practice. Others prefer food itself. Follow the guidance of your own mosque or scholar.

What if my household grows during Ramadan?

Count everyone at the time of payment. If a baby is born before the Eid prayer, most scholars count the new arrival as one more share, so add one person to your total.

When is Eid al-Fitr 2027?

Eid al-Fitr 2027 is expected on Tuesday 9 March 2027. The date depends on the sighting of the new moon, so the confirmed date may move by a day.
