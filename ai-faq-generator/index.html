How to use the FAQ generator

Type your topic or product into the first box, using the words your readers use. Then choose how many questions you need: three for a short page, six for a fuller one. Press the button and the tool drafts that many questions, each with a short answer skeleton in an editable box. Read each answer, replace the bracketed guidance with your own facts, and press Copy all when the list reads the way you want.

The questions follow the patterns people actually ask: what it is, how it works, who it is for, how to start, how long it takes, and where to get help. If a question does not fit your topic, simply leave it out of the copied list by deleting its text before you copy. The tool never saves anything, so your drafts exist only on this page until you copy them.

Writing answers people trust

A good FAQ answer is short, specific, and honest. Answer the question in the first sentence, then add one sentence of detail if it helps. Avoid vague promises and filler words: readers come to a FAQ page for facts, not for marketing. If you do not know an answer yet, say so plainly and say where the reader can find out.

Use the same words your readers use. If they say sign up and you say register, use their word in the question and the answer. Keep each answer to two or three sentences where you can. Long answers belong in a guide, not in a FAQ list. Read each answer aloud before publishing: if it sounds awkward spoken, it will read awkwardly too.

How many questions should a FAQ page have?

Four to six questions suit most pages. That is enough to cover the common doubts without turning the page into a wall of text. If you have more than eight questions, split them into groups by theme, such as getting started, pricing, and support, so readers can scan to the group they need.

Start with the questions people actually ask you, not the ones you wish they asked. Support messages, comments, and conversations are the best source. A FAQ page built from real questions earns trust; one built from guessed questions reads like a sales page. Review your list every few months and add the new questions that keep coming up.
