This tool reads your device orientation sensors and is a guide, not a precision instrument. Some devices and browsers do not report orientation at all, in which case the tool says so instead of guessing.

How to use the level

Press Start and allow the permission request if your device asks for one. For a flat surface, lay the device face up on it and watch the bubble: when the bubble sits in the centre ring, the surface is level. For an upright surface, switch to vertical mode and hold the edge of the device against it.

The angle readout shows the tilt in degrees. A reading under 1 degree counts as level and is labelled as such.

How the reading works

Horizontal mode measures tilt from flat using the front-back and left-right angles from your device sensors, combined into one tilt figure. The bubble moves in the direction of the tilt, just like the bubble in a real spirit level.

Vertical mode measures tilt from upright. A device held perfectly upright reads 0 degrees, and leaning it either way increases the reading. Cases, uneven device backs and sensor drift can shift the reading by a degree or two.

Tips for a steadier reading

Rest the device on the surface and wait a moment for the bubble to settle before reading it. Holding the device in the air gives a shakier reading than resting it on the surface you are checking.

To double-check a reading, turn the device around and measure the same surface again from the other direction. If the two readings disagree by more than a degree or two, the device back may be uneven.

Questions people ask

Why does the level ask for permission?

Some devices ask before sharing motion and orientation data. If you decline, the tool cannot read the sensors and will say so.

Why does it say my device is not supported?

Some browsers and devices do not expose orientation sensors to web pages. On those devices the level cannot work, so the page says so honestly.

How accurate is the level?

It is a handy guide for everyday jobs, not a precision instrument. Sensor drift and an uneven device back can shift the reading by a degree or two.

What is the difference between horizontal and vertical mode?

Horizontal mode checks flat surfaces, such as a table or a shelf. Vertical mode checks upright surfaces, such as a wall or a door frame.
