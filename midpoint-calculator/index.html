The midpoint formula

The midpoint of a segment is the point halfway between its two endpoints. Average the x coordinates and average the y coordinates: midpoint = ((x1 + x2) / 2, (y1 + y2) / 2). For the points (2, 3) and (8, 11), the midpoint is ((2 + 8) / 2, (3 + 11) / 2) = (5, 7). The midpoint is the same distance from both endpoints, and it sits on the segment joining them.

The distance between the points comes from the same idea as the slope: the differences in x and y form the two shorter sides of a right triangle, and the distance is the hypotenuse. Distance = sqrt((x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2). For (2, 3) and (8, 11) that is sqrt(36 + 64) = sqrt(100) = 10. The calculator shows both the midpoint and the distance with the substitution steps.

Why midpoints matter

Midpoints show up wherever something needs to be centred: the middle of a line on a map, the centre of a shape, or the average of two positions. Because the formula is just two averages, it works the same way in any number of dimensions, though this calculator works in two. In three dimensions the same idea applies, with the third coordinate averaged in exactly the same way.

Questions people ask

How do I find the midpoint of two points?

Average the x coordinates and average the y coordinates: ((x1 + x2) / 2, (y1 + y2) / 2). For (2, 3) and (8, 11) the midpoint is (5, 7).

How is the distance between two points calculated?

Use the distance formula: sqrt((x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2). For (2, 3) and (8, 11) the distance is sqrt(36 + 64) = 10.

What if the two points are the same?

Then the midpoint is that point itself and the distance is 0. The calculator shows both results.

Is the midpoint always on the segment?

Yes. The midpoint is halfway along the straight segment joining the two points, so it is the same distance from both endpoints.
