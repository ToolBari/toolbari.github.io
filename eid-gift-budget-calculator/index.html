How the fair split works

A fair split starts by taking away anything you want to keep back, and then dividing what is left equally. If your budget is 500 and you set aside 50 for wrapping and cards, there are 450 left to spend on gifts. Split between five recipients, that is 90 each.

This calculator does exactly that, in that order: total minus set-aside, divided by the number of recipients. The per-person figure it gives is the amount you can spend on each gift without going over your budget, and the steps show the working so you can see where every number came from.

A worked example

Suppose your Eid gift budget is 500, you are buying for five people, and you want to set aside 50 for wrapping paper and cards. Enter 500 for the budget, 5 for the recipients, and 50 for the set-aside, and the calculator shows 90 per person.

The working is 500 minus 50, which is 450, divided by 5, which is 90. Five gifts at 90 each come to 450, plus the 50 set aside, which brings the total back to exactly 500. Nothing is left over and nothing is overspent.

Setting a little aside

Wrapping paper, cards, ribbon, and delivery all cost money, and they are easy to forget when the budget is worked out. Setting a small amount aside first keeps those costs visible, so they do not quietly eat into the gift money and leave one recipient short.

The set-aside is optional. If you already have wrapping supplies at home, or you are handing gifts over in person with no cards, enter 0 and the whole budget is split between the recipients. The choice is yours, and the calculator works either way.

When recipients are not equal

Not every recipient gets the same gift in real life. Parents and children often get more than distant cousins, and a close friend may get something more thoughtful than a colleague. An equal split is a starting point, not a rule, and this calculator gives you that starting point.

One practical way to handle it is to work out the equal share first, then adjust. If the equal share is 90 and you want to spend 120 on one person, that extra 30 has to come from somewhere: either from another recipient's share or from a larger budget. Seeing the numbers makes the trade-off honest instead of accidental.

Gift ideas that cost little

A good Eid gift does not have to be expensive. Homemade sweets, a handwritten card, a framed photograph, or an afternoon spent together can mean more than anything bought in a hurry. The best gifts are the ones that show you thought about the person, and thought costs nothing.

Children in particular value the surprise as much as the gift itself. A small toy, a book, or a box of their favourite treats, wrapped with care and given with a smile, is remembered long after the wrapping is gone. If your budget is tight, spend the time instead of the money: the wrapping, the card, and the moment of giving are half the gift.

Eidi: money gifts for children

Eidi is the traditional money gift given to children at Eid, usually tucked into an envelope and handed over with a hug. It is one of the happiest parts of the day for the young ones, and it deserves a line in the budget of its own, because it adds up quickly when there are several children to give to.

Decide the Eidi amount per child in advance, and include it in the total budget you enter here, either as part of the per-person figure or as money set aside. Small notes in bright envelopes work best, and the joy is in the receiving rather than the amount: a little given gladly is worth more than a lot given grudgingly.

Keeping track as you buy

A budget only works if you keep track of it. Write down each gift and what it cost as you buy it, and compare the running total with the per-person figure the calculator gave you. A simple list on paper or on your phone is enough: name, gift, cost.

If one gift comes in under budget, the saving can go to another recipient or stay in your pocket. If one goes over, you know straight away and can trim the next one. The goal is not to spend exactly the same on everyone, but to finish Eid without an unpleasant surprise.

Staying inside the budget

The easiest way to stay inside a budget is to decide it before you start shopping, not after. Make the list of recipients first, work out the per-person figure with this calculator, and then shop to the list. Impulse buys are the usual reason budgets fail, and a list is the usual cure.

Buying early helps too. Prices rise and choices shrink in the last days before Eid, and rushed shopping is expensive shopping. A gift bought two weeks early is usually cheaper than the same gift bought the day before, and it gives you time to wrap it properly.

One last tip: keep the receipts. If a gift does not fit or is not wanted, a receipt makes an exchange easy, and the money goes back into the budget instead of sitting in a drawer. A tidy budget is a forgiving one.

Questions people ask

What is a fair split?

An equal share of what remains after setting a little aside for wrapping and cards. If your budget is 500, you set aside 50, and there are five recipients, the fair split is 90 each.

What if I want to spend more on one person?

Use the per-person figure as a starting point and adjust from there. If you spend more on one recipient, spend less on another or raise the total budget, so the numbers still add up.

What does set aside mean?

Money kept back from the gift budget for wrapping paper, cards, ribbon, or delivery. Setting it aside first means the per-person figure is what is really left to spend on gifts.

What if the split does not come out even?

The per-person figure is rounded to two decimal places, and any tiny rounding remainder is shown in the steps. The remainder is usually only a few of the smallest coins in your currency.

Does this tool suggest how much to spend?

No. Your budget is yours to enter, in your own currency. This page never suggests an amount, because a sensible budget depends on your own circumstances.
