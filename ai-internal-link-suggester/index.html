AI Internal Link Suggester

This tool finds internal link ideas by matching the words in your page URLs with the words in your draft text. Paste a URL list and your draft to see suggested links, with the matching words highlighted.

Matching is by shared words only. A shared word does not mean the link fits the reader — read each suggestion and link only where it helps. This tool works fully in your browser and calls no AI service, API, or backend.

How to use the link suggester

Paste your page URLs into the first box, one per line — full addresses or bare slugs both work — and paste the draft of your page into the second box. Press "Find link suggestions" to see which URLs share words with your draft, with the matching words highlighted and a short excerpt from your text.

Copy the list to keep it, then add the links by hand in your draft. A suggestion is an idea, not an instruction: keep the links that help the reader and drop the rest.

How the matching works

The tool takes the last part of each URL — the slug — and splits it into words, ignoring short filler words and words under three letters. It then checks which of those words appear in your draft text. A URL is suggested when at least one of its slug words appears in the text, and the matching words are shown with each suggestion.

Matching is by whole words, so "content" matches "content" but not "contents" or "discontent". The tool does not understand meaning: it cannot tell that two different words mean the same thing, and it cannot tell that the same word means different things in different places.

When to link and when not to

Link where the reader would genuinely want to read more — a term you mention but do not explain, a related guide that answers the next question, a tool the reader can use right away. Two or three good links beat ten forced ones.

Do not link the same page twice in one draft, do not link from every mention of a word, and do not link to a page that says the same thing as the one the reader is already on. If the link does not add something, leave it out — the reader will thank you by staying on the page.

Review your links when you update a draft. Pages move, titles change, and a link that made sense last year may point at a page you have since rewritten. A quick check of every internal link before publishing takes minutes and saves the reader from landing on the wrong page.

Questions people ask

What counts as a matching word?

A word of three or more letters from the URL slug that also appears in your draft text. Short filler words such as "how", "the" and "for" are ignored, so they never create a suggestion.

Can I paste full URLs or just slugs?

Both. The tool takes the last part of each line as the slug, so a full address and a bare slug give the same result.

Why did a URL get no suggestion?

None of its slug words appear in your draft text. Either the page is not related to this draft, or the draft uses different words for the same topic.

Does it check that the links work?

No. It only matches words. Check that every URL you link to exists and opens the page you mean before you publish.
