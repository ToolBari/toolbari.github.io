How points work

The group stage of the Cricket World Cup uses the standard points system for a one-day tournament. A win is worth two points, and a match with no result — usually because of rain — gives one point to each team. A loss is worth nothing. After five matches each, the top three teams in each group go through to the Super 7, joined by the best fourth-placed team across the two groups.

Two points for a win keeps the table simple: the most a team can earn in the group stage is 10 points from five wins, and a team on 8 points is almost always through. A team on 6 points is usually through, and a team on 4 points needs help from other results. The calculator adds the points up for you, two per win and one per no-result, and shows the working next to the table.

The no-result point matters more than it looks. A washed-out match gives both teams a point they did not have to play for, and in a tight group that single point can be the difference between third place and fourth. The calculator counts it exactly as the tournament does.

What net run rate is

When teams finish level on points, net run rate decides who goes through. Net run rate is simple in principle: it is the average number of runs a team scores per over, minus the average number of runs it concedes per over. A team that scores quickly and concedes slowly has a positive net run rate, and a team that does the opposite has a negative one.

The formula is: runs scored divided by overs faced, minus runs conceded divided by overs bowled. Both halves use the same idea — runs per over — so the result is a single number, usually between minus two and plus two, shown to two decimal places with a plus or minus sign.

Net run rate rewards big wins and punishes heavy defeats. Winning by a wide margin lifts your scoring rate and lowers your conceding rate at the same time, while losing heavily does the opposite. That is why teams keep pushing for runs even when a match is already won: every run changes the rate.

Overs, balls and decimals

Cricket counts overs in a way that confuses calculators. An over has six balls, so 45.2 overs does not mean 45.2 in the decimal sense — it means 45 overs and 2 balls, which is 45 and two-sixths of an over, or about 45.33 overs. The digit after the point is a count of balls, and it can only be 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5.

This calculator understands that notation. Type 50 for a full fifty overs, or 45.2 for 45 overs and 2 balls, and it converts the balls into a fraction of an over before doing the division. If you type something like 45.7, it tells you the digit after the point must be balls from 0 to 5, because there is no such thing as a seventh ball in an over.

Getting this right matters, because the wrong conversion changes the answer. Treating 45.2 as the decimal 45.2 would give a scoring rate of 180 divided by 45.2, which is about 3.98 — close to the right answer of 4.00, but wrong, and in a tight table the difference could decide a place in the Super 7.

Worked examples

Here are the two worked examples, checked by hand. Example one: Team A scores 250 runs in 50 overs and concedes 200 runs in 50 overs. The scoring rate is 250 divided by 50, which is 5.00 runs per over. The conceding rate is 200 divided by 50, which is 4.00 runs per over. Subtract the second from the first: 5.00 minus 4.00 is 1.00, so Team A's net run rate is plus 1.00.

Example two: Team B scores 180 runs in 45 overs and concedes 210 runs in 50 overs. The scoring rate is 180 divided by 45, which is 4.00 runs per over. The conceding rate is 210 divided by 50, which is 4.20 runs per over. Subtract: 4.00 minus 4.20 is minus 0.20, so Team B's net run rate is minus 0.20.

Reading the table

The table is sorted the way the tournament sorts it: points first, then net run rate. The top three teams in each group reach the Super 7, and so does the best fourth-placed team across the two groups — the fourth-placed team with the better record when the two groups are compared. That makes fourth place a live position right to the end of the group stage.

Watch the net run rate column as closely as the points column. Two teams on the same points are separated by net run rate alone, and a big win or a heavy defeat can move a team by several places in the rate even when the points do not change. A team on 6 points with a net run rate of plus 1.00 is in a much safer position than a team on 6 points with a rate of minus 0.50.

Questions people ask

How many points is a win worth in the group stage?

Two points for a win, one point each for a no-result, and nothing for a loss. Those are the standard points for a one-day tournament.

How is net run rate worked out?

Net run rate is runs scored per over minus runs conceded per over. Divide runs scored by overs faced, divide runs conceded by overs bowled, and subtract the second from the first.

What does 45.2 overs mean?

It means 45 overs and 2 balls, which is about 45.33 overs. The digit after the point counts balls, so it can only be 0 to 5.

Who reaches the Super 7?

The top three teams in each group go through, plus the best fourth-placed team across the two groups. Those seven teams play each other once in the Super 7.

What if two teams tie on points and net run rate?

The table sorts by points first and then by net run rate. Any further tiebreaker follows the tournament rules, which this page does not try to reproduce.

Does the calculator use live scores?

No. It works fully in your browser and calls no external service or live data. You enter the results yourself, and it does the arithmetic.
