How the average cost is worked out

Average cost per share is the total amount invested divided by the total number of shares. It is a weighted average, which means a big lot counts more than a small one. Simply averaging the prices would give the wrong answer whenever the lots hold different numbers of shares.

As a worked example, suppose you bought 10 shares at 50 and later 20 shares at 40. The first lot cost 500 and the second cost 800, so the total invested is 1300 for 30 shares. Dividing 1300 by 30 gives an average cost of 43.33 per share, which sits closer to 40 than to 50 because the second lot was bigger.

Adding and removing lots

The calculator starts with two lots. Use the "Add another lot" button for each further purchase, and the "Remove" button on a row to take a lot out. At least one lot must stay on the page. Every lot needs both a share count and a price per share before the calculation runs.

Fees and taxes are not included, because they vary by broker and by country. If you want the average to reflect what you actually paid, add the fee for each purchase into that lot's price before you enter it, or keep a separate note of the fees. Keeping a simple record of each purchase date, share count and price makes it easy to rebuild the average later.

Questions people ask

Does the calculator include broker fees?

No. It averages the share counts and prices you enter only. Add any fee into the lot's price yourself if you want the average to reflect it.

How many lots can I add?

As many as you need. Use the Add another lot button for each purchase, and remove any lot you no longer need with its Remove button.

What if I sell some of my shares?

Edit the lots to match what you still hold, or remove a lot entirely. The calculator averages whatever lots are on the page when you press Calculate.

Is the average the same as the latest price I paid?

No. The average blends every lot you enter, weighted by size, so it usually sits between your cheapest and your most expensive purchase.
