What schema markup does

Schema markup is structured data that describes a page's content in a machine-readable way. It uses the vocabulary from schema.org, written as JSON-LD, a format search engines and AI systems can read. This page builds three common types:

  • FAQPage — a list of questions and answers on a page.
  • Article — the headline, author and publication date of an article.
  • LocalBusiness — a business name, address and telephone number.

The markup sits on the page it describes and must match content that is actually visible there. This builds JSON-LD structured data, which is different from meta tags: meta tags give short facts about a page, while schema markup describes the meaning of its content.

FAQ rich results: what changed in August 2023

Google used to show FAQ rich results, expandable questions and answers, under many search results. In August 2023 Google restricted FAQ rich results, so most sites no longer see them. This tool never promises a rich result: the markup helps search engines and AI systems understand the page, and whether anything extra appears in search results is up to the search engine.

Questions people ask

What is schema markup?

Schema markup is structured data, written in JSON-LD, that describes what a page is about in a machine-readable way. Search engines and AI systems use it to understand the page's content, such as its questions and answers, its author, or a business's address.

Where do I put the JSON-LD this tool generates?

Paste it inside a JSON-LD script block on the page the markup describes, in the head or the body of the page. The markup should describe content that is actually visible on that page.

Will this markup give my page a rich result in Google?

There is no guarantee. Google restricted FAQ rich results in August 2023, so most sites no longer see them, and a rich result is never promised. The markup still helps search engines and AI systems understand the page.

Is schema markup the same as meta tags?

No. Meta tags give browsers and search engines short facts about a page, such as its title and description. Schema markup describes the meaning of the page's content in structured data, and the two can be used together.
