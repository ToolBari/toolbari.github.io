AI Content Brief Generator

This tool builds a content brief from a keyword or topic, an audience and a goal. You get working title options, an angle, suggested headings, questions to answer and internal-link reminders — ready to hand to a writer or use yourself.

Template-built text — the brief is assembled from your inputs using built-in templates.

A brief is a starting point, not a finished plan. Check the headings and questions against what your readers actually ask before you write. This tool works fully in your browser and calls no AI service, API, or backend.

How to use the brief generator

Enter the keyword or topic the page will cover, add the audience if you know it, and pick the goal of the page. Press "Build brief" to get the full brief: three working title options, an audience note, an angle for the goal you picked, five suggested headings, four questions to answer, and internal-link reminders.

Copy the brief and use it as the skeleton of your draft. Change any heading that does not fit, drop questions your readers would never ask, and add the ones they do ask. A brief you edit is a brief you own; a brief you follow word for word is a brief that will read like everyone else's.

What is inside the brief

The working titles give you three angles on the same topic: a plain guide title, a how-to title, and a questions title. Pick the one that fits your page, or mix two together. The angle changes with the goal you pick — a page meant to rank answers the question fully, a page meant to teach explains step by step, a page meant to win a customer shows value and invites one next step, and a page meant to engage keeps things lively.

The suggested headings follow a simple order: what the topic is, how it works, how to start, what to avoid, and what people ask. The questions to answer are the ones a reader brings to the page — answer them in the body, not just in the FAQ, and the page will feel complete.

How to use the brief well

A brief works best when it is short enough to keep in view while you write. Before you start, read the questions to answer and check them against real reader questions — search suggestions, comments, messages, support tickets. Add the real questions and remove the template ones that miss.

When the draft is done, use the brief as a checklist: every heading covered, every question answered, the angle kept all the way through, and the internal links added where they help the reader. If a section does not serve the goal, cut it — a brief is a plan, not a contract.

Questions people ask

What is a content brief?

A content brief is a short plan for one page: the topic, the audience, the goal, the angle, the headings and the questions it must answer. It keeps a writer on track and makes the draft easier to check.

How many title options do I get?

Three working titles: a plain guide title, a how-to title, and a questions title. They are starting points — pick one, mix two together, or write your own once the draft is done.

Can I change the goal later?

Yes. Pick a different goal and build the brief again. The angle and the emphasis change with the goal, while the topic and headings stay the same.

Does the brief write the article?

No. It plans the article. The writing, the examples and the facts still come from you — the brief only makes sure nothing important is forgotten.
