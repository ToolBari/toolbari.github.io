How to use the Video Thumbnail Extractor

Press Choose video file and pick a video from your device. The preview plays in the page, so you can watch the video and find the moment you want. Move the slider to scrub through the video, or type the exact time in seconds — the video jumps to that time.

Press Capture frame. The page waits until the video has actually reached the chosen time, then draws that frame onto a canvas and shows it below the preview, with its exact pixel size. Press Download PNG to save the frame as an image file.

For example, in a 30-second clip, capturing at 5 seconds downloads a PNG image at the video's resolution.

What resolution is the PNG?

The canvas is sized to the video's own width and height, so the PNG matches the video's resolution exactly. A 1280 by 720 video gives a 1280 by 720 image, and a 1920 by 1080 video gives a 1920 by 1080 image. The preview on the page is scaled to fit the screen, but the downloaded PNG is always the full frame.

Frame accuracy depends on the browser's seeking: the captured frame can land a fraction of a second away from the exact time you chose. Video formats the browser cannot play show a plain message instead of a frame. The file never leaves your browser — the frame is drawn on your device, and nothing is uploaded. This extracts a frame from a file you own, which is different from fetching a thumbnail from a video-site address.

Questions people ask

What resolution is the downloaded image?

The PNG is saved at the video's own resolution, so a 1280 by 720 video gives a 1280 by 720 image. The page shows the exact pixel size of the captured frame.

Why might the frame be slightly off the time I chose?

Frame accuracy depends on the browser's seeking. Most browsers land on the nearest frame they can decode quickly, so the captured frame can be a fraction of a second away from the exact time.

Which video formats work?

Any format your browser can play works. If the browser cannot play the file, the page shows a plain message instead of a frame. MP4 and WebM are the most widely playable formats.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. The file stays in your browser. The frame is drawn from the video onto a canvas on your device, and nothing is sent to a server.
