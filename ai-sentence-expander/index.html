When to expand a sentence

Short sentences are good for impact and for instructions. But a page made only of short sentences feels choppy, and a bare claim can sound unsupported. Expanding a sentence helps when a reader needs a reason to believe it, an example to picture it, or a detail to understand it. One expanded sentence in a paragraph can carry the whole paragraph.

Do not expand everything. If every sentence grows, the writing becomes heavy and the reader loses the thread. Pick the sentences that carry your main points, expand those, and leave the rest short. Variety in sentence length is what makes writing pleasant to read.

The four expansion patterns

Adding detail works by naming the parts of an idea: what it includes, how it works, and why each part counts. Adding an example works by moving from the general claim to one concrete case the reader can picture. Adding a reason works by answering the silent question why, which turns a claim into an argument. Adding a contrast works by showing the other side, which makes the original sentence look considered rather than one-sided.

Once you know the patterns, you can use them without the tool. Read a short sentence you wrote, ask which pattern fits, and write the longer version yourself. The tool is a fast way to see the patterns in action, and a useful starting point when you are stuck.

Questions people ask

How many versions does the tool write?

Three versions each time, all in the style you picked. Change the style and press the button again to see three more.

Does the tool add facts to my sentence?

No. The expansions add general wording from fixed patterns. They never invent facts, names, or numbers about your topic.

Can I edit the expanded versions?

Yes. Every version is a starting point. Add your own details and examples, and cut any wording that does not fit.

Does the tool save my sentences?

No. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere or stored.
