What goes on a receipt

A receipt names the seller and the buyer, lists what was sold with quantities and amounts, and shows the totals with the date. This maker puts those pieces together in a clean document you can print or keep as a file.

Each item line shows its own total, worked out as quantity times amount. The subtotal is the sum of the lines, and the final total takes off any discount you enter and adds any tax you enter. The working is printed on the receipt so anyone can check the maths.

Keeping records

Print a copy for the buyer and keep one for yourself, or download the receipt as a file and store it with your other records. A receipt number helps you find a receipt later, so use your own numbering in order.

All amounts on the receipt are the numbers you type. This maker never suggests prices or fills in amounts for you, so check the figures before you print.

Receipt numbers

A receipt number is a simple way to keep receipts in order. Use your own numbering, such as starting at one and counting up, or include the year and month so the number tells you when the receipt was made.

Keep the numbering consistent and never reuse a number, because two receipts with the same number are easy to mix up. If you make a mistake on a receipt, make a new one with the next number rather than editing the old one.

Questions people ask

How are the totals worked out?

Each line total is quantity times amount, the subtotal is the sum of the lines, and the final total is the subtotal minus any discount plus any tax you enter.

Can I add more item lines?

Yes. Use the Add item button to add as many lines as you need, and the Remove button on a line to take it out.

How do I print the receipt?

Use the Print button to open the print dialog with just the receipt, or download the receipt as a file to keep or send.

Do I have to enter a discount or tax?

No. Both are optional and are treated as zero when left empty, so the total is just the subtotal.
