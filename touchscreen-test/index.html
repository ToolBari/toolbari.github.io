This test shows what your browser reports about touches. It cannot find hardware faults the browser does not report, and on a device without touch it can only demonstrate the canvas with a mouse.

How to run the test

Put one finger on the test area and a green circle appears under it, numbered 1, with the count showing one touch point. Add a second finger and a second circle appears, numbered 2, and the count rises to two. Lift a finger and its circle disappears. Most phones and tablets track five or more touches at once, and the count tells you exactly how many your device reports.

Drag a finger across the canvas to leave a trail of small dots. A smooth, unbroken trail means the screen is tracking the movement well. If the trail jumps or breaks, try again more slowly: a gap that appears in the same place every time may point to a weak area of the screen.

What the grid and trails tell you

The dead-zone grid divides the test area into twenty squares. Touch each part of the canvas in turn and the matching square lights up green. When you have covered the whole area, the counter reads 20 of 20. A square that never lights up, even after repeated taps, marks a part of the screen that is not responding to touch.

Trails stay on the canvas until you press "Clear trails and grid", which also resets the grid and the count. On a computer without a touchscreen, the mouse draws a single point and lights up grid squares, which is useful for seeing how the test works, but it cannot test real multi-touch.

Questions people ask

Does this test work on a phone?

Yes. Open the page in your phone browser and touch the canvas with one or more fingers. Each touch point is drawn live with a running count.

How many touches can it track at once?

As many as your device and browser report. Most modern phones track five to ten touches at once, and the count shows the number live.

Can I use a mouse on a desktop?

Yes. Clicking and dragging draws a single touch point and lights up grid squares, but a mouse cannot test real multi-touch.

Does the test save or send anything?

No. Everything happens in your browser and nothing is recorded, uploaded or stored.
