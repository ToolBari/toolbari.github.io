One Rep Max Calculator

Estimate your one-rep max from a weight you lifted and the reps you managed. The calculator shows the Epley and Brzycki estimates side by side, with a percentage table for planning.

These are maths estimates from two common formulas, not a measured maximum and not medical advice. Lift within your ability.

How the estimate works

A one-rep max is the most weight you can lift for a single repetition. Testing it directly means lifting a true maximum, which is not always practical, so these formulas estimate it from a lighter set you have already done. You enter the weight you lifted and how many reps you completed, and the calculator applies two well known formulas to the same numbers.

The Epley formula is weight times one plus reps divided by 30. The Brzycki formula is weight times 36 divided by 37 minus reps. For example, a weight of 100 lifted for 5 reps gives an Epley estimate of 116.7 and a Brzycki estimate of 112.5. The calculator shows the working for both, so you can see exactly how each number was reached. The two answers are usually close, and the gap between them is a reminder that both are estimates.

Using the percentage table

Below the estimates, the table shows 50 percent to 100 percent of the Epley estimate in steps of 5 percent. Those percentages are a planning aid: pick the row that matches the intensity you want for a session and read off the weight. The table is based on the Epley estimate only, and the page says so under the table, so there is no confusion about which number it comes from.

Estimates work best from sets done close to failure with a moderate rep count. A set of 1 rep is already a one-rep max, so both formulas return the weight itself, and very high rep counts make the estimate less meaningful. Treat the result as a guide for planning, not as a number to chase at any cost.

Questions people ask

What is a one-rep max?

The most weight you can lift for one repetition of an exercise. This calculator estimates it from a lighter set.

Which formula should I use?

The page shows both. Epley and Brzycki often give close answers, and the percentage table uses the Epley estimate.

How many reps can I enter?

From 1 to 30 reps. The Brzycki formula needs fewer than 37 reps, so the calculator caps the range at 30.

Is the result exact?

No. It is an estimate from a formula, and your real one-rep max can differ. Treat the number as a guide.
